= qws — release notes
:toc: left
:toc-title: Contents
:toclevels: 2
:revdate: 2026-10-04

Every minor or major release is a chapter, its patches are subsections, the
newest come first (link:specs/004-releases/spec.adoc[specification 004]). The
version of a build is `qws version`. Release files are built on Debian 12:
they need glibc 2.36 or newer and the OpenGL and X11 libraries.

== 1.5.0 — 2026-10-05

*The selected tile shown after a switch to the grid*
(link:specs/028-grid-locate/spec.adoc[specification 028]), with the `glx`
renderer:

* After `q` or `g` from the carousel, the selection frame of the grid and
  the shadow of the selected tile come in larger and transparent and shrink
  onto the tile within 400 ms: the eye finds the selected window at once.
  `appearance.animation.locate_duration` sets the time, `0s` for none;
  `appearance.animation.locate_zoom`, 1.6, the scale they come from.
* A switch between the carousel and the grid shows the other layout in the
  next frame: its layers are drawn in the background while the first one is
  shown, instead of a frame drawn on the CPU after the key, 76–97 ms on
  ws1. They take some 14 MB more of GPU memory at 2560×1440.
* The first switch of an activation may still take some tens of
  milliseconds on NVIDIA: the first use of the grid's textures on the GPU.

=== 1.5.2 — 2026-10-07

*Less memory at the peaks*
(link:specs/030-drawing-memory/spec.adoc[specification 030]), the pictures
the same byte for byte:

* The font faces of the drawings are made once and kept, with a glyph
  cache a quarter of the size, instead of being made again after the
  collector dropped them; the canvases of the frames and the layers are
  reused; the memory is given back to the system as the switcher closes.
* Off the screen, 33 windows and 50 activations: the peak of the heap
  338 MB instead of 1628. A day of a service: the peak resident set
  978 MB instead of 1576, the heap kept between activations some 100 MB
  higher on a machine of 28 CPUs — the faces kept.

=== 1.5.1 — 2026-10-05

*The last picture of a window hidden*
(link:specs/016-unviewable-thumbnails/spec.adoc[specification 016]):

* A window that changed less than `behavior.snapshot_interval` before it
  was hidden — its workspace left, a tab switched — kept a thumbnail up to
  that interval old. qws now takes its last snapshot as it is hidden, from
  the picture it still holds: a terminal left at once shows the lines it
  printed last.
* Windows not shown since qws started keep their placeholders until they
  are: their contents do not exist in the X server, and qws keeps nothing
  between runs.

== 1.4.0 — 2026-10-04

*Live thumbnails* (link:specs/020-live-thumbnails/spec.adoc[specification
020]), with the `glx` renderer:

* While the switcher is shown, the thumbnails of the windows that change
  follow them — a video, a terminal printing, a build. A visible window
  whose card or tile is in view is averaged again on the GPU at most every
  `appearance.thumbnail.live_interval`, 50 ms by default, into a texture the
  switcher draws over its snapshot: on ws1 a change shows in its card
  within some 53 ms at p95.
* Thumbnails are passed again only while the switcher is at rest: not
  during a step or a fade, but 30 ms after it. Windows not shown — on
  another workspace, minimized — and cards out of view keep their
  snapshots.
* Windows of depth 24 drawn into the frame of the window manager
  (specification 022) follow as well: they are taken from the frame by
  RENDER into a pixmap of qws's own, through a chain kept for an
  activation (link:specs/023-frame-pass-cost/spec.adoc[specification 023]):
  6 requests to the X server a pass instead of 28, some 0.7 ms of qws's CPU
  instead of 1.7.
* The cost on ws1, a window changing all the time: some 2 % of one CPU
  while the switcher is shown. In the grid, the first steps after a rest
  miss a few more frames, 1.4–1.7 % of their intervals against 0.9 %
  without live thumbnails; the carousel misses as before.
* `appearance.thumbnail.live: false` keeps the snapshots taken before the
  switcher appeared, as in 1.3.4. Under `cpu`, or where the GPU cannot
  share the thumbnails with the switcher, nothing changes, and the log says
  so once; Mesa is not tried yet.

*Layout keys* (link:specs/026-layout-keys/spec.adoc[specification 026]):

* `q` toggles the carousel and the grid while the switcher is shown
  (`keybindings.layout_toggle`); the next activation opens in
  `appearance.layout` again, and `c` and `g` still choose one. The top
  right of the header says what the key does now: `Q — grid` in the
  carousel, `Q — carousel` in the grid.
* In the grid, Up and Down move a row within the column, from its bottom
  back to its top and from its top to its last tile; Left and Right go row
  after row as before.
* A configuration whose cancel key is `q` keeps it: `q` cancels, and there
  is no toggle and no hint, which the log says, until
  `keybindings.layout_toggle` names another key.

=== 1.4.1 — 2026-10-04

*The mouse in the grid* (link:specs/027-grid-mouse/spec.adoc[specification
027]):

* With the header shown, the mouse hovered and clicked another tile of the
  grid than the one under the pointer — one of a grid laid out higher, the
  band of the header left out. It now finds each tile where the grid draws
  it.

== 1.3.0 — 2026-10-02

*Faster to open* (link:specs/003-window-list/spec.adoc[specification 003]):

* qws keeps a model of the windows — their names, states, workspaces, icons,
  classes and hints — up to date from the events of the X server, and the
  switcher reads the list from it as it opens, after a single round trip:
  under a millisecond instead of 150–180 ms for 35 windows. On ws1 the first
  frame came some 120 ms after the key instead of some 345 ms.
* Of the icons a window offers, only the one shown is read; the icon of the
  class of a window without one is looked up once per class.
* Should the model fail to start, the list is collected as the switcher
  opens, as before.

*Appearance keys* (link:specs/014-appearance-keys/spec.adoc[specification
014]); with their defaults, the picture and the animations are those of
1.2.1:

* `appearance.header.enabled: false` hides the hostname and the version at
  the top left of the switcher; the grid then takes the whole window.
* `appearance.animation.hover_duration` sets the time of the hover
  animation; `0s`, the default, keeps that of `duration`.
* `appearance.animation.overlay_zoom`, 0.92 by default, is the scale of its
  size the switcher zooms from as it appears and to as it disappears;
  `appearance.animation.hover_zoom`, 1.05, that of the hover frame. A factor
  that is not a positive number gives a warning, and the default is used.

*Joined configuration keys are no longer read*
(link:specs/015-no-joined-keys/spec.adoc[specification 015]), the one
incompatible change, released in a minor version by the author's decision:

* A key written joined, as the builds before 0.1.0 wrote them —
  `snapshotinterval` for `snapshot_interval` — no longer applies: the key it
  stands for takes its default unless something else sets it, and qws logs
  a warning naming the key to use, which `qws config show` prints too. For
  example, `workspacemodifier: Super` gave `Super` under 1.2.1 and gives
  `Ctrl` now.
* A file written by `qws config init` of any release is read as before.

The documents under `doc/` are in English
(link:specs/017-english-documents/spec.adoc[specification 017]).

=== 1.3.4 — 2026-10-04

*Thumbnails current under load*
(link:specs/025-snapshot-wait-x/spec.adoc[specification 025]):

* A thumbnail taken on the GPU could show a window a step old — its last
  rows as they were before the window's latest drawing — when the desktop
  was busy: the snapshot read the window before the X server had finished
  drawing it. It now waits for that drawing, some hundredths of a
  millisecond at rest.

*Resource identifiers reused*
(link:specs/024-xid-reuse/spec.adoc[specification 024]):

* Each connection to the X server has some two million identifiers for the
  pixmaps, pictures and other resources it creates, and qws never reused
  them: a window of depth 24 changing all the time on a visible workspace
  would have used up the snapshots' connection in some three days, after
  which its thumbnail and those of new windows stopped. They are now
  reused through the XC-MISC extension, and none handed out ahead is
  given again.

=== 1.3.3 — 2026-10-02

*Windows that had no thumbnail*
(link:specs/022-uncaptured-windows/spec.adoc[specification 022]):

* A window of depth 24 drawn into the frame of the window manager — Dion,
  Telegram, KeePassXC, Yandex Music, xterm — has no pixmap of its own, and
  since 1.1.0 the snapshots took that for a window not shown: it had no
  thumbnail. It is now taken from the pixmap of its frame and scaled in the X
  server by RENDER, in about a millisecond; windows of depth 32 are captured
  on the GPU as before.

*A crash* (link:specs/021-xgb-extension-init/spec.adoc[specification 021]):

* qws could end with `fatal error: concurrent map read and map write` as the
  switcher opened while windows changed: an X extension was set up again at
  every activation, writing tables that every connection reads. The
  extensions are now set up once, before the first connection.

=== 1.3.2 — 2026-10-02

*Snapshots out of the switcher's way* (link:specs/011-snapshot-pause/spec.adoc[specification
011]):

* While the switcher is shown, the capture of windows waits instead of
  spinning on a CPU, as it did since 1.1.0 whenever a window changed
  meanwhile.
* Every qws stops capturing windows while the switcher of any qws on the
  display is shown, not only its own: a second instance — a second service,
  a trial build — no longer takes the frames of the first one's switcher.
  Beside one, a step used to miss 1.1 % of its frames instead of 0.5 %, a
  fade-in about one in ten.

=== 1.3.1 — 2026-10-02

*The grid drawn faster* (link:specs/019-grid-speed/spec.adoc[specification
019]), the picture the same byte for byte:

* A frame of the grid is drawn in some 80 ms instead of some 520 ms for 36
  windows on ws1 — as the switcher opens in the grid, and at every step with
  `cpu`: the thumbnails of the tiles are scaled in parallel, on up to half of
  the processors, and a long title is measured once instead of once for each
  length tried.
* With `glx` a step through the grid ends with its animation, 150 ms after
  the key, instead of some 530 ms: its last frame no longer waits for the
  drawing.

== 1.2.0 — 2026-10-01

*Animation options* (link:specs/010-animation-options/spec.adoc[specification
010]), with the `glx` renderer, under `appearance.animation`:

* `enabled: false` turns every animation off; `duration` sets how long each
  takes, 150 ms by default.
* `step: false` keeps the selection from moving: it is at the next window
  in the next frame.
* `show` and `hide` say how the switcher appears and disappears: `fade`,
  `zoom` — growing from 0.92 of its size — both, or `none`.
* `hover` animates the hover frame of the tile or card under the pointer:
  `fade`, `zoom` — closing in on the tile from 1.05 of its size — both, or
  `none`, at once as in 1.1.0.
* New defaults: the switcher fades and zooms as it appears and disappears,
  and the hover frame fades and zooms. `show: [fade]`, `hide: [fade]` and
  `hover: none` keep the look of 1.1.0.

=== 1.2.1 — 2026-10-01

*Thumbnails of a second instance*
(link:specs/018-snapshot-bind-conflicts/spec.adoc[specification 018]):

* A second qws beside the service — a test instance, a second service —
  captured every window on the CPU again, about 100 ms each, some 10 % of a
  CPU while idle: on NVIDIA only one client may bind the pixmap of a window
  as a texture, and the service already does. Such a window is now scaled
  down in the X server by RENDER, in about 2 ms; the CPU remains the
  fallback without RENDER or MIT-SHM.
* A refused binding is logged once per window, with the X error and the
  request that caused it.

== 1.1.0 — 2026-10-01

*Thumbnails* (link:specs/008-window-snapshots/spec.adoc[specification 008]):

* Every visible window is captured when its contents change, not only the
  active one: the cards of windows you have not focused for a while show
  what they show now, as long as they are on a visible workspace. A window
  on a hidden workspace keeps the thumbnail taken while it was visible.
* A capture costs about a millisecond instead of about a hundred: the
  window is scaled on the GPU, through `GLX_EXT_texture_from_pixmap`,
  averaging its pixels by area, and only the thumbnail is read back. An
  idle qws no longer spends some 9 % of a CPU on snapshots.
* When the switcher opens, the visible windows changed since their last
  capture are captured first, so their cards are current.
* `behavior.snapshot_interval` — now how often, at most, a changed window
  is captured again — defaults to 1 s instead of 10 s.
* Without GLX or `texture_from_pixmap`, qws captures the active window on
  the CPU as before; `appearance.thumbnail.scaling_algorithm` applies there
  only.

== 1.0.0 — 2026-10-01

The first major version, by the author's word once the switcher moves well.
Nothing is incompatible with 0.1.0: the configuration, the keys, the flags
and the environment variables are read as before.

*Animation* (link:specs/007-animation/spec.adoc[specification 007]), with
the `glx` renderer:

* A step through the carousel or the grid moves in 150 ms, easing out, a
  frame every refresh of the monitor the switcher is on. A key pressed
  mid-way sets a new target and the motion turns towards it, so a held key
  glides rather than queues.
* At rest the switcher shows exactly the picture of `cpu`, as before.
* The switcher fades in when it appears and fades out when it goes; the
  chosen window is activated first, so the fade costs no time.
* In the grid the hover follows the mouse at once; it used to lag by a
  frame drawn for every tile the pointer crossed.
* `cpu` changes its picture at once, as before.

On the author's desktop at 144 Hz a step shows 23 frames, the interval
between frames is 7.1 ms at p95, and about one frame in two hundred misses
a refresh. The grid still takes about half a second to draw its exact
picture on the CPU: its motion is on time, the exact picture follows.

*Smaller changes*: no thumbnail snapshots are taken while the switcher is
shown, and the keyboard mapping is read once per activation, not on every
key — both used to hold the X server up while the switcher was on screen.

== 0.1.0 — 2026-09-30

The first release: a carousel and grid window switcher for X11 with MRU
order, live thumbnails, keyboard and mouse navigation, and configuration
through a YAML file, `QWS_*` environment variables and flags.

*Rendering* (link:specs/001-rendering-speed/spec.adoc[specification 001]):

* The `glx` renderer shows exactly the picture of `cpu`: the frame is drawn
  by the same code and presented through GLX. It is now the default, and
  falls back to `cpu` with a warning where GLX cannot be initialised.
* Both renderers are faster. On the author's desktop a step through the
  carousel takes 62 ms at p50 and 91 ms at p95 with `glx`, 72 and 98 ms
  with `cpu`, against 223 and 274 ms before; the first step can follow the
  activation after about 200 ms instead of 660.

*Header* (link:specs/005-host-and-version/spec.adoc[specification 005]):
the overlay shows the hostname in large letters and the qws version at its
top left, so that it is plain which machine and which build it is.

*Configuration* (link:specs/002-config-names/spec.adoc[specification 002]):

* Environment variables are read under the documented names,
  `QWS_APPEARANCE_RENDERER`, and `QWS_CONFIG` gives the configuration file.
* `qws config init` and `qws config show` write multi-word keys with
  underscores, as qws reads them. Files written by earlier `config init`,
  with such keys joined (`snapshotinterval`), are read as they were meant,
  with a warning per joined key — so their values now take effect.

Build::
cgo, `pkg-config` and the OpenGL and X11 development packages
(`scripts/debian-deps.sh` lists them for Debian 12). `make build` builds in
the version.
