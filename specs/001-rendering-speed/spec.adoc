== What and why

Every frame qws shows is drawn on the CPU and sent to the X server as an
image. The `cpu` renderer draws the scene with fogleman/gg and ships it as
BGRA through `PutImage` in 200 KB strips, `CopyArea` and a blocking `Sync`.
The `glx` renderer draws the scene on the GPU into an offscreen framebuffer,
reads it back with `glReadPixels`, flips it on the CPU and sends it down the
same path — and its picture is not the one `cpu` draws (research, "Visual
parity"). The January 2026 profile attributes 80% of the process CPU time to
`Selector.render` (research, "Prior measurements").

The change makes switching faster — activation, every step, CPU work per
frame — with `glx` as the fast path. Author's requirement (2026-09-30): `glx`
shows exactly, bit for bit, what `cpu` shows. The `cpu` drawing code
therefore produces the pixels for both renderers; `glx` only presents them
through GLX on the overlay window, without the readback and `PutImage` path.
The `cpu` renderer stays the reference and the fallback, and neither its
picture nor its speed may get worse.

The specification is a delta to `develop` at `2c59c26`. Unchanged: the
layouts (carousel and grid) and their look as `cpu` draws it today, key and
mouse handling, MRU order, window filtering, thumbnail capture (strategies,
`scaling_algorithm`, the 512×512 cap, the snapshot routine), configuration
keys, their meaning and defaults, the default renderer `cpu`.

Branch `issue/optimize-gl` (`81116cd`, `93ebc6e`) is a prototype: what fits
this specification is carried over stage by stage, and each stage is
measured (plan). Issue #1, migration to Gio, is an alternative that stays
open and is outside this specification.

== Metrics

All times are wall-clock, taken in-process with a monotonic clock.

*P* — the end of presentation of a frame: for `cpu`, the reply to
`conn.Sync()` after the `CopyArea` of the frame; for `glx`, the return of
`glFinish()` after `glXSwapBuffers()`. At P the frame is in the overlay
window's drawable; the compositor's own frame comes on top for both renderers
and is not measured.

M1 — activation latency:: From the entry to `handleKeyPress` for the
activating `KeyPress` to P of the first frame. Measured with
`behavior.showdelay` 0. Recorded by phases: M1.list — the window list,
MRU sort and thumbnails from the cache; M1.show — `Selector.Show` up to the
first `render` (active window, monitor, window creation, map, keyboard grab,
modifier state); M1.draw — drawing the scene; M1.present — from the end of
drawing to P. _Cold_ — the first activation after the process starts;
_warm_ — every other.

M1.ready — ready for the first step:: From the entry to `handleKeyPress` to
the end of the first frame after the activation's, the one of the `Expose`
that mapping causes: until then the selector does not read keys. Reported,
no criterion; recorded since stage 3 (`activation_ms` of the frame records).

M2 — step latency:: From reading a navigation `KeyPress` in
`handleEventsSync` to P of the resulting frame; phases M2.draw and
M2.present.

M3 — CPU work per frame:: M3.cpu — CPU time in the `Selector.render` subtree
of a CPU profile (`--cpuprofile`) divided by the number of frames drawn in
the session, without the samples of `glFinish`: it is called only while
timings are logged, to mark P, and the NVIDIA driver busy-waits in it;
M3.alloc — bytes allocated in the same subtree (`--memprofile`,
`alloc_space`) divided by the same number.

Session S1:: A measuring instance on environment E1 (research) with a key
combination not grabbed by i3 or by the running instances. A script drives
it with `xdotool`: 20 activations; in each, 23 steps forward through the 24
windows, then `Escape`, so the desktop's focus does not change. One run of S1
gives 20 samples of M1 (1 cold, 19 warm) and 460 of M2. Percentiles p50 and
p95 are taken over the samples of a run; the baseline is three runs. The
desktop changes between sessions — 24 windows at T2, 30 at T5 — so a
comparison with the baseline also runs the baseline binary, `44e8cb1`, in
the same session (`QWS=… measure.sh run`).

Scene set Σ:: Fixed input data rendered without an X server
(`pkg/carousel/scenes_test.go`, 18 scenes): both layouts; the frame of E1,
2520×1400, and 1260×700; selection at the first, the second, a middle and the
last card; hover on another card; an urgent window; windows without a
thumbnail (placeholder) and without an icon; square, non-square and NRGBA
icons, and icons with straight alpha as `_NET_WM_ICON` gives them; a title
long enough to truncate; Cyrillic titles; titles with glyphs only the
fallback font has (★ ✓ ⌘) and a CJK title no font has; 1, 2, 6 and 24
windows; dark and light theme. Portable scenes use only the Go font of
`golang.org/x/image`; host scenes — placeholders and fallback titles — also
use DejaVu Sans and are skipped when the host's file differs from the one
the digests were recorded with.

== Behavior

. *`renderer: glx`* draws the frame with the `cpu` drawing code and presents
  it through GLX: the overlay window is created with the visual of a
  double-buffered GLX FBConfig with a depth-32 ARGB visual; the frame is
  uploaded into a texture and drawn 1:1 into the back buffer — nearest
  sampling, no blending, no dithering — then `glXSwapBuffers`. No
  `PutImage`, `CopyArea` or `Sync` on the frame path.
. *The GPU-drawn scene* of today's `glx` (shaders, MSAA framebuffer,
  `glReadPixels`) is removed together with the hidden GLFW window, and so is
  its stale size after a monitor change. Users of `renderer: glx` see the
  `cpu` picture.
. *`renderer: cpu`* draws exactly the pixels it draws on `develop` and
  presents them through `PutImage` as today; the conversion to BGRA and the
  requests may be sped up as long as the bytes sent to the server are the
  same.
. *`Expose` of the overlay window* presents the last frame again instead of
  drawing it anew — the same pixels, without the second full frame that now
  stands between an activation and its first step (research, "Findings").
. *Failure to initialise GLX* — no GLX, no suitable FBConfig, no context —
  falls back to `cpu` with a warning in the log, as today.
. *The shared drawing code* may be sped up only by changes that keep every
  pixel: both renderers gain from them.
. *Configuration:* no new keys; `renderer` keeps its values `cpu` and `glx`
  and its default `cpu`.

Outside the specification: drawing the scene on the GPU; the prototype's
stages 2–5 — raw BGRA upload and GPU downscale (off the frame path, and the
latter changes pixels), the texture cache with XDamage, pre-warm, instanced
drawing; changes to the `cpu` picture, including its quirks (side
thumbnails and icons ignore card alpha, `shadow.blur` is not applied) — such
a change is a specification of its own and changes both renderers at once;
the window list collection, M1.list — specification 002 (author's decision,
2026-09-30).

== Acceptance criteria

[cols="1,6"]
|===
| # | Criterion and method

| K1
| `cpu` picture unchanged: for every scene of Σ, the SHA-256 of the `cpu`
  frame's pixels equals the digest recorded from the drawing code of
  `develop` at `2c59c26`, before any change to it (T3). Test in
  `pkg/carousel`.

| K2
| `glx` equals `cpu` in-process: for every scene of Σ, the back buffer of
  `glx`, read back before the swap by a test-only hook, equals the `cpu`
  frame byte for byte. Test in `pkg/carousel`; skips without a display with
  GLX.

| K3
| `glx` equals `cpu` on screen: E1 with picom running; for three activations
  of each renderer, the contents of the overlay window after the first frame,
  read from the server with `GetImage`, equal the frame the renderer
  produced, dumped by `--debug-dump-frames` — `console/framecmp` gives an
  absolute error of 0 pixels (`measure.sh k3`).

| K4
| Fallback: a GLX initialisation error gives the `cpu` renderer and a
  warning in the log. Test of the backend selection with an injected
  initialisation error.

| K5
| `glx` step latency: M2 p95 ≤ 100 ms (baseline `cpu`: 273 ms). S1, three
  runs.

| K6
| `glx` activation latency: M1 warm p95 ≤ 300 ms (baseline `cpu`: 549–581
  ms), with the window list collected as today; M1 cold reported. S1, three
  runs.

| K7
| `glx` CPU work per frame: M3.cpu ≤ 70 ms and M3.alloc ≤ 16 MB (baseline
  `cpu`: 202 ms and 57 MB). S1, three runs.

| K8
| `cpu` without regression: p50 of M1 warm, M2, M3.cpu and M3.alloc of `cpu`
  do not exceed those of the baseline binary in the same session by more
  than the spread between the runs of the baseline binary. S1, three runs
  of each.

| K9
| `go vet ./...` and `go test ./...` pass.
|===

== Decisions

Author, 2026-09-30, from the baseline (T4): the targets of K5–K7 as above —
a one-refresh step (6.94 ms) is out of reach while the CPU draws every pixel
(research, "Where the time goes"); the window list collection is left to
specification 002; the presentation of `cpu` without the per-pixel `At` and
`Expose` without redrawing join stage 3.
