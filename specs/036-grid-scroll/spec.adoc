== What and why

The grid lays every window out on one screen: as they grow in number its
tiles shrink. On ws1 on 2026-10-13, 47 windows, an overlay of 2520×1400:
6 columns — the most `GridColumns` gives — by 8 rows, tiles of some
396×140, a thumbnail some 60 px high in each, squashed — `layoutGrid`
bounds the width and the height of a tile each on its own, and the
thumbnail takes what the title leaves.

The author, 2026-10-13: with many windows, lay the grid out over several
screens, scrolled vertically, and do not make the thumbnails that small.

The specification is a delta to `develop` at `d6d535a` (1.6.1). Unchanged:
the carousel; the grid of windows that fit (D2); the keys, but for the
scroll they now bring with them.

== Decisions

D1, D4 and D5, and D3, decided by the author on 2026-10-13, each as
recommended; D2 and D6–D9 made in the specification; D1 and D3 amended and
D9 made in the plan.

D1 (the author's). *A least thumbnail.* The thumbnail of a tile at least
`appearance.grid.min_thumbnail_width` wide, 320 px by default, in the
proportions of the overlay — 320×178 on ws1's 2520×1400. 0: no least,
the grid on one screen as now. The least is at most what
`appearance.thumbnail` lets a tile be, a thumbnail of (width + 20) ×
height: 276 px with the defaults of 256×256 (amended in the plan).

D2. *One screen while it fits.* A grid whose thumbnails come out at least
that wide on one screen is laid out as now, pixel for pixel; past that,
its tiles keep the least size, or the size the columns give them, and the
rows run below the screen.

D3 (the author's). *As many columns as fit.* With `columns: 0`, the
columns as now, `ceil(sqrt(1.5 n))`, but not at most 6: at most as many as
fit across at the least size. On ws1's 2520 px, 6: 7 would give
thumbnails of 317 px (amended in the plan). `columns` set, that many, the
tiles as wide as they come.

D4 (the author's). *The view follows the selection.* A step, by the keys
or the arrows, that takes the selection to a row out of the view scrolls
it the least to bring that row in whole — animated as a step is, at once
when still (031). An activation starts with the view at the top, or where
it brings the first selection in.

D5 (the author's). *The wheel.* The mouse wheel over the grid scrolls it by
a row, within its ends; the selection stays where it is. The hover, the
click (027) and the live thumbnails see the tiles where they are drawn.

D6. *There is more.* A thin scrollbar at the right edge of the grid, its
thumb the view among the rows; the rows cut by the bottom — and the top —
of the view drawn cut, not hidden.

D7. *The arrows of 026.* Up and Down within the column, around its ends,
over all its rows: from the last to the first scrolls the view to the top.

D8. *Live thumbnails seen.* Only the tiles in the view, whole or cut, are
live (020); the others are drawn from their snapshots.

D9. *The layers.* The tiles of the whole grid stay one layer (007), the
view a window onto it, the scroll a move of it under the header: no tile
drawn again as the view moves. The layer of all the rows fits the
presenter — 48.6 MB and 4898 px high at 100 windows on E1, against a
texture of 16384 px at least —, not cut in bands; a grid taller than
16384 px has no layer and is drawn by the CPU (research, plan).
