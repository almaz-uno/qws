== The grid now

`layoutGrid` (`pkg/carousel/renderer-cpu.go`) lays n tiles out in c
columns and r = ⌈n/c⌉ rows below the band of the header, of height `top`,
with the spacing s between and around them:

----
c      = appearance.grid.columns, or when 0: clamp(⌈√(1.5 n)⌉, 2, 6)
tileW  = min((W − s(c+1)) / c, thumbnail.width + 40)
tileH  = min((H − top − s(r+1)) / r, thumbnail.height + 60)
----

the grid centred in the window below the band. Width and height are bound
each on its own. Of a tile the thumbnail takes a box of (tileW − 20) ×
(tileH − 60) — 10 px around it, 60 px below it for the title and the
workspace (`gridThumbnail`) — and the picture is scaled into the box, its
proportions kept.

The layout on E1 — the overlay 2520×1400, the author's cards of 512×512,
Noto Sans and DejaVu Sans at 20, the header with `ws1` and the version:
`layoutGrid` itself, through a throwaway test in `pkg/carousel`,
2026-10-13. The band of the header is 90 px.

[cols="1,1,2,2"]
|===
| Windows | Columns × rows | Tile | Thumbnail box

| 24 | 6 × 4 | 396.7 × 302.5 | 376.7 × 242.5
| 33 | 6 × 6 | 396.7 × 195.0 | 376.7 × 135.0
| 47 | 6 × 8 | 396.7 × 141.2 | 376.7 × 81.2
| 100 | 6 × 17 | 396.7 × 55.9 | 376.7 × 0 — none
|===

At 47 a picture of 16:9 comes out 144×81; at 100 the box has no height
left, and the tiles show their titles alone.

== The least thumbnail (D1, D2)

A tile shows a thumbnail at least m wide in the proportions of the overlay
when its box holds m × m·H/W: tileW − 20 ≥ m and tileH − 60 ≥ m·H/W. On
E1, m = 320: 320×177.8, the tile 340×237.8.

The tiles of `layoutGrid` are no larger than `appearance.thumbnail` lets
them: a box of at most (width + 20) × height. A least above that cannot be
had — with the defaults, 256×256, at most 276 px wide —, and with no grid
fitting, every grid would scroll and its tiles grow past the bound. The
least is therefore taken as at most what the bound allows: m′ = min(m,
width + 20, height·W/H); 320 on E1, 276 with the defaults. An amendment of
D1 (plan).

== The columns (D3)

As many columns as fit across at the least size: the tiles of m′ + 20 and
the spacing,

----
cfit = max(1, ⌊(W − s) / (m′ + 20 + s)⌋)
----

On E1, ⌊2500 / 360⌋ = 6, not 7 as the draft says: 7 columns leave tiles of
(2520 − 8·20)/7 = 337.1 px, thumbnails of 317.1. An amendment of D3 and K1
(plan). On a screen of 1920×1080 less the default padding of 20 px — an
overlay of 1880×1040 —, ⌊1860 / 360⌋ = 5.

D3 bounds `⌈√(1.5 n)⌉` by cfit in place of 6. Where cfit is the smaller,
the columns of `develop` give tiles narrower than m′: their grid did not
fit, and D2 lays it out anew anyway. Where cfit is above 6 — an overlay
wider than 2540 px at 320 — a grid of more than 24 windows gets more
columns than in `develop`, by D3.

== The grid that scrolls

Past the least, the columns as D3 gives them, the tiles as wide as they
come, the thumbnail box in the proportions of the overlay, under the bound
of `appearance.thumbnail`:

----
tileW = min((W − s(c+1)) / c, thumbnail.width + 40)
boxH  = min((tileW − 20)·H/W, thumbnail.height)
tileH = boxH + 60
----

the rows from the band of the header down, the first at `top` + s, the
content `top` + r·tileH + (r+1)·s high, the view from `top` to the bottom
of the window, the scroll from 0 to the content's excess over the window,
in whole pixels.

On E1:

[cols="1,1,2,2,1,1"]
|===
| Windows | Columns × rows | Tile | Thumbnail box | Rows in view | Scroll at most

| 47 | 6 × 8 | 396.7 × 269.3 | 376.7 × 209.3 | 4.53 | 1025 px
| 100 | 6 × 17 | 396.7 × 269.3 | 376.7 × 209.3 | 4.53 | 3628 px
|===

A row is 289.3 px from the next; the view, 1310 px, holds four rows whole
and a part of a fifth. A picture of 16:9 comes out 372×209.

== The layer of all the rows (D9)

The tiles layer, `GridTiles`, is drawn on a canvas of the window and
cropped to what it draws (specification 007). The rows of a grid that
scrolls need a canvas of the content: 2520 × 2425 px at 47 windows on E1,
2520 × 5028 at 100; cropped to the tiles, x from 20 to 2500 and from the
first row's top to the last row's bottom, some 2480 × 2295 and 2480 × 4898
px — 22.8 and 48.6 MB at 4 bytes a pixel, against 14.1 MB of the window.

GL_MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE, the largest side of a texture:

[cols="2,3"]
|===
| ws1, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4080 SUPER, driver 580.105.08 | 32768
  (`glxinfo -l`, 2026-10-13)
| ws2 and ws3, NVIDIA T1200 | not measured here; the minimum of OpenGL
  4.6 core, the context of the presenter, is 16384
|===

16384 px are 56 rows of E1 at 289.3 px: 336 windows in 6 columns. The
memory of the GPU — 16 GiB on ws1, 8.1 of them in use on 2026-10-13 —
takes 48.6 MB more without notice.

While a layer is drawn the CPU holds the canvas of the content, the
scratch canvas of the heads of the same size (specification 019) and the
crop: some 150 MB at 100 windows on E1, given back to the free list of
canvases (specification 030), which drops them as the overlay is unmapped.

*Decision.* One layer of all the rows, its bounds in the coordinates of
the rows; a scene places it at the scroll and cuts it to the view. Bands
would bound the canvas and the texture but put the seams of their edges
through the tiles, more layers in the cache and more items in a scene,
and nothing of them is needed below 336 windows. A grid taller than 16384
px is not drawn as a layer: `GridTiles` gives none, and the grid is drawn
by the CPU frame by frame, its steps at once, as before its layers come.

== The frame and the layer drawn

After T3, `BenchmarkE1Grid` in `pkg/carousel`, the scenes of Σ with the
header of E1 and the Go font (`go test ./pkg/carousel -run '^$' -bench
E1Grid -benchtime 20x -count 3 -benchmem`), ws1 — Intel Core i9-7920X, 24
threads —, 2026-10-14 00:01, load average 6.9–9: ms per drawing, the
three runs.

[cols="3,2,2"]
|===
| Drawing | Frame | Tiles layer

| `grid-e1-header`, 24 windows on one screen | 45.1–45.8 | 52.6–54.7
| `grid-e1-47-middle`, the view at 446 px | 66.8–69.3 | 85.6–87.5
| `grid-e1-100-middle`, at 1892 px | 68.0–68.1 | 170.7–175.6
|===

The frame draws the tiles of the view alone, five rows of six, whatever
the windows; the layer, all the rows. Its bounds: (19,109)–(2501,2405),
22.8 MB, at 47; (19,109)–(2501,5008), 48.6 MB, at 100 — as estimated
above. Until it comes, steps are at once (specification 007); the frame at
rest of a step is drawn in the background in the time of a frame.

== What the presenter can cut

A scene item is drawn as a quad covering its rectangles (`drawScene`,
`pkg/carousel/present_glx_scene.go`); nothing cuts it. The rows that pass
under the header need a cut at the top of the view: the quad intersected
with a rectangle of the item, `Clip`, before it is drawn. In whole pixels,
and with the scroll in whole pixels, the texels of the tiles layer fall
on the pixels of the frame as at rest, and a scene at a scroll is the
frame at rest drawn at that scroll up to the rounding of the composition
(K4). A live item (020) is drawn into its rectangle of pixels: cut to the
view by cutting that rectangle and its holes.

== The wheel

The overlay asks for `ButtonPress` (`pkg/carousel/window.go`); the X server
reports a turn of the wheel as a press of button 4, up, or 5, down, at the
pointer — no events beyond the core protocol.
