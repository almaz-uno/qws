The plan of the options recommended in the specification; should the author
choose others, it is written again.

== Approach

*One owner, one share group.* The snapshotter keeps owning the windows'
pixmaps, their GLX pixmaps and their textures, as since 008. The presenter's
context is created with the snapshotter's as its share context, so that the
textures the snapshotter writes — and the fences after them — are objects of
the presenter's context too (research, "Sharing"). Nothing else crosses: the
presenter never binds a pixmap and never touches a window texture.

*Live textures.* A window gets, at its first live pass, two thumbnail
textures of the size of its snapshot, written in turn, with a framebuffer
each; a pass averages into one and makes a fence, without the read back.
Snapshots keep the one target of 008 and are read back from it: a window
that is never passed while the switcher is shown — most of them, on hidden
workspaces — gets no live textures, some 1.1 MB of the GPU's memory a
window of E1 saved. A generation number counts the pictures of a window,
snapshots and passes, so that the selector can tell a live picture newer
than the snapshot a card was drawn from; the snapshots do not change
during an activation (below), so the newest snapshot is the card's.

*Fences.* The commands of the two contexts run in no order of each other
(research of 013, "What the specifications allow"). The snapshotter tests
the fence of a pass on its own thread, at most every 0.5 ms while a pass is
under way, and only once it has signalled publishes the texture as the
window's picture: the presenter draws complete pictures and tests no
fence. A pass writes the texture published before the last publication —
the one the presenter drew until then — so it must come after every frame
that may draw it. The selector takes the pictures of a frame from the
snapshotter (`BeginFrame`) and gives back, once the frame is presented, a
fence the presenter made after it if it drew a live picture (`EndFrame`);
a pass is not made while a frame that took the pictures before the
window's last publication is being drawn, and waits otherwise, on the GPU,
for the presenter's last fence — as the uploads of 013 waited for a
texture the presenter had drawn. Textures of a window gone, or of a size
no longer its, are deleted once no frame is being drawn: a frame may bind
them, and a name deleted may be given again.

*The live schedule.* Between `SetLive(overlay, interval)`, which the
selector calls once its overlay is shown in full — at the end of the
fade-in, since the first runs of S5 (D5) — and `SetLive(0, 0)`, as the
fade-out starts, the loop of the snapshotter takes the
windows that changed, each at most once an interval, without the settle of
100 ms; of those due, the one that changed first. Only the windows the
switcher shows: those of its last frame, which `BeginFrame` gives — not a
window on a workspace the filter of the switcher leaves out, viewable on
another monitor. The interval is
`live_interval`, or the refresh period of the overlay's monitor where it is
0: the selector knows it. One window a pass, the passes spread: a pass no
sooner than the interval divided by the number of windows taking passes —
waiting, or passed within the interval — after the one before it, the
`spread` of the probe; each followed by a fence and a flush. Counting only
the windows waiting, a window passed alone would hold the pass of another
back by a whole interval, and its lag past the bound of K7.

*Passes in the pauses.* While frames come one after another — the last two
ended less than 10 ms apart, the last less than 10 ms ago — a pass due
less than 4 ms before the next frame ends, by the period of the last two,
waits for that end: `EndFrame` wakes the loop, and the pass runs on the GPU
in the pause after the frame. A pass made whenever due ran beside a frame
about one time in six. `BenchmarkLiveBeside`, the probe of "Beside the
frames" with the code of qws: with one window changing 50 times a second,
the frames presented in more than 2 ms were 0.57–0.75 % with passes
whenever due, 0.13 % with them in the pauses, 0–0.18 % with none, for a
lag at p95 of 40.2–40.4 ms against 39.1–39.7; with four windows 30 times a
second, 1.8–3.4 % against 0.48–0.66 %, the lag 46.7–47.0 ms against
39.7–40.7 (research, "Passes in the pauses"). Every pass right after a
frame, as first made, cost the lag a period and more: 60.9–61.8 ms at p95
with four windows. At rest no frame comes, and a pass goes when due. After
a pass is published the snapshotter tells the selector (below). Windows
changed since their snapshot when live begins wait for a pass from the
time they changed.

*The pause of 011.* `Pause` stays around an activation, as before, and
stops the snapshots on change only: between `SetLive(overlay)` and
`SetLive(0)` the loop of the snapshotter takes live passes, paused or not
(D5, made in the implementation). With #35, merged in 1.3.2, the live
passes are paused by the overlays of `_QWS_SWITCHERS` other than the one
`SetLive` gave it — `Switchers.Shown` gets a window to leave out, and
`othersShown` gives it the overlay of `SetLive` — and the snapshots by
`pausedNow` of #35 as it is, the own overlay counted: the change is the
condition of `liveTick` and of the timer of `liveWait`. The snapshotter
holds the switchers behind an interface of its own, so that a test can
give them.

*From the frame* (D2, the author's answer A). A window taken from its
frame's pixmap (022) has no texture of its own, and the frame's pixmap is
the compositor's: a pass scales the window by RENDER — `xrender.scale`,
the passes of 022's `thumbnail` without its read back — into a pixmap of
the snapshotter's own, a8r8g8b8 of the thumbnail's size, bound on the GPU
once (`scaledPixmap` of `live.go`); once the composites are checked — a
round trip, by when the X server has done them — and `glXWaitX`, the
pixmap is bound again and the average copies it, at a scale of 1, into
the live texture not published. The live textures, the fences, the
schedule and the drawing are those of every pass. `livePassable` takes
such a window with RENDER at hand and the passes from frames not turned
off; a pass that fails leaves it stale, a pixmap that will not bind turns
the passes from frames off. The pixmap is freed with the window, or at
another size.

*Drawing.* The selector knows where each thumbnail lies: the geometry of a
card at its offset — integer at rest, fractional in a step — and of a tile
(`CardLive`, `CarouselLive`, `GridLive` of `pkg/carousel/live.go`, from
`carouselCard`, `gridThumbnail` and the matrices the drawing uses). A live
item is a texture, the matrix the CPU draws the thumbnail under, the live
rectangle and its holes: what is drawn over the thumbnail at rest. On a
tile, the icon and the glyphs of the labels; on a card, what the cards
drawn after it draw — their covers, rectangles that hold every pixel a
card draws, as `drawWindowWithData` draws them, checked against it by
`TestLiveCovers`. The border of a card is `2 / scale` px wide, not 2: `gg`
strokes in pixels of the canvas. The selector gives the presenter the
items of a frame at rest (`SetLiveItems`), drawn over the next frame that
is not a scene, through its fade; a scene carries them among its items
(`SceneItem.Live`): in the carousel each after its card and before the
cards after it, in the grid after the tiles and before the frames. A
program of its own computes, for each pixel of a
live rectangle, the kernel of `x/image/draw` `BiLinear` at the point
the inverse of the matrix maps the pixel's centre to: a tent of support 1
widened to the scale when shrinking, the weights normalized, summed in 16
bits a channel and truncated, as `transform_RGBA_RGBA_Src` does —
`~/.cache/qws-probe-live/kernel` on ws1 is that program, in single
precision. The same program draws the live items of frames in motion, at
the card's place and scale of the frame. The pictures are published once
complete (above): the presenter tests no fence. It makes one before the
swap of a frame that drew live pictures (`TakeLiveFence`), for the
selector to give the snapshotter, and deletes the one it replaces
(`ReleaseFence`). The presenter is a `LivePresenter`, apart from
`Animator`, so that the presenters and the fakes of the animations stay as
they are.

*Waking at rest.* At rest the selector waits in `WaitForEvent` of its own
connection. Once a pass is published, the snapshotter sends the overlay
window a `ClientMessage` of the atom `_QWS_LIVE` through its own
connection, at most one between two frames of the selector (`BeginFrame`
clears it); the selector reads it as any event, not as a cause of the
frames of keys, and presents the frame shown — again, with `Refresh`, or
the scene at the target while its frame at rest is drawn — with the live
items over it, no sooner than a refresh period after the frame before: it
polls its connection meanwhile, as for the uploads of 007. Such a frame is
logged as a record "Frame" of cause `live`. In motion every frame takes
the pictures anyway. No goroutine reads the selector's connection, and the
order of its events is kept.

and CPU paths; `snapshot_interval`.

== Order

Sharing first (`pkg/glx`, K4), then the live textures and their schedule
(`pkg/snapshot`, K5), the drawing (`pkg/carousel`, K3), the selector and
the configuration (K6, K10), and last the sessions on E1 (K7–K9, K11), with
`live` on and off in turn. #35 was merged after the schedule: `pkg/x11`
came with the merge of `develop` at `759c6d3`.
