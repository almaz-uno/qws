The plan of the options recommended in the specification; should the author
choose others, it is written again.

== Approach

*One owner, one share group.* The snapshotter keeps owning the windows'
pixmaps, their GLX pixmaps and their textures, as since 008. The presenter's
context is created with the snapshotter's as its share context, so that the
textures the snapshotter writes — and the fences after them — are objects of
the presenter's context too (research, "Sharing"). Nothing else crosses: the
presenter never binds a pixmap and never touches a window texture.

*Live textures.* A window gets, at its first live pass, two thumbnail
textures of the size of its snapshot, written in turn, with a framebuffer
each; a pass averages into one and makes a fence, without the read back.
Snapshots keep the one target of 008 and are read back from it: a window
that is never passed while the switcher is shown — most of them, on hidden
workspaces — gets no live textures, some 1.1 MB of the GPU's memory a
window of E1 saved. A generation number counts the pictures of a window,
snapshots and passes, so that the selector can tell a live picture newer
than the snapshot a card was drawn from; the snapshots do not change
during an activation (below), so the newest snapshot is the card's.

*Fences.* The commands of the two contexts run in no order of each other
(research of 013, "What the specifications allow"). The snapshotter tests
the fence of a pass on its own thread, at most every 0.5 ms while a pass is
under way, and only once it has signalled publishes the texture as the
window's picture: the presenter draws complete pictures and tests no
fence. A pass writes the texture published before the last publication —
the one the presenter drew until then — so it must come after every frame
that may draw it. The selector takes the pictures of a frame from the
snapshotter (`BeginFrame`) and gives back, once the frame is presented, a
fence the presenter made after it if it drew a live picture (`EndFrame`);
a pass is not made while a frame that took the pictures before the
window's last publication is being drawn, and waits otherwise, on the GPU,
for the presenter's last fence — as the uploads of 013 waited for a
texture the presenter had drawn. Textures of a window gone, or of a size
no longer its, are deleted once no frame is being drawn: a frame may bind
them, and a name deleted may be given again.

*The live schedule.* Between `SetLive(overlay, interval)`, which the
selector calls when it maps its overlay, and `SetLive(0, 0)`, when it unmaps
it at the end of the fade-out, the loop of the snapshotter takes the
windows that changed, each at most once an interval, without the settle of
100 ms; of those due, the one that changed first. The interval is
`live_interval`, or the refresh period of the overlay's monitor where it is
0: the selector knows it. One window a pass, the passes spread: a pass no
sooner than the interval divided by the number of windows taking passes —
waiting, or passed within the interval — after the one before it, the
`spread` of the probe; each followed by a fence and a flush. Counting only
the windows waiting, a window passed alone would hold the pass of another
back by a whole interval, and its lag past the bound of K7. After a pass is
published the snapshotter tells the selector (below). Windows changed
since their snapshot when live begins wait for a pass from the time they
changed.

*The pause of 011.* Without #35 merged, `SetLive` replaces `Pause` around
an activation with `live: true` on `glx`. With it, the snapshotter is
paused by the overlays of `_QWS_SWITCHERS` other than the one `SetLive`
gave it — `Switchers.Shown` gets a window to leave out — and its own
`paused` is not set while live; with `live: false` the code of #35 is kept
as it is.

*Drawing.* The selector knows where each thumbnail lies: the geometry of a
card at its offset — integer at rest, fractional in a step — and of a tile.
It gives the presenter a list of live items, each a texture, its fence, the
matrix the CPU draws the thumbnail under, the live rectangle and, for a
tile, the rectangle of its icon; the presenter draws them after every frame
it presents — the frame at rest, a scene, a frame of a fade — through the
fade of that frame. A program of its own computes, for each pixel of a
live rectangle, the kernel of `x/image/draw` `BiLinear` at the point
the inverse of the matrix maps the pixel's centre to: a tent of support 1
widened to the scale when shrinking, the weights normalized, summed in 16
bits a channel and truncated, as `transform_RGBA_RGBA_Src` does —
`~/.cache/qws-probe-live/kernel` on ws1 is that program, in single
precision. The same program draws the live items of frames in motion, at
the card's place and scale of the frame. A texture is drawn once its fence
has signalled, tested with `glClientWaitSync` at timeout 0; until then the
one before it.

*Waking at rest.* At rest the selector waits in `WaitForEvent` of its own
connection. Once the fence of a pass has signalled — the snapshotter waits
for it on its own thread — the snapshotter sends the overlay window a
`ClientMessage` of the atom `_QWS_LIVE` through its own connection, at most
one a refresh period; the selector reads it as any event and presents the
frame shown with the live items over it. No goroutine reads the selector's
connection, and the order of its events is kept.

Reused: the area average of 008 and its test; the window texture and its
binding again; the fences and the shared context of the patch of 013
(`~/.cache/qws-013-upload-context.patch` on ws1), its X error handler for a
display of its own; the geometry of `carouselCard`, `gridThumbnail` and
`gridIconScale`; the fades of 007 and 010.

Unchanged: the drawing of `cpu`, its frames and `testdata/scenes.json`; the
layers of 007 and the heads of 019; the snapshots while hidden, their RENDER
and CPU paths; `snapshot_interval`.

== Order

Sharing first (`pkg/glx`, K4), then the live textures and their schedule
(`pkg/snapshot`, K5), the drawing (`pkg/carousel`, K3), the selector and
the configuration (K6, K10), and last the sessions on E1 (K7–K9, K11), with
`live` on and off in turn. Should #35 be merged first, `pkg/x11` comes with
the schedule.
