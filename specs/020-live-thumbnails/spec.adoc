== What and why

The thumbnails of the switcher are snapshots (specification 008): a window
is captured when DAMAGE reports a change, at most once a
`behavior.snapshot_interval` (1 s), and the windows changed since their
snapshot are captured again before the first frame of an activation. While
the switcher is shown no snapshot is taken (007): a card or a tile keeps
the picture it opened with, and a video, a terminal that scrolls, a build
log stand still on it. The author asks (issue #58) for thumbnails that
follow their windows while the switcher is shown.

What constrains it (research, "What the code does today"):

* only a viewable window has contents: on E1, 2 of 36 client windows at
  the time of the research, one per monitor; the others are on hidden
  workspaces and keep their snapshot;
* the X server of E1 lets one GLX pixmap exist on a window's storage
  (018): the snapshotter holds the window textures, and the presenter
  cannot bind them a second time, in the same process or another;
* on `glx` the frame at rest is the frame `cpu` draws, byte for byte
  (007, `TestGLXPresenterMatchesCPU`, the digests of Σ) — a thumbnail that
  moves can no longer be part of a frame the CPU draws once;
* the GPU of E1 drops its clock to 210 MHz under a light load, where a
  pass over a whole window costs about 1 ms, and work of another GL context
  that comes in bursts delays the frames of the presenter (research, "The
  cost of sampling", "Beside the frames").

The change, on `glx`: while the switcher is shown, a viewable window that
changes is averaged again on the GPU, at most once a
`appearance.thumbnail.live_interval`, into a texture the presenter's GL
context shares; every frame — in motion and at rest — draws the latest of
these textures over the thumbnail of the window's card or tile. Nothing is
read back and nothing is drawn by the CPU for a change. `cpu`, and `glx`
where the presenter cannot share the textures, keep the snapshots of today.

The specification is a delta to `develop` at `6fd664a` (1.3.1), written
before 011 (pull request #35) was merged; the branch was then merged with
`develop` at `759c6d3` (1.3.2), which has it, and at `9774075` (1.3.3),
which has 021 — the extensions of xgb set up once a process, every
connection made by `x11.NewConn` — and 022 — a window without a pixmap of
its own taken from its frame's pixmap by RENDER; the change is now a delta
to 1.3.3. Unchanged: the snapshots while the switcher is hidden (008, 018,
022) and before the first frame of an activation; their pause for the
switchers of other instances (011); every frame of `cpu`, and the digests
of Σ; the frames of `glx` while no shown window has changed since its
snapshot; the layers and the animations of 007 and 010; the existing
configuration keys and their meaning, `behavior.snapshot_interval` among
them. D5 says how it reads with and without 011.

== Decisions

Author, 2026-10-02: the options recommended below, D1–D7, as written, and
the bounds of K7 and K9 as proposed, provisional until the first measurement
on the screen. Each decision lists its options, the facts they rest on
(research) and the option recommended, which is the one taken.

=== D1. How live

[cols="1,3,3,3"]
|===
| | Option | For | Against

| a
| *GPU-live.* The presenter samples each window's texture in every frame,
  scaled into its card by a shader.
| A frame of lag behind the window, once the presenter knows of the change.
| A frame pays for every window shown, changed or not: by area average
  0.85 ms for 12 windows and 2.7 ms for 36, at 1.3–2.6 GHz; by bilinear
  filtering a tap reads 4 of the some 100 texels under a pixel of a tile of
  E1, so text aliases, and 36 windows took 2.6–2.9 ms; mipmapped, 0.26 ms a
  frame but 3.1 ms of GPU a change for the mipmaps, at 210 MHz (research,
  "The cost of sampling"). Beside paced frames, 12–100 % of them took over
  2 ms against 0.15–10 % for the clients alone ("Beside the frames"). The
  presenter would draw textures the snapshotter holds (018) — possible only
  shared with it. Another filter than the cards around it.

| b
| *Semi-live by read back.* The snapshots of today, taken while shown at a
  bounded rate; the CPU draws again what shows the thumbnail.
| Exact by construction: every frame is a frame of `cpu`.
| Each change costs a card layer, or the tiles layer of the grid (52 ms,
  019), and a frame at rest (32–45 ms, 019) on the CPU, and their uploads,
  1.4–14 MB, which 013 found to cost frames; under `cpu` a whole frame,
  70–100 ms with presenting (007, 019). At 5 changes a second, a fifth to
  a half of a CPU.

| c
| *GPU semi-live into shared textures.* On change, at most once a
  `live_interval`, the window is averaged on the GPU into a thumbnail
  texture of at most 512×512 — the snapshot of 008 without its read back —
  in a context that shares it with the presenter's; every frame draws the
  latest of them over the thumbnail of the card or tile, with the kernel
  `cpu` draws a thumbnail with.
| A change costs one pass: about 1 ms of GPU at 210 MHz, and 0.1–0.2 ms
  of the snapshotter's CPU with binding again. A frame costs 4–8 µs more a
  live thumbnail. The picture is the snapshot's, drawn with the kernel of
  `cpu`: a live card looks as a card of a fresh snapshot, within 1 per
  channel.
| Lags behind the window by up to a `live_interval` and a frame. Passes
  that run together hold up the frames meanwhile, so they are spread
  (below). Needs a GL context shared across two Xlib displays, which works
  on NVIDIA (research, "Sharing") and is not known on Mesa.
|===

*Recommended: c*, taken. Option a pays per frame
what c pays per change, and its one advantage, a frame of lag, c reaches
with a `live_interval` of 0; b pays with the CPU and with uploads.

Where the passes of c run, and when, was measured beside paced frames
(research, "Beside the frames"): in the snapshotter's context all at once
(`thumb` there); the same, one window at a time, spread over the interval
(`spread`); and in the presenter's own context, in the pauses between its
frames, as the uploads of 007 (`inline`). *Recommended: in the
snapshotter's context, one window at a time, spread*, taken. With 4–36
windows changing 30 times a second it left 0.07–2.0 % of the frames over
2 ms, `inline` 0.8–3.5 %, `thumb` 4.7–12.3 %, the clients' drawing alone
1.0–10.1 %; at 144 changes a second, 0.2 %. And the window textures stay
inside the snapshotter: the presenter only draws thumbnails and tests their
fences, and its loop gets no work of passes to schedule.

=== D2. Which windows are live

Options: every card and tile shown; only the selected and the hovered; a
number at most.

*Recommended: every card and tile the switcher draws whose window is
viewable*, taken. A window not viewable — on a hidden workspace, a hidden
tab — has no contents (008) and keeps its snapshot; one never viewable
keeps its placeholder (016). On E1 that leaves a few windows live, one or
two a monitor; the bound on the work is the rate of each window and the
spreading of the passes (D1), not a number of windows: a frame costs 4–8 µs
more a live thumbnail (research), a window that does not change costs
nothing, and 36 windows changing 30 times a second kept the frames as well
as 4 did.

The cards and tiles the switcher draws are those in view: every tile of
the grid, and the cards of the carousel with a live rectangle at the
selection — eleven at most, fewer where the frame is narrower than the
strip. A window whose card is off the strip takes no pass; when a step
brings its card into view, it takes one as soon as the spreading lets it.
Found in S5, 2026-10-02: every window of the list took passes, those off
the strip as well, and their pictures were drawn seconds after their
changes, when their cards came back.

A window drawn into the pixmap of its frame — of depth 24 in a frame of
depth 24, not redirected apart from the frame — has no pixmap of its own:
Composite names none, and the snapshotter has no texture of it to average.
022, in 1.3.3, takes its snapshots from the frame's pixmap by RENDER and
never binds that pixmap on the GPU: it is the compositor's, which picom
binds on ws1, and NVIDIA lets one GLX pixmap exist on a storage (018). On
ws1, 2026-10-02: Dion, Telegram, KeePassXC and Yandex Music, 4 of 35
windows, and the xterm the first runs of S5 changed; on 2026-10-03
ilovlya, of depth 24 and visual 0x2b in a frame of 0x21, which changed all
the time in the author's trial at rest. How such windows are live:

[cols="1,3,3,3"]
|===
| | Option | For | Against

| A
| *By RENDER into a pixmap of qws's own.* A pass scales the window's
  rectangle out of the frame's pixmap by RENDER, as its snapshot is
  scaled, straight into a pixmap of the snapshotter's of the thumbnail's
  size, bound on the GPU, and copies it into the live texture.
| Reads only, as 022: nothing changes in how the X server and the
  compositor draw the window. The pixmap bound is qws's own storage. The
  picture is the snapshot's, the filter of RENDER. No read back.
| A scale in the X server a pass, some 1 ms of its CPU for a window of
  E1, and more of the snapshotter's than a pass from a pixmap of its own.

| B
| *Redirected by qws.* The snapshotter redirects each such window,
  `CompositeRedirectWindow` automatic: a pixmap and a texture of its own.
| The passes of every other window, unchanged.
| Changes how the X server draws the author's windows while qws runs: a
  pixmap more a window, 14 MB for one of E1, a copy into the frame at each
  change, the window unmapped and mapped again in the X server (022, D1).

| C
| *Snapshots.* As 1.3.3.
| Nothing to do.
| The window that changes stands still; K11 fails on the author's desktop.
|===

*Author, 2026-10-03: A.* A window taken from its frame takes passes as
any other — in view, one window a pass, at most once a `live_interval` —
by RENDER into a pixmap of its own (Behavior, "From the frame").

=== D3. What replaces the frame at rest byte for byte

The frame of `glx` at rest can no longer be the frame `cpu` draws once,
since its thumbnails move. *Recommended*, taken:

. `cpu` keeps every byte: the digests of Σ in `testdata/scenes.json`
  unchanged.
. Outside the _live rectangles_ a frame of `glx` is what 007 presents: at
  rest the `cpu` frame byte for byte, in motion its scene of layers. The
  live rectangle of a window is the part of its thumbnail that is the
  thumbnail alone in the `cpu` frame: the pixels whose centres fall inside
  the thumbnail, less those its border touches — 1 px in from the edge on
  a tile; on a card the border is 2 px wide at the full size of its
  thumbnail and wider as the card is smaller, 2 divided by the scale the
  thumbnail is drawn at, since `gg` strokes in pixels of the canvas — and
  less what is drawn over it: the rectangle of the icon of a tile, and the
  glyphs of its labels where a large font takes them over the thumbnail;
  what the cards drawn after a card draw over it, its neighbour's title
  where long (research, "Where a thumbnail lies"). A window has
  a live rectangle only once it has changed since the snapshot its card was
  drawn from. So with live thumbnails off, or until a shown window
  changes, every frame at rest is the `cpu` frame byte for byte, as now.
. Inside a live rectangle the frame is the live thumbnail drawn with the
  kernel of `x/image/draw` `BiLinear` that `cpu` draws thumbnails with — a
  tent widened to the scale when shrinking. At rest it is within 1 per
  channel of the `cpu` frame drawn with that thumbnail, and equal to it in
  at least 99.5 % of the pixels: a shader computing the kernel in single
  precision gave 99.85–99.97 % at the places and scales of E1, never more
  than 1 apart, in 4–8 µs a thumbnail (research, "The kernel on the GPU").
  The thumbnail itself is the area average of 008, within 1 of the one
  computed on the CPU (K4 of 008).

Checked on a scene of fixed content: pictures written in a context like
the snapshotter's, drawn over frames of windows of the test off the
screen; the passes that make the pictures from pixmaps of known contents
are checked on their own (K4). Rejected: a tolerance over the whole frame, which would let any
pixel drift; a frame at rest drawn on the CPU at each change, which is
option b.

=== D4. The `cpu` renderer

Options: semi-live (option b of D1), or snapshots as today.

*Recommended: snapshots as today*, taken. `cpu` draws
a whole frame for any change, 70–100 ms on E1 with presenting; it is the
renderer of machines without GLX, where the snapshots themselves are taken
on the CPU at some 100 ms a window (008). `live` and `live_interval` are
read and have no effect under `cpu`.

=== D5. The pause of snapshots, `snapshot_interval`

Since 007 the snapshotter takes no snapshot on change while its switcher
is shown: `cmd/qws/root.go` calls `Pause(true)` before `Show` and
`Pause(false)` at the end of the fade-out. Specification 011 (pull request
#35, released as the patch 1.3.2) pauses the snapshots for the switcher of
any instance as well: its snapshotter is paused while `paused` is set or
`x11.Switchers.Shown()` — any overlay listed in the root property
`_QWS_SWITCHERS` is mapped — and each instance lists its own overlay there
when it creates it (`ListSwitcher` in `pkg/ui/selector.go`), so that its
own switcher makes `Shown()` true as well (read on the branch at
`aff81ee`).

*Recommended*, taken:

* while its own overlay is mapped and live thumbnails are on, the
  snapshotter takes live passes of the windows shown, and no snapshot with
  a read back; with them off, or under `cpu`, it pauses as now;
* the pause for the switcher of another instance stays as 011 makes it:
  neither a live pass nor a snapshot while another overlay is mapped — its
  reason, the frames of the other instance, holds for live passes too; two
  switchers are not shown at once anyway, since each grabs the keyboard;
* `behavior.snapshot_interval` keeps its meaning — at most once a period
  while the switcher is hidden; `live_interval` is the period while it is
  shown. A window changed while shown is captured with its read back by the
  schedule of 008 once the switcher is hidden, and at the next activation
  if not before.

How it reads against each base:

[cols="1,4"]
|===
| Base | The change

| `develop`, without #35
| `root.go` calls `Pause` around an activation as now; with `live: true`
  on `glx` the selector calls `SetLive(overlay)` of the snapshotter once
  its overlay is shown in full — at the end of the fade-in, or after the
  first frame when there is none — and `SetLive(0)` as the fade-out
  starts, or when it unmaps the overlay without one, and the snapshotter
  takes live passes in between, paused or not. With `live: false`, under
  `cpu`, or where live thumbnails are off (D7), `Pause` alone, as now.

| after #35 is merged — `759c6d3`, 1.3.2, and `9774075`, 1.3.3, the
  bases the branch is merged with
| The same; and the live passes are paused by `Switchers.Shown` with the
  overlay `SetLive` gives left out — it gets a window to leave out — so
  that they stop for the other instances' overlays only; the snapshots on
  change keep the pause of #35, `paused` or `Shown`. With `live: false`,
  #35 as it is.
|===

Made in the implementation, 2026-10-02: `root.go` keeps calling `Pause`
with live thumbnails on, rather than not at all, as the table first read.
Live passes run whatever `paused` says, so the pause now stops only the
snapshots on change, from before the window list of an activation is taken
to the end of its fade-out, as before: the snapshots its cards are drawn
from stay the snapshotter's newest during the activation, and a live
picture is drawn exactly when it is newer than the newest snapshot of its
window. Without the pause, a snapshot taken between the list and the
mapping of the overlay would be newer than a card's.

The overlay is made anew when the monitor changes; `SetLive` is called at
each activation with the overlay of that activation.

Changed after S5, 2026-10-02: `SetLive(overlay)` came when the overlay was
mapped, before the first frame, and `SetLive(0)` at its unmapping. The
first pass of an activation then ran right after its first frame, which
uploads a whole frame and the layers, and held up the frames after it:
the second frame of the carousel's fade-in presented in 10.0 ms on
average against 5.4 with `live: false`, the fade-ins of the carousel and
of the grid missing 10.4 and 15.2 % of their intervals against 6.3 and
9.5. The fades now take no pass; their frames draw the pictures published
before them. A switch released during the fade-in takes none at all.

=== D6. Configuration

Two keys under `appearance.thumbnail`, beside `width`, `height` and
`scaling_algorithm` — the thumbnail is a part of the picture, as
`appearance.header` is (014):

[cols="2,1,5"]
|===
| Key | Default | Meaning

| `appearance.thumbnail.live`
| `true`
| Thumbnails follow their windows while the switcher is shown, on `glx`.
  `false`: snapshots, as in 1.3.3.

| `appearance.thumbnail.live_interval`
| `50ms`
| A shown window that changed is averaged again at most this often; `0`:
  at most once a refresh of the monitor the overlay is on.
|===

Environment variables and flags as in 002: `QWS_APPEARANCE_THUMBNAIL_LIVE`,
`QWS_APPEARANCE_THUMBNAIL_LIVE_INTERVAL`, `--appearance-thumbnail-live`,
`--appearance-thumbnail-live-interval`. A negative interval is reported and
the default used, as 010 and 014 do with their durations.

*Recommended: `live: true` and 33 ms*, taken. 33 ms,
some 30 changes a second, is a video's rate and lags behind a window by at
most 33 ms and a frame. At 0 — once a refresh, 144 a second on E1 — the
frames hold as well (research, R = 144), but the snapshotter's CPU follows
the rate: 0.1–0.2 ms a pass, so four windows changing all the time take
some 2.6 % of a CPU at 30 a second and 6.6 % at 144.

*Author, 2026-10-04: 50 ms, for K9.* In the rest variant of S5 one window
changing all the time, passed every 33 ms, cost qws 4.70 points of a CPU
above `live: false`: a pass costs, with the frame of cause `live` it
brings, some 1.7 ms, of which 0.35 ms is the `glFinish` the timings of
`-v` add (research, "The second runs"), and 023 lowers the passes from the
frame to the cost of the others, not below. At 50 ms, 20 pictures a second
at most a window, such a window costs some 2.3 % of a CPU without that
`glFinish`, within K9; a window lags behind by at most 50 ms and a frame
(K7).

Rejected: one key whose 0 means off — 0 means "at once" for
`animation.duration`; reusing `snapshot_interval` while shown, which would
reinterpret an existing key.

=== D7. Mesa and Intel, ws3

Whether Mesa shares textures between contexts of two Xlib displays, and
whether its texture of a pixmap reflects the pixmap's changes, is not known:
ws3 was not tried, and `glx` itself is untested there (008).

*Recommended*, taken: where the presenter's context
cannot be created sharing the snapshotter's, or the snapshotter runs
without the GPU, live thumbnails are off for the session, reported once at
info level, and the thumbnails are the snapshots of today. Creating both
contexts on one Xlib display, which Mesa may need, is an open decision.

== Behavior

All of it on `glx` with `appearance.thumbnail.live: true`; otherwise as in
1.3.3.

. *Sharing.* The presenter's GL context is created with the snapshotter's
  as its share context, on the presenter's own Xlib display, as now. If
  that fails, the presenter is created as now and live thumbnails are off,
  "Live thumbnails off: …" at info level, once.
. *Live textures.* The snapshotter keeps, for each window it has passed
  live, a thumbnail texture of the size of its snapshot, two in turn, so
  that it writes one while the presenter draws the other. A snapshot is
  averaged and read back as now, in the one framebuffer of 008: a window
  never passed live — on a hidden workspace — gets no live textures.
. *While shown* — while the overlay is shown in full, from the end of the
  fade-in, or the first frame without one, to the start of the fade-out —
  the snapshotter takes no snapshot on change, and no live pass either
  while the overlay of another instance is mapped (D5, 011); its own
  overlay, listed among the switchers as well, does not pause them. From
  before the first frame to the end of the fade-out no snapshot is taken
  on change either (D5). A viewable window of a card or tile in view (D2)
  that changes is bound again and averaged into its live texture without
  a read back — no settle of 100 ms — at most
  once a `live_interval`, the window that has waited longest first, one
  window a pass: a pass no sooner than the `live_interval` divided by the
  number of windows taking passes — waiting, or passed within the
  interval — after the one before it. While the switcher presents frames
  one after another — a step, a hover moving; the fades take none — no
  pass is made: one due waits for the frames to stop, 30 ms after the
  last — past the pause between the steps of two keys — and a frame in
  motion draws the pictures published before it. Frames come one after
  another when the last two ended less than 10 ms apart.
  Changed after the rest variant of S5, 2026-10-03: passes made in the
  pauses between such frames, 4 ms before the next at the latest, held
  them off the screen but not on the desktop of E1, where the steps of the
  grid missed 5.63 and 8.14 % of their frames against 0.68 and 0.91 %,
  the X server's work for a pass delaying the overlay's frames (research,
  "S5 at rest"). A fence and a flush follow each pass; once the fence has
  signalled, tested by the snapshotter, the texture is published as the
  window's picture.
. *From the frame.* A window taken from its frame's pixmap (022) is
  passed by RENDER: its rectangle in the frame's pixmap — where 022 finds
  it, asked of the X server at each pass — scaled as its snapshot is, by
  bilinear halvings and a last bilinear pass, straight into a pixmap of
  qws's own, a8r8g8b8 of the thumbnail's size, made at the window's first
  pass and anew at another size, freed with the window. That pixmap, and
  never the frame's, is bound on the GPU; once the X server has answered
  and `glXWaitX` returned, it is bound again and copied into the live
  texture not published, under the fences of every pass. Nothing is read
  back. The frame's pixmap is named anew when the frame is configured,
  as 022 does: the window is then stale and keeps its picture until the
  next activation, as a window whose size changed. A pass that fails
  leaves the window so too; should a pixmap of qws's own not bind on the
  GPU, no window is passed from its frame for the session, "Live
  thumbnails of the windows taken from their frame off: …" at info
  level, once. Without RENDER, no such pass: the snapshots of 022.
. *Not live:* a window not viewable keeps its snapshot; a window whose size
  changes while shown keeps its last live picture until the next
  activation; a window the GPU will not bind (018) keeps its snapshot; a
  window whose card is not in view takes no pass.
. *Drawing.* A window whose live texture is newer than the snapshot its
  card or tile was drawn from has a live rectangle (D3). Every frame the
  presenter draws — the frame at rest, a scene of layers, a frame of a
  fade — draws, after it, each live picture published — its fence
  signalled — into its live rectangle with the kernel of `cpu` (D3),
  through the fade of the frame. A pass not yet published is not drawn;
  the presenter never waits for it. In a scene of the carousel the live
  rectangle follows its card, at the card's place and scale in the frame,
  drawn after the card and before the cards after it; in the grid it is
  drawn over the tiles and under the selection and the hover frames.
. *At rest.* A pass makes the selector present a frame — the frame shown,
  with the live textures over it — no sooner than a refresh period after
  the frame before it.
. *Hidden.* At the end of the fade-out the snapshotter resumes the
  snapshots of 008; a window changed since its last read back is captured
  by their schedule.

=== Existing configurations

No key, variable or flag is renamed or removed; `snapshot_interval` keeps
its meaning. A configuration without the two keys gets live thumbnails on
`glx` — a change the user sees, with two new keys: a minor version
(constitution, "Releases and versions"). `live: false` restores the
thumbnails of 1.3.3. `README.md`, `doc/configuration.asciidoc` and
`config.yaml.example` describe the keys in the same change.

== Metrics

L — lag:: For a live pass, the time from the snapshotter's reading of the
DAMAGE event of the change it covers to the end of the presentation, P, of
the first frame that draws it; the compositor shows the frame at its next
vertical blank, which L does not count. Only a window whose card or tile
is in view (D2) counts, and from when its card came into view — with the
layers of the activation it is drawn from — when that is later than the
change: a card that comes back into view with an older picture counts
from its coming back. The frame records of 001 and 007
get a field `live_ms`, the lag of the latest pass the frame draws for the
first time, in the first record of the frame; a frame at rest presented
again for a pass is a record "Frame" of cause `live`. The snapshotter logs
each pass at debug level, "Live", with the window and the time it took.

A1, A2, A3:: As in 007, session S2.

C — CPU:: CPU time of the qws process over a session, from
`/proc/<pid>/stat`, against the time the switcher was shown.

Session S5:: S2 of 007 on E1 — the overlay on DP-0 at 144 Hz, the service
stopped — with a window on DP-4 changing all the time: a terminal printing
a line every 20 ms, the window viewable on the visible workspace of DP-4,
of depth 32 with a pixmap of its own — a mate-terminal on E1 — and, for
the windows taken from their frame, of depth 24 drawn into the pixmap of
its frame — an xterm (D2). Run with the
carousel and with the grid, with `live: true` and with `live: false` in
turn, three times each. Its rest variant: after the steps of each
activation the overlay is held still, `REST` seconds of `measure.sh`, a
second after the frame at rest of the last step; R is the live passes a
second over those spans, of every window, and the frames of cause `live`
with them.

Made in the implementation, in the plan and above:

* `root.go` keeps `Pause` around an activation (D5): the snapshots of the
  cards stay the newest during it, and a live picture is drawn exactly
  when newer than its window's snapshot.
* Snapshots keep the one framebuffer of 008; live textures are made at a
  window's first pass.
* The snapshotter publishes a pass once its fence has signalled; the
  presenter tests no fence. A pass waits on the GPU for the presenter's
  last fence, and is not made while a frame that may draw its texture is
  drawn; before binding a pixmap again it waits for the X server's
  drawing, `glXWaitX`.
* The passes spread over the windows taking passes, waiting or passed
  within the interval; only of the cards and tiles in view, from the end
  of the fade-in to the start of the fade-out; none while frames come one
  after another, but 30 ms after they stop. A slice of windows shown
  other than the last wakes the snapshotter's loop.
* The live rectangle leaves out what the cards drawn after a card, and
  the labels of a tile, draw over the thumbnail; the border of a card is
  `2 / scale` px wide; a face keeps the bounds of its glyphs.
* A frame at rest presented again for a pass is a record "Frame" of cause
  `live`; `measure.sh` does not count it as a frame of a key. L counts a
  card in view, from when it came into view if later than the change.
* With 011 merged, `Switchers.Shown` takes the overlay to leave out; the
  snapshotter holds the switchers behind an interface of its own.
* D2 A: a window drawn into its frame's pixmap is passed by RENDER into a
  pixmap of qws's own, bound on the GPU; never the frame's.
* `live_interval` 50 ms by default (D6, the author, 2026-10-04).
* `measure.sh`: S5's window a mate-terminal or an xterm (`CHANGING_TERM`),
  second in the order of the switcher; the rest variant (`REST`), and K9
  read without `glFinish` from the pools (tasks).

Not checked: Mesa and ws3 (D7); ws7.

== Open decisions

. The bounds of K7 and K9, provisional until the first measurement on
  the screen.
. Passes in motion: none since `ae12383`, the guard of 4 ms before a
  frame having held the frames off the screen but not on the desktop.
  Should the pictures follow during long motions — a key held — a pass
  every few hundred milliseconds in motion might cost the frames little;
  not measured on the desktop.
. The pools of the font sets (007) and of the canvases of the heads (019)
  are `sync.Pool`s, which the drawings of the frames at rest found empty a
  third more often with live thumbnails on, and made their faces anew: a
  third of a point of a CPU in S5 (research, "S5 on the desktop, the first
  runs"). A list of free sets under a mutex would keep them on and off.
. The passes made cheaper by the damaged rectangles: a terminal that prints
  a line damages a band of a window, and a pass could average only the
  thumbnail rows under it — DAMAGE reports the region through XFIXES at a
  round trip a capture. Not measured.
. Mesa: sharing across Xlib displays, the contexts on one display
  otherwise, and whether a pixmap's texture there needs binding again (D7).
. 019, "heads kept across frames": the thumbnails a frame of the CPU is
  drawn from no longer change during an activation — live pictures are
  drawn by the GPU over them — so a head drawn for a tile stays valid for
  the activation but for the selection and the hover.
. Under `cpu`, semi-live thumbnails at a low rate, should the author want
  them there (D4).
. A pass from a frame asks the X server, at each pass, where the window
  lies in the frame, and makes and frees the pixmaps of its halvings:
  kept between passes, with the rectangle asked again on a ConfigureNotify
  of the window, a pass would send some 5 requests of RENDER instead of
  some 25, and save the snapshotter's CPU (results). Not made.
