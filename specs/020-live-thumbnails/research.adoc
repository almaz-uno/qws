== What the code does today

* *Snapshots* (008, 018; `pkg/snapshot`). The snapshotter has an xgb
  connection of its own, a DAMAGE object per client window
  (`ReportLevelNonEmpty`), and a GL context of its own
  (`glx.NewOffscreen`: a direct 4.6 core context on a pbuffer of 1×1, on an
  Xlib display of its own, current on the thread of its loop). A capture
  subtracts the damage, binds the window's pixmap again
  (`TexturePixmap.Rebind`), averages it by area into a framebuffer of at
  most 512×512 and reads it back into a new `image.RGBA` — never one changed
  in place. A window is captured 100 ms after a change (`settle`), at most
  once a `snapshot_interval`.
* *An activation* (`cmd/qws/root.go`, `handleKeyPress`). `Refresh(20 ms)`
  captures the changed windows; the images are put into the window list of
  the activation (`WindowInfo.Preview`, `WindowData.Thumbnail`) and stay
  there to its end; `Pause(true)` before `Show` stops the snapshots on
  change, and the deferred `Pause(false)` runs once `handleKeyPress` has
  evaluated `selector.FadeOut()` in its `return` — at the end of the
  fade-out.
* *The presenter's context* (`glx.NewContext`) has its own Xlib display as
  well; it is created by `NewSelector` at the first activation, on the
  thread of the main loop, without a share context. The snapshotter's is
  created when qws starts.
* *Frames* (007, 019). At rest, the frame the CPU draws, staged in pieces
  and presented byte for byte; in motion, scenes of layers drawn by the CPU
  from the same window list — the base, a card layer per window and integer
  offset, cross-faded; the tiles layer of the grid with the selection and
  hover layers. At rest the loop of the selector blocks in `WaitForEvent` of
  its own connection: nothing is presented but for an event.

== Where a thumbnail lies

Read in `pkg/carousel/renderer-cpu.go`, `fogleman/gg` v1.3.0 and
`golang.org/x/image` v0.46.0.

*How it is drawn.* `gg.DrawImage` calls `draw.BiLinear.Transform` with
`draw.Over`; a thumbnail is opaque — the snapshots write alpha 1 — so
`Transform` takes it as `draw.Src`. A pixel of the canvas whose centre maps
inside the thumbnail is written with the kernel's value whatever lay under
it, and no other pixel is: the kernel is a tent of support 1, widened to the
scale when shrinking ("When shrinking, broaden the effective kernel support
so that we still visit every source pixel", `impl.go`), its weights
normalized. A pixel of the thumbnail's interior is therefore a function of
the thumbnail image, its place and its scale alone, until something is drawn
over it.

*What is drawn over it.*

[cols="1,4",options="header"]
|===
| Where | Over the thumbnail

| Card
| The border, a stroke of 2 px centred on the edge — its inner half 1 px in,
  antialiased; nothing else. The shadow lies under the thumbnail; the
  selection frame begins 3.5 px outside its edge, the hover frame 3 px
  outside; the icon, the title and the workspace lie above and below it.

| Tile
| The border, a stroke of 1 px on the edge — 0.5 px in; the icon, 24 px at
  8 px from the tile's corner, over the thumbnail where they meet. The
  thumbnail ends 50 px above the tile's bottom and the urgent bar begins 45
  px above it; the selection and hover frames lie on the tile's edge, at
  least 8 px from the thumbnail.
|===

== What the specifications allow

`GLX_EXT_texture_from_pixmap`
(https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/extensions/EXT/GLX_EXT_texture_from_pixmap.txt):

* "The contents of the texture after the drawable has been bound are
  defined as the result of all rendering that has completed before the call
  to glXBindTexImageEXT"; "Rendering to the drawable while it is bound to a
  texture will leave the contents of the texture in an undefined state." A
  texture shows a change of its window for certain only once bound again.
* "The texture image is taken from <buffer> and need not be copied."
* Mipmaps: `GLX_MIPMAP_TEXTURE_EXT` sets storage aside, and
  `GenerateMipmapEXT` defines the levels.
* Nothing on share lists, on other contexts or other displays; a revision
  "removed language referring to sharing of color buffers when pixmaps are
  bound to textures".

Sharing between contexts — `GLX_ARB_create_context` and chapter 5 of OpenGL
4.6 core: the research of 013, "What the specifications allow". Textures and
sync objects are shared, framebuffers and vertex arrays are not; a change
made in one context is seen in another once complete and the object bound
again there; `glClientWaitSync` with a timeout of 0 tests a fence without
blocking.

== Sharing

`liveprobe share`: the window of its own, of 2556×1357, painted one colour
after another by a third connection; context A as the snapshotter's, on
display A; context B on display B, created with A as share context; B
draws a texture into a framebuffer of 64×64 and reads the mean colour back.

[cols="4,3",options="header"]
|===
| Step | Outcome

| A names the window's pixmap and binds it to texture T
| bound; `GLX_Y_INVERTED_EXT` 1

| B created with A as its share context
| created; `glIsTexture(T)` true in B

| B draws T, the window red
| red

| the window painted blue, T not bound again
| B draws blue

| A binds T again and makes a fence; B waits for it and draws T
| blue

| the window painted green; A binds T again and averages it into a
  thumbnail texture of its own, then a fence; B waits and draws the
  thumbnail
| green

| B binds A's GLX pixmap to a texture of its own, on display B, while A
  holds it bound
| X error 2, BadValue, on 152.16 (`glXBindTexImageEXT`)

| the same once A has released it
| bound; B draws the window, white by then

| B makes a GLX pixmap of its own of the window
| X error 11, BadAlloc, on 152.22 (`glXCreatePixmap`), as in 018

| D created with A as share context on a display of its own while A is
  current on another thread
| created; D draws T

| C created with B as share context, A binds T again
| C draws T as A last bound it
|===

One GLX pixmap serves every context of the process: the context that binds
it — the snapshotter's — owns it, and every context of its share group, on
any display and thread, draws its texture and the thumbnails averaged from
it. A second owner is refused. NVIDIA's texture is the pixmap's storage
itself: B saw the blue window without a new binding, which the extension
does not promise.

*The clock.* NVIDIA's driver sets the clock of the GPU to the load:
between frames of a fraction of a millisecond it stays at 210 MHz, P8, its
memory at 405 MHz; it rises only under loads of a millisecond or more a
frame. A light load is measured at the clock it leaves the GPU at — so at
210 MHz the frames of 1–36 thumbnails from snapshots cost 0.18–0.25 ms, 2 µs
a thumbnail, and the frames that average whole windows, at 0.3–2.6 GHz,
0.35–2.7 ms. Each window read in full costs a frame some 3.5 million texel
fetches, 14 MB.

*Per change*, one window at a time, 30 a second, at 210 MHz:

[cols="3,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Change | GPU p50 / p95, ms | CPU p50 / p95, ms

| bind again | 0.003 / 0.003 | 0.230 / 0.365
| bind again, make the mipmaps | 3.111 / 5.171 | 0.279 / 0.502
| bind again, average into 512×271 | 1.009 / 1.592 | 0.264 / 0.443
| the same, 36 windows at once, back to back (09:10) | 1.013 / 1.828 a window | —
|===

200 changes each after 30, at 08:38; the CPU is that of the calls, the GPU
that of the commands by a timer query. The longest call took 10.1 ms in
each variant then, under 0.8 ms at 09:10. 36 windows back to back, as at
an activation, did not raise the clock: 36 ms of GPU at 210–300 MHz.

Binding again costs the GPU nothing; the CPU of the thread that calls it
some 0.25 ms — a request to the X server — and up to 10 ms when the X server
is busy, as 008 found. The mipmaps of a window cost three times a pass into
a thumbnail.

== Beside the frames

`liveprobe paced`, after the probe of 013: a presenter in context B on a
window of 2520×1400 off the screen, swap interval 0, a frame due every
1/144 s — slept to 3 ms before it, then spun, as `waitFrame` does — of the
base copied and N thumbnails drawn bilinear from textures of 512×271, a
swap and `glFinish`. Meanwhile a second thread changes the N windows R
times a second through a connection of its own — a band of 20 rows painted
over and 150 strokes, a terminal printing a line — and, with display A,
follows them by the mode:

[cols="1,5",options="header"]
|===
| Mode | What follows the windows

| `clients` | nothing: the clients' drawing alone, the baseline
| `thumb` | A binds every changed window again and averages it into the
  other of its two thumbnails, all at once, then a fence; B draws the new
  thumbnails from the first frame whose fence test, `glClientWaitSync` with
  timeout 0, finds it signalled, never waiting for it
| `spread` | as `thumb`, one window at a time, spread over 1/R
| `inline` | A binds the changed windows again, then a fence; B averages
  them from A's window textures into thumbnails of its own, in the pause
  after a frame if it ends no sooner than 3 ms before the next — as the

| `live` | A binds them again; B averages each window's texture into its
  tile in every frame, option a
|===

*What the frames show.* The clock confounds the comparison: work that a
mode adds raises the clock, and the frames then take less — `spread` with
36 windows ran at 1.4–1.6 GHz and presented its frames sooner than the
clients alone at 240–270 MHz. Where the clocks were alike — 4 windows at
30 a second, 270–440 MHz — and over all the runs:

* `live` put 12–100 % of the frames over 2 ms: each frame averages whole
  windows, 2.3–3.3 ms on the GPU at p95.
* `thumb`, the passes all at once, 4.7–12.3 % at 30 a second, against
  1.0–10.1 % for the clients alone and 7.4–12.3 % where the clocks were
  alike: a burst of passes in the other context holds up the frames that
  come meanwhile, though no frame waits for its fence; p99 up to 5.8 ms.
* `spread`, 0.07–2.0 %, and `inline`, 0.8–3.5 %, stayed within a point or
  so of the clients alone, and below them where they raised the clock;
  the p99 of a frame 0.8–3.4 ms, but 5.3 ms in one run of `spread`.
* At 144 changes a second with 4 windows the clock rose to 0.7–1.5 GHz,
  and every mode but `live` left 0.2–0.6 % of the frames over 2 ms.
* `inline` with no bound on the windows a pause, B = 36, did as with 4.

*The snapshotter's CPU.* A pass of `spread`, binding again included, took
0.09–0.22 ms of its thread at p50; binding again alone, as in `inline` and
`live`, 0.08–0.19 ms a window; the passes of `thumb` 0.4–3.6 ms a burst.

Off the screen nothing waits for a vertical blank: a frame is late only by
the GPU or the CPU. What the vertical blank of picom makes of these
figures only the session on the screen tells, S5 of the specification.

*A frame that waits for a pass takes the pass in.* The first build of the
probe made B wait on the GPU, `glWaitSync`, for A's last fence before every
frame of `thumb` and `live`
(`runs/20261002-0840-blocking`, the first round, stopped there): with 36
windows at 10 a second a frame took 15.0 ms on the GPU at p95, 16.0 ms to be
presented. The presenter tests a fence and draws the older thumbnail; it
does not wait.

== The kernel on the GPU

`kernel` (`~/.cache/qws-probe-live/kernel`, Go, with the offscreen context
of `pkg/glx` and the code of the worktree): a snapshot of 512×271 about as
hard to scale as a window — lines of strokes on a light page, a dark panel,
a bar with a gradient — drawn into a frame of 2520×1400 at the places and
scales of E1 by `draw.BiLinear.Transform` with `draw.Over`, as
`gg.DrawImage` draws it, and by a fragment shader that computes the kernel
of `transform_RGBA_RGBA_Src` — for each pixel whose centre the inverse of
the matrix maps inside the snapshot, a tent widened to the scale when
shrinking, the weights normalized, the sum in 16 bits a channel truncated
and shifted by 8 — in single and in double precision; the pixels compared.

[cols="3,1,2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Place | Scale | Pixels | Equal, float / double | GPU, float / double

| card, offset 0 | 1.000 | 138 752 | 99.90 / 99.90 % | 0.004 / 0.121 ms
| card, offset 1 | 0.867 | 103 896 | 99.91 / 99.95 % | 0.008 / 0.231 ms
| card, offset 2 | 0.800 | 88 560 | 99.88 / 99.88 % | 0.007 / 0.203 ms
| card, offset 3, cut by the edge | 0.760 | 17 922 | 99.94 / 99.97 % | 0.003 / 0.051 ms
| tile 0, grid of 36 | 0.498 | 34 425 | 99.85 / 99.85 % | 0.004 / 0.117 ms
| tile 7, grid of 36 | 0.498 | 34 560 | 99.90 / 99.91 % | 0.004 / 0.119 ms
|===

Every pixel the CPU writes the shader writes, and no other; where they
differ, it is by 1 in a channel — likely a sum that falls on a step of the
truncation, which the two sides round to either side of it.
The cards at offsets 4 and 5 lie outside the frame at the spacing of 600.
Double precision is no closer and some 30 times slower; the GPU ran at 210
MHz but for a moment. The live picture can be exact but for 0.03–0.15 % of
its pixels, by 1, for 4–8 µs a thumbnail.

== Found on the way

* NVIDIA shows a window's change in its bound texture without binding it
  again ("Sharing"). qws binds again all the same: the extension promises
  the contents only then, and what Mesa does is not known.
* `glXCreateContextAttribsARB` takes as share context a context current on
  another thread and created on another display.
* A live pass needs no read back: `glReadPixels` and the copy into an image
  were most of the CPU of the snapshots in 008 ("After the services were
  replaced").
* Once A releases its binding, B can bind A's GLX pixmap on its own
  display ("Sharing"): the ownership of a window texture could pass from
  one context to another, at a release and a bind — not needed by the
  plan.
