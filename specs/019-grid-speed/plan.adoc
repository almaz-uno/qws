== Approach

*Head and tail.* `drawGridTile` is split into `drawGridTileHead` — the
shadow, the background, the thumbnail and its border, the icon, the urgent
bar — and `drawGridTileTail` — the title, the workspace, the frame. Each
pushes the state of the context, translates to the tile and pops it, so
every operation keeps its matrix; the order of the operations of a tile is
the order of `develop`, the urgent bar having been the first thing of the
title. The geometry both use — the place and scale of the thumbnail, of the
icon, the offset of the shadow — is computed by functions of their own
(`gridThumbnail`, `gridIconScale`, `gridShadowOffset`), with the arithmetic
of `develop`.

*The heads* (`pkg/carousel/gridheads.go`). `startGridTiles` lays the tiles
out, computes for each the rectangle of its head (`gridHeadBounds`), leaves
out the tiles whose rectangles meet another, takes a scratch canvas of the
size of the frame and starts the jobs, one a head, in the order of the
tiles, on `GOMAXPROCS/2` goroutines. A job resamples the thumbnail
(`prepareOpaque`), waits for the canvas, fills its rectangle of the scratch
canvas with the pixel the canvas has at its corner, and draws the head there
with a context of its own. `draw` reads those pixels once the canvas and the
header are drawn, lets the jobs go on, and draws the tiles in order: a tile
waits for its job, copies its head into the canvas if the canvas is that
pixel all over the rectangle (`tileHead.drawTo`) or draws it in place, then
draws its tail.

*The title* is truncated from the sums of the advances and kernings of its
prefixes, computed once. *`MultiFallbackFace`* keeps the results of
`GlyphAdvance` in a map.

== Why it is exact

For a tile i, let R be the rectangle of its head and c the pixel the job
filled it with. Every operation of the head writes only pixels of R
(`gridHeadBounds`), each as a function of that pixel alone, of the operation
and of the size of the canvas (research, "What keeps the bytes"). The scratch
canvas has the size of the frame, and in R it held c and only what the head
of i wrote: the rectangles of the heads drawn there do not meet. So if the
canvas, when the tile comes, is c all over R, drawing the head in place
would turn R into what the scratch holds in R and leave the rest of the
canvas as it is — which is what the copy does. If it is not, the head is
drawn in place. Either way the canvas after the head is that of `develop`
at the same point; the tail and the tiles after it are drawn in place, in
the same order.

The thumbnail of a job is resampled apart by the argument of specification
001: an opaque image in _src_ mode, the same affected rectangle, its written
pixels copied. The truncation compares the same widths: integer sums
(research). The advances kept are those the face would return again.
