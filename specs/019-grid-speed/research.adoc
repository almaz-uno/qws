The tiles layer, `GridTiles`, is the same work with `opaqueBounds`, 6 ms,
and `crop`, 4 ms, for 385 ms.

*Truncation.* `truncateTitle` measures the title, then every prefix with
`"..."` from the longest down until one fits. `MeasureString` gets the
advance of every rune from the face, and truetype loads and hints the glyph
for every call (`truetype.(*face).GlyphAdvance`): the title of Σ that does
not fit, 76 runes in a tile 357 px wide, takes some 40 prefixes, and its 4
tiles about 10 000 glyph loads a frame, 8 µs each.

*Resampling by size.* A program of `x/image/draw` alone, `BiLinear.Transform`
as gg calls it, 50 runs each:

[cols="3,1",options="header"]
|===
| Image | ms

| thumbnail 512×288 to 377×212 — a tile of Σ on E1 | 10.2
| thumbnail 512×512 to 196×196 — a tile of 29 on E1 | 11.9
| icon NRGBA 48×48 to 24×24 | 0.13
| 64×64 | 0.20
| 128×128 | 0.64
| 256×256 | 2.26
| 512×512 | 8.87

When it shrinks, the kernel widens its support to the scale, so the cost
goes with the pixels of the source. The icons of Σ are small; on E1 an
application that gives only a large `_NET_WM_ICON`, or whose icon is found as
a file by `WM_CLASS`, costs nearly as much as a thumbnail, in every tile of
the grid at once.

== What keeps the bytes

Read in `fogleman/gg` v1.3.0, `golang/freetype` and `x/image` v0.34.0.

* *A thumbnail is opaque* — the snapshots of specification 008 read back
  alpha 1.0 — so `Transform` resamples it in _src_ mode and writes pixels
  that do not depend on the canvas (specification 001, `prepareOpaque`).
* *The icon of a tile lies on its thumbnail*: the icon at 8–32 px from the
  corner of the tile, the thumbnail from 10 px. `prepareOnUniform` of 001,
  which needs one colour under the icon, does not apply.
* *An operation of gg writes a pixel as a function of the pixel under it.*
  A fill and a stroke compose a colour over the pixel by the coverage of the
  path (`raster.RGBAPainter`); the rasterizer gives spans only where the
  coverage is not zero, within the cells of the path. `Transform` writes the
  pixels of `adr`, the bounds of the canvas within those of the transformed
  source, and reads only the pixel it writes. Text is glyph masks composed
  through `Transform`.
* *The coverage depends on the size of the canvas*: `gg.NewContextForRGBA`
  makes `raster.NewRasterizer(w, h)`, which picks how finely to split curves
  by the size (`SetBounds`: `splitScale2`, `splitScale3`). Nor can a tile be
  drawn at another place: gg rounds `x·64` toward zero (`fix`), and `x+px`
  rounds differently from `(x−n)+px`. On a canvas of the size of the frame,
  at the same coordinates, a tile gets the same pixels.
* *Of a tile, the part under the title has bounds computed from geometry*:
  the shadow and the background, rounded rectangles; the thumbnail and the
  icon, the rectangle `placement` gives; the border, a stroke one pixel wide;
  the urgent bar. The arcs of gg are quadratic curves through the midpoint,
  whose control point is at 1.0012 of the radius — `2 − cos(π/64)` — from
  the centre. *The part over it is text*: its ink depends on the glyphs, and
  the workspace is not truncated at all, so it can reach the next tile.
* *A title is truncated by sums.* `MeasureString` (`x/image/font`) sums, in
  26.6 fixed point, the kerning of each pair and the advance of each rune,
  and gg takes `float64(sum >> 6)`. The sum for a prefix with the dots is the
  sum for the prefix, the kerning from its last rune to `.`, three advances of
  `.` and two kernings between dots; integer addition, so in any order.
* *The advance of a glyph is a function of the rune for a face* — which the
  drawing code already rests on. Faces are cached across frames, and the
  FontSets of specification 007 start with fresh ones: `TestParallelDrawing`
  compares the frames of fresh faces with those of faces used by the
  drawings before it. The hinter of truetype resets the graphics state for
  every glyph (`hinter.run`) and the twilight zone for every load
  (`hinter.init`); it keeps the CVT and the storage area, and a font whose
  glyph programs left there something another glyph read would make the
  frames of today depend on what was drawn before them — the digests of Σ on
  the order of the tests.
* *The translucent images of a frame, apart from the canvas.* For an
  `*image.RGBA` and an `*image.NRGBA`, `Transform` in _src_ mode into an
  `*image.RGBA64` (`transform_RGBA64Image_RGBA64Image_Src`) writes the 16-bit
  premultiplied colour that `transform_RGBA_RGBA_Over` and
  `transform_RGBA_NRGBA_Over` compose over the canvas: the code is generated
  from one template, with the same weights and sums, and `RGBA64At` is the
  colour the specialised code reads. Composed with their integer arithmetic,
  `(d·(0xffff−a)·0x101/0xffff + c) >> 8`, it gives their bytes over any
  canvas; a pixel not written stays zero and leaves the canvas as it is.
  `TestScenes` passed with the icons of the grid drawn so (the first
  prototype below); the heads took the icons over, and the change does not
  use it.

== Prototypes

On a copy of the code, the frame of `grid-e1-24-second`; every step kept the
digests of Σ.

[cols="5,1",options="header"]
|===
| Step | ms

| `develop` | 380
| thumbnails resampled in parallel, a goroutine each, with `prepareOpaque`;
  icons with the RGBA64 colour; titles truncated by sums | 75
| the same through a pool of workers, in the order of the tiles | 73–76
| the parts of the tiles under their titles — the heads — drawn in
  parallel into a scratch canvas | 58–60
| the advances of glyphs kept by the face | 45
| the thumbnails resampled before the canvas is made | 40
|===

The second row changed nothing: the drawing waited 19 ms for the thumbnail
of the first tile and then drew for some 50 ms on its own — the backgrounds
24 ms, the text 15 ms, the truncation 7 ms — while 24 thumbnails took less
than the wait. The heads took the backgrounds off the drawing; then the
text was the most of it, and the measuring of glyphs most of the text; the
canvas, 7 ms, was last.

== Workers

`grid-e1-24-second`, three runs of 40 frames for each number of workers;
the CPU time of the workers from the profile, per frame.

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Workers | ms a frame | CPU of the workers, ms

| 23 | 44.9, 45.1, 46.1 | 502
| 16 | 42.8, 42.3, 43.0 | 467
| 12 | 44.9, 45.6, 44.4 | 418
| 8 | 50.3, 51.8, 53.2 | 355
| 6 | 59.9, 61.7, 61.1 | 330
|===

Past the 12 cores, the second thread of a core adds nothing to the frame:
the resampling is arithmetic on floats, and two threads of a core share it.
Each thread then takes longer for its thumbnail: the work of the heads,
some 290 ms on one thread — resampling 265, backgrounds 23 — takes 502 ms of
CPU on 23 workers and 330 on 6. Not measured, but read in the scheduler:
with a worker on every CPU, the loop of the animation, which draws its
layers and frames at rest in the background and presents a frame every
6.9 ms, could wait for a processor until a worker is preempted, after up to
10 ms.

== Checks of the checks

Each on a copy of the change, undone after.

* A byte flipped in every head copied into the canvas: all 12 grid scenes of
  `TestScenes` fail, no carousel scene.
* Counted: in every grid scene of Σ every head is drawn apart and used;
  none is drawn in place.
* Without the check that the canvas is the colour of the head:
  `TestGridHeads/corners` and `/wide-workspace` fail. Without the tiles
  whose heads meet left out: `/tight` and `/small` fail. Without the shadow
  in the bounds of a head: the bounds check fails for the selected and
  hovered tiles.
* With a margin of 0 pixels instead of 2 the bounds check holds: the
  rectangles are already rounded out to whole pixels, and the margin is
  spare.
* `TestScenes`, `TestGridHeads`, `TestLayers`, `TestParallelDrawing` pass
  five times in a row, with `GOMAXPROCS` 1 and 3, and under `-race`.

== What would change pixels

* *Separable resampling.* `Kernel.Scale` — horizontal, then vertical — takes
  5.5 ms against 10.0 ms of `Transform` for the thumbnail of a tile of E1,
  but it places an image on whole pixels at a size of whole pixels, and the
  grid places its thumbnails at fractions of a pixel: at the place and size
  of a tile of Σ, 52 960 of 79 924 pixels differ, by up to 42. The same
  shape of `Transform`'s own arithmetic sums in another order. Not done.
* *Resampling on the GPU*, as the snapshots do: another filter. Not done.

== Exact options not taken

* *The weights of `Transform` once per column and row.* For a matrix without
  rotation — every image of the grid and the carousel — the weights of a
  column do not depend on the row; a copy of the kernel of `x/image/draw`
  that computes them once would give the same sums for less CPU. It would
  copy library code that Dependabot (specification 012) now updates. Not
  measured.
* *Heads kept across frames.* A step changes the heads of two tiles, the
  old selection and the new; the others are the same bytes as in the frame
  before, as long as the images of a window are not changed in place. It
  would leave a step a few milliseconds of drawing, but it has to know when
  a thumbnail changes — the live thumbnails reserved as specification 020.
* *`opaqueBounds` and `crop` of the tiles layer*, 10 ms of its 52: the
  bounds can be found from the edges inwards instead of over every pixel.
* *A scratch canvas kept for good*, as the window background is, instead of
  a `sync.Pool`: 14 MB held by each instance for fewer allocations.
