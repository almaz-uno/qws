== The refusal

The log of a second instance, R-before (spec, "Metrics"): for every
viewable window but one, "Snapshot on the GPU failed, taken on the CPU",
"glx: binding pixmap 0x…: X error 161". With the first error of the call
kept and its request (this change): 11 on 152.22 — BadAlloc on
`glXCreatePixmap`. The handler of `pkg/glx` kept the last error: the
`glXBindTexImageEXT` (vendor private, 152.16) that follows on the GLX pixmap
never made gets 161, GLXBadPixmap.

`bindprobe` (`bindprobe.c`, built with `gcc -O2 -o bindprobe bindprobe.c
$(pkg-config --cflags --libs gl x11 xcomposite)`) calls `glXCreatePixmap`
from up to two Xlib connections, with the FBConfigs that bind the depth as a
2D texture, chosen as `pkg/glx` chooses — 12 for depth 24, 3 for depth 32:

[cols="3,5",options="header"]
|===
| Pixmap | Outcome

| Its own, 64×64, depth 24, never shown
| The first: created. The same client again, with the same FBConfig or the
  second: 11 on 152.22. Another client, either FBConfig: 11 on 152.22. Once
  the first is destroyed, the other client: created.

| Its own window, 64×64, depth 32 in an override-redirect parent of depth 24
  at −4000,−4000, off every monitor: the X server redirects it on its own;
  each client names its pixmap, two names of one storage
| As above: the first created, every other attempt 11 on 152.22 — through
  the other name as well — until the first is destroyed.

| A terminal, 2556×1357, depth 32, a client window the service holds
| Every attempt: 11 on 152.22.

| Its frame, the top-level window of i3, which picom holds
| Every attempt: 11 on 152.22.
|===

One GLX pixmap at a time on a storage, whoever names it and with whatever
FBConfig. The reference page of `glXCreatePixmap` says so: "BadAlloc is
generated if there is already a GLXFBConfig associated with pixmap"
(https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL-Refpages/gl2.1/xhtml/glXCreatePixmap.xml).

The service and picom do not collide: a client window of ws1 has visual
0x82 and its frame 0x23, both of depth 32; a child whose visual differs from
its parent's is redirected by the X server on its own, with a pixmap of its
own. picom binds the frames, the top-level windows; qws binds the client
windows. Two instances of qws bind the same ones.

In R-before and R-after one window, a terminal of 2556×1392, was bound on
the GPU by the second instance: the service held no GLX pixmap of it then.
In a first run of R-before, at 22:06, every window was refused; a
measurement run of 1.2.0 went on from 22:18 to 22:21 between them, and a
window the service lost to it is bound again by the service only at its next
capture ("Found on the way") — likely, not shown.

== Scaling in the X server

The path of this change is the prototype of 008 (`~/.cache/qws-probe-cap2`)
but for one thing: each side is scaled on its own, the last pass from the
window's own extent at the scale of the halvings — W/2^k^ — not from the
width of the halved pixmap, which a halving of an odd side pads by a pixel;
the prototype scaled both sides by the ratio of the widths.

*It computes what it is meant to.* The same halvings and pass computed in
floating point on the CPU, against the thumbnail of RENDER: mean absolute
difference per channel 0.004–0.019, worst 1, on the image of K3; on an image
with a channel of noise, 0.07–0.26, worst 2, the thumbnail of RENDER darker
by 0.24 at most — the X server rounds down.

*Against the area average, on the windows of E1*, the four viewable at the
time — in memory, the pixmap named and read whole, nothing written:

| terminal, 2556×1357 | 0.47 | 0.94 | 0.31 | 56
| Claude, 2556×1357 | 1.57 | 3.06 | 1.23 | 64
| terminal, 2556×1392 | 0.98 | 1.58 | 0.66 | 56
| terminal, 2556×1392 | 1.31 | 2.17 | 0.94 | 56

The mean absolute difference per channel from the exact area average;
"prototype", the one factor of the prototype computed on the CPU. 008

*The image of K3.* `windowImage` of `xrender_test.go`: lines of text of
random lengths, a third of them blank — glyphs of two to four strokes a

windows.

== Found on the way

* Any second instance captured on the CPU since 1.1.0 — a test instance, a
  measurement run of `measure.sh`, a second service: some 100 ms of a CPU
  per window changed, at most once a `snapshot_interval` each.
* The tests of `pkg/snapshot` that start a snapshotter run as such an
  instance beside the service: `TestNewAndClose` took its snapshots on the
  CPU, now by RENDER.
* A second instance can win the binding of a window from the service: if
  it names the pixmap first after a map or a change of size, the service is
  the one refused. 1.2.0 then tries again at each capture — on the CPU
  meanwhile — and binds it once the other has ended; this change takes it
  by RENDER until the window is named anew.
