== What and why

Specification 008 leaves open: "A window never viewable in a session has no
snapshot but its placeholder, as now." i3 unmaps the frames of hidden
workspaces and hidden tabs, and an unviewable window has no contents in the
X server; so after qws starts, every window on a hidden workspace shows its
placeholder until it is shown once — after every restart of the service and
after a login. Asked on 2026-10-01 what could be done about it without
breaking anything, the draft of this specification researched it (research,
the sections up to "What qws does now") and found on the way a second case,
of windows once viewable: a window that changed after its last snapshot —
within the settle of 100 ms or within `behavior.snapshot_interval` — and is
then hidden, its workspace left or its tab switched, keeps an older
thumbnail than it could. The snapshotter frees the pixmap it holds of the
window at the unmap, and the change, never captured, is dropped.

The author decided both on 2026-10-05 (D1, D2): windows never viewable keep
their placeholder; a window hidden with a change not yet captured gets one
last snapshot, if the X server still gives its contents through what the
snapshotter holds. It does (T1, research, "The pixmap held after the
unmap"): a pixmap named before the unmap keeps the window's last drawing
after it, read on the GPU and by RENDER — the window's own pixmap however
the window becomes unviewable, its frame's when the frame is unmapped.

The specification is a delta to `develop` at `1de774d` (1.5.0). Unchanged:
which windows are captured and when, but for the one snapshot of a window
hidden, and for a window taken from its frame, which a restack of the frame
no longer makes stale and changed (M3); the settle and `snapshot_interval`
for windows viewable; the paths —
the GPU (008, 025), RENDER (018, 022), the CPU — and the choice among them;
live thumbnails (020); thumbnails of at most 512×512; nothing kept between
runs; the configuration — no key, variable or flag changes.

== Decisions

=== The author's, 2026-10-05

D1. *Windows never viewable keep their placeholder* — option A of the
draft. A window on a workspace i3 has not shown since qws started has no
contents in the X server (research, "Which windows have no contents"), and
nothing is kept between runs: the constitution is not amended. The options
researched and not taken are below, in "Options for windows never
viewable".

D2. *A window hidden with a change not yet captured gets one last
snapshot.* A window that changed after its last snapshot, within
`behavior.snapshot_interval`, and is then hidden — its workspace left, a tab
switched: i3 unmaps its frame — is captured once more as it becomes
unviewable, if the X server still gives its contents through what the
snapshotter holds: the named pixmap of the window or of its frame, the GLX
pixmap bound to it (008, 018, 022, 025).

=== Made on the way, 2026-10-05, taken by the author with the trial

M1. *At once, or when the pause ends.* The last snapshot is taken at the
next turn of the snapshotter's loop, without the settle or the interval:
nothing is drawn into the pixmap held after the unmap (research), so
waiting would only keep the pixmap longer. While snapshots on change are
paused — the switcher of any qws instance shown (007, 011), live thumbnails
included (020) — the pixmap is held and the snapshot taken when the pause
ends, as the snapshots due meanwhile are (011), or at an activation before
its first frame, as any window changed since its snapshot (008). The case
is common: a window chosen in the switcher on another workspace makes i3
hide the workspace shown during the fade-out, which runs paused.

M2. *Only from a pixmap that holds the window's last drawing.* The pixmap
named for the window and current at the unmap — not stale: named since the
window and its frame were last mapped and since their size last changed.
For a window drawn into its frame's pixmap (022), only when the frame
itself is unmapped: a window unmapped inside its frame still mapped is
painted over in the frame's pixmap by the frame's background (research). No
snapshot on the CPU: `GetImage` of a window not viewable gives nothing of
it.

M3. *A frame configured at its size keeps its pixmap.* A window taken from
its frame is made stale, its frame's pixmap named anew, only when the frame
is configured to another size or border, not at every `ConfigureNotify` of
the frame as 022 made it: Composite gives a window a new pixmap only for a
new size or border (research, "A frame configured"), and i3 restacks the
frames of the workspace it leaves right before it unmaps them (research,
"What i3 does when it hides a window") — by the rule of 022 every such
window would be stale at its unmap and get no last snapshot. The window's
offset in the frame is asked at each capture as before (022, D3).

M4. *No key.* The last snapshot is not configurable; its record "Snapshot"
says cause `hidden`.

== Behavior

. *The last snapshot.* A window of `_NET_CLIENT_LIST` that becomes
  unviewable — its frame unmapped by the window manager, or the window
  itself unmapped — with a change not yet captured, gets one snapshot from
  the pixmap the snapshotter holds of it, when that pixmap holds its last
  drawing (M2):
  * a window with a pixmap of its own (008), on either unmap: on the GPU,
    the GLX pixmap bound to it, after `glXWaitX` (025), or by RENDER from
    its own pixmap, when the GPU did not bind it (018);
  * a window drawn into its frame's pixmap (022), on the unmap of that
    frame: by RENDER from the frame's pixmap, at the window's offset in it.
+
A change not yet captured is drawing that DAMAGE reported since the last
snapshot, whether the window was waiting for the settle or for the
interval. The snapshot is of a new generation, as any; the record
"Snapshot" says cause `hidden`. The pixmap is freed after it, as it is at
an unmap now.
. *When* (M1). At the next turn of the loop. While snapshots on change are
  paused, the pixmap is held, and the snapshot taken when the pause ends or
  at an activation (`Refresh`), whichever comes first.
. *Superseded.* A window shown again before its last snapshot — its frame
  mapped — is captured as any window that becomes viewable, from a pixmap
  named anew; the pixmap held is freed then. A window resized or reparented
  meanwhile gets no last snapshot, as one stale at the unmap. A window
  destroyed or gone from the client list meanwhile is forgotten with what
  it holds, as now.
. *None* — the pixmap freed at the unmap, as now — for a window unchanged
  since its last snapshot; a window stale at the unmap, its pixmap not named
  since its map or its last change of size; a window drawn into its frame
  and unmapped itself, its frame still mapped; a window taken on the CPU,
  which holds no pixmap.
  A last snapshot that fails leaves the window its thumbnail, with a record
  at debug level.
. *A frame configured* (M3). A `ConfigureNotify` of the frame a window is
  taken from makes the window stale, and changed, only when the frame's
  width, height or border width differs from those it had when its pixmap
  was named. A frame moved still makes the window changed through DAMAGE,
  which the X server reports of the whole window at a move (research); the
  window is then captured from the pixmap it holds.
. *Windows never viewable* (D1) show their placeholder until they are
  shown once, as now.

== Options for windows never viewable

Researched by the draft, 2026-10-01 (research, the sections up to "What qws
does now"); the author took A (D1).

[cols="1,4,4,4"]
|===
| | What it gives | What it needs | What it risks

| A. As now — taken
| The placeholder until the window is shown once.
| Nothing.
| Nothing.

| B. Thumbnails kept in the X server
| On start, the thumbnails the previous qws kept of the windows still in
  `_NET_CLIENT_LIST`, matched by window ID: after a restart of the service,
  not after a login. Nothing is written to disk.
| The constitution amended: "qws keeps no state of its own between runs".
  An answer to "Window contents" for thumbnails kept in the X server after
  qws exits.
| Up to 1 MiB of the X server's memory per window — some 16 MiB on ws1 —
  left while no qws runs, until the next frees them or the session ends;
  more if a crash loses the property that names them. A thumbnail as old as
  the window's last view; the picture of a window destroyed meanwhile shown
  for a new one given its ID, until its first snapshot. Each instance keeps
  its own. A pixmap is readable by any X client that knows its ID, as the
  windows themselves are.

| C. Thumbnails on disk
| Thumbnails after a login too.
| The rule on "Window contents": contents written to disk, by file mode and
  place. A way to match a window of a new session to a picture of an old
  one — window IDs do not outlive the X server — by class and title.
| Pictures of closed and private windows on disk; a picture shown for
  another window of the same title; a picture of another day.

| D. Show the window to capture it
| A current thumbnail.
| Showing each hidden workspace at start, through i3, or mapping i3's frames.
| Seen by the user; the focus and the workspaces move; i3's frames mapped by
  another client. Breaks the desktop.

| E. `backing-store` `Always`
| Nothing on Xorg: `Always` is `WhenMapped`.
| Setting an attribute of every client window.
| Changes other clients' windows for no effect.
|===

Not taken: B keeps state between runs, which the constitution does not
allow, and the author did not amend it; C writes window contents to disk,
on which the constitution has no rule, and matches windows across sessions
by guess; D breaks the desktop; E has no effect on Xorg.

== Acceptance criteria

The tests are in `pkg/snapshot`, on windows of their own off the screen,
under the compositor running; each skips without an X display, a
compositing manager, RENDER or offscreen GLX. The window's snapshot is
checked against the area average of the picture drawn last: within 1 per
channel on the GPU, as K4 of 008; within a mean of 1.2 per channel by
RENDER, as K3 of 018. The two pictures of a test are a picture and its
negative, far apart.

[cols="1,6"]
|===
| # | Criterion and method

| K1
| The last snapshot, on the GPU: `TestHiddenSnapshot` — a window with a
  pixmap of its own, of depth 32 in a frame of depth 24, captured on the
  GPU, drawn anew, then hidden at once: its frame unmapped, as i3 hides it,
  and in a case of its own the window unmapped by its client. The events as
  the snapshotter's connection reads them, handled as the loop handles
  them, then the loop's turn: a snapshot of a new generation within 1 of
  the last picture, its pixmap freed after. Fails without the change: no
  new snapshot.

| K2
| The last snapshot by RENDER, in the same test: a window drawn into its
  frame (depth 24), the frame unmapped — from the frame's pixmap, also when
  the frame was restacked at its size just before, as i3 does (M3); and a
  window with a pixmap of its own that the GPU did not bind, as 018 leaves
  it — from its own pixmap. Each within a mean of 1.2 of the last picture.

| K3
| None when nothing is to be had: `TestHiddenNoSnapshot` — a window
  captured and not drawn since, hidden: no new snapshot, its pixmap freed
  at the unmap; a window drawn into its frame and unmapped itself, its
  frame still mapped: no new snapshot; a window drawn anew and hidden before
  its first capture, and one after a change of its size not yet captured:
  no snapshot.

| K4
| Nothing more for windows not hidden, in the same test: a viewable window
  drawn anew within the interval is due as its schedule says — an interval
  after its last snapshot — and not captured sooner, while another window
  is hidden and given its last snapshot; a frame restacked at its size
  leaves its window neither stale nor changed, moved at its size not stale
  — changed by the damage the X server reports of a move, as now — and
  resized, stale and changed, as before (M3). `TestSchedule`, `TestWait`,
  `TestCaptureFromFrame`, `TestLiveFromFrame` and `TestFramePassMoved`
  pass.

| K5
| The pause: `TestHiddenPaused` — a window hidden with a change while the
  snapshots are paused gets no snapshot while paused, its pixmap held, and
  its last snapshot, within 1 of the last picture, at the first turn of
  the loop once the pause ends; another, hidden alike while paused, gets it
  at an activation's capture of the changed windows.

| K6
| `go build ./...`, `gofmt -l .` empty, `go vet ./...`, `make check-specs`,
  `go test ./...` and `go test -race ./pkg/snapshot ./pkg/ui ./pkg/x11` pass
  on each commit of the change in a clean worktree; outside tests
  `xgb.NewConn` stays in `pkg/x11/extensions.go` and the two console
  programs, and `Init(` of an extension of xgb is not called but `gl.Init`
  (021).

| K7
| The author's trial on E1, open: the build of the change in place of the
  service, or beside it with its own keys; a terminal on a visible workspace
  printing a line every 50 ms (`while :; do date +%T.%N; sleep 0.05; done`),
  its workspace left at once by i3's key, then Alt+Tab: the card of the
  terminal shows the last lines it printed before the switch, not those of
  up to `snapshot_interval` earlier.
|===
