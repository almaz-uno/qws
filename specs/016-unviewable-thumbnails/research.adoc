== Which windows have no contents

The core protocol calls a window _unviewable_ "if it is mapped but some
ancestor is unmapped" (`GetWindowAttributes`). i3 hides a window on a hidden
workspace or in a hidden tab by unmapping its frame, the window's parent;
the window stays mapped and becomes unviewable (008, research, "Found on the
way"; i3, `x_push_node_unmaps` of `src/x.c`, `xcb_unmap_window` of
`con->frame.id`). Of the 30 client windows of ws1 when 008 counted them, one
was viewable.

Composite gives a redirected window a pixmap of its own only while it is
realized — mapped, with every ancestor mapped. `compCheckRedirect`
(`composite/compwindow.c`), which `compUnrealizeWindow` calls, allocates the
pixmap when the window becomes realized and, when it stops being so, hands
the window back to its parent's pixmap and destroys its own,
`DestroyPixmap`; a client that named it holds a reference, which
`ProcCompositeNameWindowPixmap` counts, so the pixmap lives on with what the
window showed. When the frame is mapped again, the window gets a new pixmap,
and the application an `Expose` to draw it anew. The Composite protocol says
so from the client's side (`compositeproto.txt`, `NameWindowPixmap`): a
named pixmap "will remain allocated until freed, even if 'window' is
unmapped, reconfigured or destroyed", "'window' will get a new pixmap
allocated each time it is mapped or resized", and the request "generates a
'Match' error if 'window' is not redirected or is not visible" —
`ProcCompositeNameWindowPixmap` (`composite/compext.c`) returns `BadMatch`
when the window is not viewable.

So the X server holds nothing of a window hidden before qws started: what
it showed then went with its pixmap. No client gets more — a compositor
neither: the server names no pixmap of an unviewable window to anyone.

== Backing store

The core protocol has a window attribute for contents kept while a window is
unmapped: `backing-store` `Always` "advises the server that maintaining
contents even when the window is unmapped would be beneficial" — advice the
server may not follow.

Xorg implements backing store by the automatic redirection of Composite and
offers `WhenMapped` only: `compScreenInit` (`composite/compinit.c`) sets
`backingStoreSupport` to `WhenMapped`, and `compChangeWindowAttributes`
redirects a window whose `backing-store` is anything but `NotUseful` — the
redirection that keeps a pixmap while the window is realized, as above.
`Always` is served as `WhenMapped`.

An implementation of `Always` exists only as a patch of Red Hat's,
`0001-composite-Implement-backing-store-Always.patch` of `xorg-x11-server`
1.17.4 in RHEL 6.8 and 6.9, whose first builds broke the ABI of the video
drivers (Red Hat bug 1484125). It is not in the X server: neither branch has
state for a window painted while unmapped, and `WindowRec` keeps
`backingStore` as the protocol's value alone.

Besides, `backing-store` is an attribute of the application's window;
setting it from qws would change another client's window.

== Making a window viewable

Only the window manager shows a window: i3 maps the frame when its
workspace is shown. qws could ask i3 to show each workspace in turn at its
start, or map the frames itself. Either is seen by the user — picom paints
every mapped window — moves the focus and the visible workspaces, and the
second maps windows of i3's that i3 did not map.

== Resources kept after a client exits

The core protocol lets a client keep its resources when its connection
closes: after `SetCloseDownMode` `RetainPermanent` or `RetainTemporary`, "all
resources ... allocated by the client are marked as permanent or temporary
... (but this does not prevent other clients from explicitly destroying
them)". `KillClient` with one of them destroys every resource of the client
that made it; they all go when the server resets, as when the session ends.

Root pixmaps rest on it: feh (`src/wallpaper.c`) sets `RetainPermanent`,
names the pixmap of the background in the properties `_XROOTPMAP_ID` and
`ESETROOT_PMAP_ID` of the root window, and frees the previous one by
`KillClient`.

qws could keep its thumbnails so — pixmaps in the X server, named in a
property of the root window by the window they belong to — for the next qws
to read and free. They would outlive a restart of qws, not the session. A
thumbnail is at most 512×512 pixels of 4 bytes, 1 MiB; one of a window of
2560×1381 is 512×276, 552 KiB; the 30 windows of ws1, some 16 MiB of the X
server's memory, or of the GPU's where the driver keeps pixmaps there. A
window keeps its ID for its life, so the ID names the same window across
restarts of qws within the session; a window destroyed meanwhile is gone
from `_NET_CLIENT_LIST`, though its client may give its ID to a new window,
which would show the old picture until its first snapshot.

== What qws does now

`pkg/snapshot/snapshot.go` at `1de774d`:

* `follow` selects the structure events of a client window of
  `_NET_CLIENT_LIST` and of its frame, and makes a DAMAGE object of the
  window, `ReportLevelNonEmpty`, which reports drawing into its children as
  well (022). `viewable` is the window mapped and its frame mapped, as the
  events tell.
* A `DamageNotify` marks the window changed (`schedule.change`); it is
  captured a settle of 100 ms after its first change, and no sooner than
  `snapshot_interval` — 1 s by default — after its last snapshot; `capture`
  subtracts the damage first, so that the next drawing reports again.
* `captureGPU` of a stale window names its pixmap, `NameWindowPixmap` — its
  own (008), or, when the X server does not redirect it, its frame's (022,
  `via`) — and binds its own on the GPU after `glXWaitX` (025); a pixmap the
  GPU will not bind, and the frame's always, is scaled by RENDER (018, 022).
  The pixmap stays named, and bound, while the window is not stale. It is
  made stale when it or its frame is mapped or unmapped, when it changes
  size or is reparented, and — a window taken from its frame — when the
  frame is configured; its pixmap is then freed at its next capture, at once
  at an unmap.
* `handle` of an `UnmapNotify` of the window or of its frame marks it
  unmapped and frees its pixmap at once (`release`); a window that comes due
  while unviewable is not captured: `capture` frees its pixmap and clears
  its change.
* Paused — the switcher of any instance shown (007, 011), live thumbnails
  included (020) — the loop takes no snapshot on change; those due meanwhile
  are taken when the pause ends; an activation, `Refresh`, captures every
  changed window before its first frame.

At its start the snapshotter captures every viewable window, within 15 ms on
ws1 (008, results, K3). A window never viewable since qws started shows its
placeholder until it is shown once: every window on a hidden workspace after
a restart of the service — an upgrade, `systemctl --user restart` — and
after a login, for the windows a session places on workspaces it does not
show. The constitution keeps both cases as they are: "qws keeps no state of
its own between runs", and its open question "Window contents" has no rule
on whether qws may write window contents to disk.

A window that changed after its last snapshot — within the settle or the
interval — and is hidden before it comes due keeps the older thumbnail: its
pixmap is freed at the unmap, and the change is dropped when it comes due.
The draft of this specification found it (its "Found on the way",
2026-10-01); the Composite protocol gives the named pixmap for exactly this
use, to "hold onto window contents after the window is unmapped (and hence
animate it's disappearance)".

== The events of the unmap

On the snapshotter's connection, after the last drawing and the unmap:
`DamageNotify` of the window, then `UnmapNotify` of the frame — or of the
window, when it was unmapped — in every round of every case, 432 of 432. The
X server queues a client's events in the order it generates them, and it
executed the drawing before the unmap; so the snapshotter reads the change
before the unmap, and handles the unmap while the pixmap it holds has the
drawing. A drawing the X server executes after the unmap reaches neither the
pixmap nor the damage: the window is unviewable then, as above.

== A frame configured

`TestProbe016Restack`: a window drawn into its frame, its frame's pixmap
named by a capture, then the frame restacked above another window of the
probe, moved by 10 pixels, or made 10 pixels wider; the events of the
frame read, and, in the run of 13:16, the damage of the window; a new
picture drawn and the pixmap held read by RENDER. The run of 13:05 gave the
same pictures.

[cols="2,3,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Frame | Its ConfigureNotify | Damage of the window | The new picture in the pixmap held

| restacked | 804×524, above the other window | none | yes, a mean of 0.647
| moved | 804×524 | all of it, 800×500 | yes, 0.647
| resized | 810×524 | all of it | no, 4.099 from it
|===

`compConfigNotify` (`composite/compwindow.c`) calls `compReallocPixmap`
(`composite/compalloc.c`), which makes a new pixmap only when the width or
the height of the window with its border, `w + 2·bw` and `h + 2·bw`, differs
from the pixmap's: a frame restacked or moved keeps its pixmap, one of a new
size or border gets a new one. A move, of the same pixmap, still reports
damage of the whole window inside; a restack none. The snapshotter makes a
window taken from its frame stale at every `ConfigureNotify` of the frame
(022, D3: "the frame's pixmap is named anew when the frame is configured — a
new size is a new pixmap"), and so drops the knowledge that the pixmap it
holds is the frame's.

== What i3 does when it hides a window

`x_push_changes` (`src/x.c`), the end of every change i3 makes to the
screen, first restacks the frames, then maps, then unmaps:

. walking its stack of frames from the bottom, once one frame has another
  above it than at the last push, every frame from there up is configured,
  `XCB_CONFIG_WINDOW_SIBLING | XCB_CONFIG_WINDOW_STACK_MODE`, above the one
  below it;
. `x_push_node` maps the frames to be shown;
. `x_push_node_unmaps` unmaps the frames to be hidden — the workspace left,
  the tabs not shown — setting `WM_STATE` withdrawn on their windows first.

`render_con` (`src/render.c`) raises each child it renders and, in a tabbed
or stacked container, the children in focus order, the focused last; a
workspace not shown is not rendered. On a switch of workspace, the frames of
the workspace shown come up to the top of i3's stack, above those of the
workspace left, which they were below: the order changes below the frames
left, and these are restacked — a `ConfigureNotify` each, at their size —
in the same push, before they are unmapped. On a switch of tab, the tab
shown is raised above the tab hidden alike.

So a window drawn into its frame and hidden by i3 is, by the rule of 022,
stale when its unmap arrives, though the frame's pixmap it holds is still
the one its last drawing went into.

This is i3's code, read, not i3 watched: `observe016` logged the structure
events of the children of the root of E1 from 12:56 — selecting
`SubstructureNotify` on the root, as any client may, and changing nothing —
and by 13:18, when this was written, no frame of i3 (IDs `0x80….`) had been
configured, mapped or unmapped: the desktop was not used meanwhile, and
qws's tests do not drive it. Criterion K2 of the specification has a test
make the same sequence with a frame of its own: restacked at its size, then
unmapped.

== Found on the way

By the code at `1de774d`, not measured: a window taken from its frame whose
frame is restacked while the switcher is shown gets no live pass for the
rest of the activation — the restack makes it stale (022), `livePassable`
passes no stale window (020), and no snapshot names its pixmap anew until
the live passes end. A frame kept across a restack, as the change makes it,
removes that as well.
