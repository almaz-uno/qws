== What and why

A window drawn into the pixmap of its frame — of depth 24 in a frame of
depth 24, which the X server does not redirect apart from it: xterm, Dion,
Telegram, KeePassXC and ilovlya on ws1 (022, 020) — has no pixmap of its
own, and the frame's is the compositor's. Since the author's answer to D2 of
specification 020 (A, 2026-10-03), such a window takes live passes by
RENDER: its rectangle in the frame's pixmap is copied out, halved and
scaled in the X server into a pixmap of qws's own of the thumbnail's size,
which is bound on the GPU and copied into the live texture after
`glXWaitX` (020, Behavior, "From the frame").

Such a pass costs more than one from a window's own pixmap (research, "What
020 measured"): for a window of 2556×1357, 2.40–2.47 ms of qws's CPU and
0.8–1.3 ms of the X server's, against 0.97–1.00 ms and 0.1–0.9 ms. A window
of that size changing all the time, passed every 33 ms, costs some 7 % of
a CPU of qws and 3 % of the X server's. K9 of 020 allows 3 % of a CPU of
qws for all the live thumbnails of S5, and the second runs of S5, with a
mate-terminal of depth 32 changing, used 2.36 of them in the grid. In the
runs at rest of 2026-10-03, an xterm of 604×394 passed from its frame,
beside the Claude app changing all the time, raised `C shown` of the grid
by 8.67 points, against 7.40 with a mate-terminal in its place: 1.27 points
are the frame's (research, "At rest, 2026-10-03").

Where it goes (research, "What a pass from the frame sends"): a pass sends
14 + 7k requests on the snapshotter's connection, k the number of halvings —
28 for the window of 2556×1357, 14 for the xterm — where a pass from a
window's own pixmap sends one. Three of them are round trips: where the
window lies in the frame, `TranslateCoordinates` and `GetGeometry`, asked
at each pass, and the `GetInputFocus` by which xgb checks the composites.
The others make and free at each pass what the halvings need — the copy of
the window and its graphics context, the source picture, a pixmap and a
picture a halving, their transforms and filters — with 3 + 2k new resource
IDs. xgb writes each request on its own, through its goroutines, while the
thread the GL context is locked to waits for each. And the copy, made
through a graphics context with graphics exposures on, sends the
snapshotter a NoExposure event at each pass, which wakes its loop for
nothing.

The change: the same pictures for a fraction of the requests. What the
passes of a window need in the X server is made at its first pass of an
activation and kept to the end of it; where the window lies is checked by
the one round trip a pass needs anyway; the copy sends no event. A pass
then sends k + 4 requests — 6 for the window of 2556×1357, 4 for the
xterm — with one round trip, no event and no new ID. It takes up the last
open decision of 020, which proposed the chain kept and the rectangle asked
again on a ConfigureNotify (D2 b).

The author asked (issue #66) for the change to be described in a
specification of its own and done later, if needed; on 2026-10-03, with K9
of 020 unmet in the rest variant of S5, the author took its decisions as
recommended and asked for it to be done (020, K9, option c).

The specification is a delta to 020 as it lands with pull request #60 — the
branch `issue/qws#58-live-thumbnails`, read at `fe9e85f` (2026-10-03),
merged at `be34624` into the branch of this one, which builds on it.
Unchanged: which windows take passes and when — the schedule, the pause and
the windows in view of 020; the passes of the windows with a pixmap of their
own; the scaled pixmap of qws's own, its binding again, `glXWaitX` and the
copy into the live texture; the pictures of the passes from the frame, byte
for byte; the snapshots of 018 and 022 — their pictures, and their requests
but for their order and the graphics exposures of the copy; the frame's
pixmap never bound on the GPU (022, D2); the configuration — no key,
variable or flag changes. With no behavior the user sees, it goes out as a
patch of the release that carries 020, or with it.

== Decisions

Author, 2026-10-03: as recommended — D1 b, D2 c, D3, and a for D4–D7 —
and the change made (issue #66), so that K9 of 020 may be met with its
bound kept (020, K9, option c). Each lists its options, with what it saves,
its risk and what it does to the picture, and the one recommended, which is
the one taken.

=== D1. What the passes keep in the X server

[cols="1,3,3,3"]
|===
| | Option | For | Against

| a
| *Nothing, as 020.* Each pass makes and frees the copy of the window, its
  graphics context, the source picture, a pixmap and a picture for each
  halving, and sets the transform and the filter of each picture.
| Nothing held in the X server between passes.
| 10 + 7k requests a pass for the scaling alone, 24 at k = 2, and 3 + 2k new
  resource IDs; some 18 MB of pixmaps made and freed in the X server at each
  pass of a window of 2556×1357.

| b
| *The chain kept for the activation.* The copy of the window — a pixmap of
  its size and of the depth of the pixmap it is taken from — its graphics
  context, the source picture, the pixmaps and pictures of the halvings,
  each picture with its transform and filter, are made at the window's first
  pass of an activation and kept until the end of the activation's passes,
  the window forgotten, or another size, visual or depth.
| A pass sends the copy and k + 1 composites: 4 requests at k = 2 instead of
  24, 2 at k = 0 instead of 10; no new ID; nothing made or freed in the X
  server a pass. The same requests on the same pixels: the pictures byte
  for byte.
| The X server holds the chain while the switcher is shown: 18.2 MB for a
  window of 2556×1357 — the copy 13.9, the halvings 3.5 and 0.9 — and 1 MB
  for the xterm, for each window drawn into its frame and in view, a few at
  most. The first pass of a window in an activation costs as now, and the
  freeing at its end 3 + 2k requests.

| c
| *The chain kept for the session*, the snapshots of 022 taken through it
  too.
| Not made again at each activation; the snapshots of such windows cheaper.
| Up to some 18 MB held in the X server for every such window for as long
  as qws runs — 4–5 windows on ws1, up to 70–90 MB — for snapshots taken at
  most once a second.
|===

*Recommended: b*, taken.

=== D2. Where the window lies in the frame

[cols="1,3,3,3"]
|===
| | Option | For | Against

| a
| *Asked at each pass, as 020*: `TranslateCoordinates` of the window to the
  frame and `GetGeometry` of the frame for its border, before the copy.
| Always current.
| Two round trips a pass: 2.40 ms of qws's CPU a pass against 2.17 when
  asked once (020).

| b
| *Kept, and asked again after a ConfigureNotify* of the window, or of its
  parent where the window is taken from the parent's parent — as the last
  open decision of 020 proposed.
| One round trip a pass, the check of the composites.
| Rests on the events. xgb drops the bit that tells a synthetic
  ConfigureNotify — i3's, in root coordinates (ICCCM 4.1.5) — from the X
  server's own (research), so an event can only make qws ask again, not tell
  where. A window lower in the tree moves with windows whose events the
  snapshotter does not select: asked at each pass there, as a.

| c
| *Kept, and checked by the round trip of the pass.* The frame's border is
  kept from its naming, where 022 asks the frame's geometry already; the
  offset as the X server last told it, at a snapshot or a pass. The pass
  sends `TranslateCoordinates` after its composites, instead of the
  `GetInputFocus` xgb sends to check them: its reply checks them as well and
  tells where the window lies now. Should the window lie elsewhere than where
  the copy was taken, the pass is made again at once at the new place.
| One round trip a pass, as b, without relying on events, at any depth. A
  window moved in its frame — a title bar shown or hidden — costs one pass
  more.
| A pass whose window moved sends its requests twice. The border is that of
  the naming: a frame configured makes the window stale and named anew
  (022), the border asked with it — as the pixmap named, which a frame of
  another size no longer draws into.
|===

*Recommended: c*, taken.

=== D3. The event of the copy

The copy's graphics context is made with no values, so with graphics
exposures on, the protocol's default: each `CopyArea` sends a NoExposure
event, which xgb reads, the snapshotter's reader passes to its loop, and the
loop, woken on the thread GL is locked to, matches with nothing — 30 a
second for a window passed every 33 ms (research, "The event of each copy").
*Recommended*, taken: the graphics context made with graphics exposures
off, for the snapshots too. Nothing changes in the
pictures.

=== D4. How the requests reach the X server

[cols="1,3,3,3"]
|===
| | Option | For | Against

| a
| *As now*: from the loop, on the thread the GL context is locked to, each
  through xgb, whose writer goroutine writes it on its own — one `write` a
  request — while the caller waits.
| Nothing to change.
| A hand-over between the locked thread and xgb's goroutine a request: 6 a
  pass after D1 b and D2 c.

| b
| *From a goroutine not locked*: the loop hands the requests of a pass to a
  goroutine of its own and waits for it once.
| The waits for xgb's writer become switches of goroutines; the same
  requests, the same pictures.
| A goroutine and a hand-over more, for the some 6 requests D1 b and D2 c
  leave.

| c
| *One write a pass.*
| One system call and one hand-over a pass.
| Not possible through xgb 1.3.1: `NewRequest` numbers one request a call,
  and a buffer of several would put its sequence numbers behind the X
  server's (research). Needs xgb changed, upstream or in a fork.

| d
| *Through Xlib*: `libXrender` on the Xlib display of the GL context.
| Buffered, flushed by the `glXWaitX` a pass makes anyway, no goroutine: no
  xgb request in a pass but DAMAGE's.
| A library more to build with (`libxrender-dev`, `pkg-config xrender`);
  the IDs of the pixmaps named on the snapshotter's connection used on
  another; the composites checked by `XSync` and the error handler of
  `pkg/glx`, not by xgb's cookies. The largest change.
|===

*Recommended: a* for this specification, taken; c rejected; b or d should
the measurement after D1–D3 leave K5 unmet — an open decision.

=== D5. How the window is scaled

[cols="1,3,3,3"]
|===
| | Option | For | Against

| a
| *Halvings, as 018, 022 and 020*: bilinear passes at ½ along each side
  more than twice the thumbnail's, then one bilinear pass.
| The pictures of 020, the snapshots', byte for byte.
| k + 1 composites a pass, and the chain of D1.

| b
| *One bilinear composite* at the scale of the thumbnail.
| One composite; of the chain, the copy alone.
| At a fifth a pixel of the thumbnail takes 4 of the some 25 under it: text
  aliases, as bilinear filtering did on the GPU (020, D1 a). Far from the
  bound of 022, a mean of 1.2 per channel from the area average, and from
  the snapshots.

| c
| *One composite with the filter `convolution`*, a box of ⌈s⌉×⌈s⌉ at the
  scale s.
| One composite; at an integer scale the area average.
| Another picture than the snapshots': 022's would change alike, or a live
  card would differ from its snapshot; at a fractional scale not the area
  average either. Some 25 taps a pixel of the thumbnail, 3.5 million a pass
  at E1's window, by pixman's general convolution in the X server, which no
  driver here is known to do on the GPU (research, "RENDER's filters"). Not
  measured.

| d
| *The GPU averages a copy of qws's own*: the window copied out of the frame
  into a pixmap of qws's own of its size, bound on the GPU — qws's storage,
  not the frame's (022, D2) — and averaged as a window's own pixmap is.
| A pass sends the copy and the check, 3 requests whatever the size; the X
  server only copies. The picture of every other live window: the area
  average of 008, within 1 of the CPU's, without the blur of the halvings on
  strokes of a pixel — 92–93 off the area average at worst (022).
| Another picture: the snapshots of such windows (022) would move to the same
  path, or a live card would differ from its snapshot by RENDER's error — a
  change of 022 beyond what #66 asks. A pixmap of the window's size held and
  bound for the activation, 13.9 MB at E1's window, and the GPU's average of
  the whole window a pass, as for every other window.
|===

*Recommended: a*, taken: the same pictures, as #66 asks. d is left open, for a specification of its own should the author want
the area average for these windows.

=== D6. The copy out of the frame

[cols="1,3,3,3"]
|===
| | Option | For | Against

| a
| *Copied, as 022 and 020*, into a pixmap of the window's size.
| The passes pad at the window's own edges: the pictures.
| A copy of the window in the X server a pass, 13.9 MB at E1's window, and
  its pixmap in the chain of D1 b.

| b
| *Read in the frame's pixmap*, the transform of the first pass translated
  to the window.
| No copy, no pixmap of the window's size; one request less a pass.
| The pad at the window's edges is lost: where a side of the window is
  halved and of odd length, the last column or row of the first halving
  takes half of the frame's pixel beyond the window — its border — instead
  of the window's own, and the outermost one or two pixels of the thumbnail
  on that side differ. The same bytes only where every halved side is even,
  and at k = 0, where a bilinear pass at a scale of 1 or more reads nothing
  beyond the window with a weight.
|===

*Recommended: a*, taken.

=== D7. The wait for the X server

[cols="1,3,3,3"]
|===
| | Option | For | Against

| a
| *`glXWaitX`, as 020.*
| 0 of 200 passes read the scaled pixmap as it was before, against 10 of 200
  without (020).
| The X server's CPU: 0.8–1.3 ms a pass with it, 0.34–0.61 ms without (020)
  — some half of the X server's work of a pass.

| b
| *A fence of the X server*: the snapshotter triggers a fence of the SYNC
  extension after the composites, which the GL context imports
  (`GL_EXT_x11_sync_object`) and waits for on the GPU — as picom's
  `--xrender-sync-fence` does on NVIDIA.
| Neither process waits on its CPU; the round trip of the check might go
  too.
| SYNC is not among the extensions 021 sets up, and xgb 1.3.1 has no package
  for it: its requests built by hand. NVIDIA is the only driver known to
  have the GL extension (research, "Fences of the X server"). Whether its
  fence orders the X server's drawing before the GL context's reading as
  `glXWaitX` does is to be measured anew — the 0 of 200.
|===

*Recommended: a*, taken; b is an open decision, once D1–D3 are measured.

== Behavior

On `glx` with live thumbnails on, for a window taken from its frame's pixmap
(022) and passed live (020, D2 A); everything else as in 020.

. *The chain.* At the window's first pass of an activation, the snapshotter
  makes in the X server what its passes need: a pixmap of the window's size
  and of the depth of the pixmap it is taken from, with a graphics context
  whose graphics exposures are off, and its picture in that pixmap's visual,
  padded at its edges; for each halving a pixmap a8r8g8b8 and its picture;
  and it sets the transform and the bilinear filter of each picture once.
  The sizes, the transforms and the filters are those 020's passes use. A
  window at the corner of its frame's pixmap has no copy, as now: the
  source picture is that of the frame's pixmap, and the chain is made anew
  with the pixmap named.
. *A pass* sends the copy of the window's rectangle out of the frame's
  pixmap into the chain's, the composites of the halvings and of the last
  pass into the scaled pixmap of 020, and `TranslateCoordinates` of the
  window to the frame. Its reply checks the composites — a failed one leaves
  the window stale, as in 020 — and tells where the window lies. Should it
  lie elsewhere than where the copy was taken, the pass is made again at
  once at the new place, once; should the window have moved again, it waits
  for its next pass as a window changed. Then `glXWaitX`, the scaled pixmap
  bound again and copied into the live texture, the fence: as in 020.
. *Where the window lies* is kept for the window: the frame's border from
  the naming of its pixmap (022 asks the frame's geometry there), the offset
  as the X server last told it — at the window's last snapshot, which asks
  it as now, or at its last pass. 020's "asked of the X server at each pass"
  becomes "checked at each pass".
. *Freed.* The chain is freed at the end of the activation's passes — when
  the live passes end, `SetLive` without an overlay — when the window is
  forgotten, and when a pass finds the window of another size, or its pixmap
  of another visual or depth, and makes another. The scaled pixmap of 020
  stays as 020 keeps it.
. *Snapshots* of such windows (022) keep their pictures and their requests,
  made and freed at each snapshot as now, but for the order of the requests
  and the graphics exposures of the copy, off as for the passes.
. *Unchanged*: the schedule, the pause and the windows in view of 020; the
  passes of the windows with a pixmap of their own; a pass that fails and a
  scaled pixmap that will not bind, as in 020; no pass without RENDER.

=== Existing configurations

None changes: no key, variable or flag is added, renamed or reinterpreted.

== Metrics

R — requests of a pass:: The requests a pass sends on the snapshotter's
connection, with nothing else sending on it — a test off the screen: a
`GetInputFocus` before the pass and one after, the difference of their
sequence numbers less one; and the events the connection receives meanwhile.
The requests of the GL context's Xlib display — binding again, `glXWaitX` —
are those of 020 and are not counted.

P — the cost of a pass:: `BenchmarkLivePassFrame` of 020, a window in its
frame off the screen passed 30 times a second: qws's CPU a pass
(`qws-cpu-ms/pass`), the X server's (`xorg-cpu-ms/pass`), the pass on the
snapshotter's thread and to its publication, at p50 and p95. At 2556×1357,
k = 2, as 020 ran it, and at 604×394, k = 0, the xterm of S5;
`BenchmarkLivePass`, a window's own pixmap, in the same runs as the
reference.

C, A1, R at rest, L:: As in 020.

Session S5x:: S5 of 020 with the xterm `measure.sh` starts
(`CHANGING_TERM=xterm`) — 100×30, 604×394, floating on DP-4, of depth 24
and drawn into its frame's pixmap — as the window changing all the time; the
carousel and the grid, `live: true` and `live: false` in turn, three runs
each. Its rest variant: 020's, the grid only — the carousel had no window
changing in view at rest — with the xterm and with the mate-terminal,
`live: true` and `false`, three runs each. X, the frame's share of C at
rest: `C shown` with `live: true` less `live: false` for the xterm, less the
same for the mate-terminal — 1.27 points on 2026-10-03.

== Open decisions

. The window of S5x is a floating xterm of 604×394, k = 0, the cheapest
  pass from a frame; a window of E1's size changing all the time —
  ilovlya, a maximized Dion — is measured off the screen only (K5).
. The bounds of K5 and K6, provisional until the first measurement; the
  savings they rest on are estimates (research, "What the change is
  expected to save").
. Which part of the X server's 0.8–1.3 ms a pass goes to the pixmaps made
  and freed, the copy, the composites and the wait is not measured; D6 b
  and D7 b rest on it.
. The passes of ilovlya at rest took 3.4–5.5 ms at p50 on the snapshotter's
  thread, against 1.57–1.63 ms for the xterm and 1.48–1.57 ms off the
  screen for a window of nearly its size; why is not known (research, "At
  rest, 2026-10-03").
. The frames: in the runs at rest the steps of the grid missed 8.14 % of
  their intervals with the xterm against 5.63 % with the mate-terminal
  (0.91 and 0.68 % with `live: false`). K8 of 020 is 020's; what this
  change does to the frames is read in S5x, not bound.
. D4 b or d, and D7 b, should the measurement after D1–D3 leave K5 or K6
  unmet; D5 d, a specification of its own should the author want the area
  average for the windows drawn into their frame.
. Found on the way, for an issue of its own: xgb hands out the resource IDs
  of a connection in order and reuses none unless given a function to, and
  qws gives none. The snapshots of 022 alone, of a window of E1's size
  changing all the time, would use up the snapshotter's 2 097 151 IDs in
  some 2.7 days, after which it names no pixmap and takes no snapshot by
  RENDER (research, "Found on the way: resource IDs"). D1 b takes the
  passes out of it; the snapshots stay.
