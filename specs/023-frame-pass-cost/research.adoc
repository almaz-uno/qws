== What a pass from the frame sends

The code at `fe9e85f`. `livePass` (`pkg/snapshot/live.go:400`) subtracts
the window's damage (`:434`), as for every pass, and for a window taken from
its frame calls `scaleFromFrame` (`:497`): `scaledFor` (`:522`) gives the
scaled pixmap of qws's own — made, with two checked requests and its
binding, at the window's first pass only; `pixmapRect`
(`pkg/snapshot/snapshot.go:800`) asks where the window lies,
`TranslateCoordinates` of the window to the frame (`:805`) and `GetGeometry`
of the frame for its border (`:809`); `xrender.scale`
(`pkg/snapshot/xrender.go:151`) sends the scaling; each composite's cookie
is checked. Then `WaitX` (`live.go:444`), `glXWaitX` on the GL context's
Xlib display (`pkg/glx/pixmap_linux.go:215`), and `Rebind` (`live.go:446`),
the release and the binding of the scaled pixmap on that display without a
round trip (`pixmap_linux.go:209`).

`scale` copies a rectangle not at the corner of its pixmap — a window in its
frame, below the frame's title bar or border — into a pixmap of its own, so
that the passes pad at the window's edges (022), then halves along each side
more than twice the thumbnail's, k times, and makes one last bilinear pass
into the picture it is given. What it makes is freed when it returns
(`:163–170`). For k halvings, on the snapshotter's connection:

[cols="3,5,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| Where | Requests | Count | Replies

| `livePass`, every pass | `DamageSubtract` | 1 | —
| `pixmapRect` | `TranslateCoordinates`, `GetGeometry` | 2 | 2
| `scale`, the copy (`:184–197`) | `CreatePixmap`, `CreateGC`, `CopyArea`, `FreeGC` | 4 | —
| `scale`, the source (`:178`) | `CreatePicture`, padded | 1 | —
| `scale`, a halving (`:223–238`) | `CreatePixmap`, `CreatePicture`, `SetPictureTransform`, `SetPictureFilter`, `Composite` | 5k | —
| `scale`, the last pass (`:235–238`) | `SetPictureTransform`, `SetPictureFilter`, `Composite` | 3 | —
| `scale`, on return | `FreePicture`, `FreePixmap` | 2k + 2 | —
| `scaleFromFrame`, the check | `GetInputFocus`, sent by xgb's `Check` | 1 | 1
| *A pass* | | *14 + 7k* | *3*
|===

Besides: one NoExposure event a pass ("The event of each copy"); 3 + 2k new
resource IDs — the copy, its graphics context, the source picture, a pixmap
and a picture a halving; on the GL context's display, the two requests of
`Rebind` and the round trip of `glXWaitX`. A pass from a window's own pixmap
sends `DamageSubtract` alone on the snapshotter's connection, and `Rebind`.

== How xgb sends a request

xgb 1.3.1, `github.com/jezek/xgb@v1.3.1`:

* `NewRequest` (`xgb.go:396`) puts the request on the connection's channel
  of requests, buffered for 100, and waits until `sendRequests` (`:416`) has
  numbered it (`:445`) and written it — `writeBuffer` (`:473`), one
  `c.conn.Write` a request, no buffering. One `NewRequest` is one sequence
  number: a buffer holding several requests would leave xgb's numbers behind
  the X server's, and every reply after it matched to the wrong cookie.
* `Check` of a checked request without a reply (`cookie.go:148`) returns at
  once if a later reply has been read — the reader marks the void cookies
  before a reply as done — and otherwise sends `GetInputFocus` (`Sync`,
  `sync.go:8`; `cookie.go:168`) and waits for it: the `GetInputFocus` of a
  pass from the frame, after which no reply comes.
* `NewId` (`xgb.go:240`) takes the IDs from `generateXIds` (`:264`), which
  hands out those of the client's mask in order and reuses none unless a
  function is given with `SetIDRangeFunc` (`:177`).
* The reader drops the bit of an event's code that marks it sent by
  `SendEvent` (`xgb.go:556`); `xproto.ConfigureNotifyEvent` has no field
  for it. The synthetic ConfigureNotify events i3 sends a client window, in
  root coordinates (ICCCM, 4.1.5; 022), cannot be told from the X server's.
* The snapshotter's loop runs on the thread its GL context is locked to
  (`glx.NewOffscreen`, `pixmap_linux.go:248`, `runtime.LockOSThread`). A
  goroutine so locked that waits — for xgb's writer at each request, for the
  reader at each reply — stops its thread and hands its processor to
  another, which wakes it again when the wait ends: two switches of threads
  a request, where an unlocked goroutine would switch on its thread.

== The event of each copy

`scale` makes the copy's graphics context with no values
(`xrender.go:194`), so with graphics exposures on, the protocol's default
of `CreateGC`. With graphics exposures on, `CopyArea` sends GraphicsExposure
events for the parts of the destination it could not take from the source,
and NoExposure when there are none — always, for a pixmap's source. xgb
reads the event; the snapshotter's `read` passes it to the loop through its
channel of events; the loop, woken on its thread, finds no case for it in
`handle` and computes its timer again. Every pass from the frame, and every
snapshot of 022, sends one.

== RENDER's filters

`render.h` of the RENDER protocol on ws1
(`/usr/include/X11/extensions/render.h`) names the filters `nearest`,
`bilinear` and `convolution`, and the aliases `fast`, `good` and `best`. A
convolution filter is a kernel of a fixed size and fixed weights in the
pixels of the source, given with `SetPictureFilter`, applied around the
point the transform takes each pixel of the destination to — so at a
fractional scale not the area average. Which filters Xorg 21.1.7 and the
driver of NVIDIA offer, and which of them the driver does on the GPU,
`QueryFilters` and a measurement would tell; neither was asked here.

== Fences of the X server

`GL_EXT_x11_sync_object` imports a fence of the X server's SYNC extension
into GL, for the GL context to wait on the GPU for what the X server drew
before the fence was triggered. picom's `--xrender-sync-fence` uses it, "to
make sure all draw calls are finished before picom starts drawing", needed
"on nvidia-drivers with GLX backend for some users"
(https://man.archlinux.org/man/picom.1[picom(1)]); NVIDIA is the only driver
known to have the extension
(https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/compositor-sync-fences-and-redraw-lag-problems/67099[NVIDIA
forums]). xgb 1.3.1 has no package for SYNC — none in its module — and 021
sets up Composite, DAMAGE, MIT-SHM, RANDR, RENDER and XFIXES.

== Found on the way: resource IDs

The Xorg of ws1 gives every client a mask of 0x1fffff (`Xorg.0.log`: "Max
clients allowed: 256, resource mask: 0x1fffff"): 2 097 151 resource IDs a
connection. xgb hands them out in order and reuses none without
`SetIDRangeFunc` ("How xgb sends a request"); `grep` finds no
`SetIDRangeFunc` and no `xcmisc` in qws. Once they are used up, `NewId`
fails, and with it every request that makes a resource on that connection.

The IDs the snapshotter's connection takes: one a naming of a pixmap
(`namePixmap`), one a DAMAGE object of a window followed; a snapshot by
RENDER of a window's own pixmap (018) 3 + 2k, of a window from its frame
(022) 5 + 2k — 9 at E1's size; a live pass from the frame (020) 3 + 2k. A
window of E1's size changing all the time, taken from its frame once a
`snapshot_interval` of 1 s while the switcher is hidden, takes 9 a second:
the 2 097 151 last some 2.7 days. ilovlya's 133 snapshots in some 4 minutes
of 020's trial at rest, 0.55 a second, would last some 4.9 days. The
passes add 7 a pass at k = 2 while the switcher is shown. Not seen; after
it, no pixmap is named and no snapshot is taken by RENDER on that
connection. xgb has `xcmisc` with `GetXIDRange` for `SetIDRangeFunc`; XC-MISC
is not among the extensions of 021. Not a part of this change: D1 b takes the
passes out of it, the snapshots stay.
