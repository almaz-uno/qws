== The first switch of the trial of 028

The author's trial of 028 on ws2, 2026-10-05 12:13 — 2560×1440 at 165 Hz,
a period of 6.07 ms, an RTX 5060 Ti with the driver 580.105.08, 39
windows, the live thumbnails on at an interval of 50 ms; the build of
`98594a8` on Alt+F11, the service stopped (`log-ws2.txt`, a copy in
`~/.cache/qws-trial-028/` on ws1). The whole trial is one activation, the
first of a fresh process (`cold=true`). Its five switches, each with the
layers of the layout switched to held, from the record "Frame" of cause
`key` after each "Switching layout", times from the start of the
activation:

[cols="1,1,1,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| At, ms | To | `draw_ms` | `present_ms` | `total_ms` | Before it
| 1782.0 | the grid | 8.22 | 29.33 | 37.79 | the fade-in, the carousel at rest
| 4176.8 | the carousel | 0.05 | 0.25 | 0.46 | the convergence, the grid at rest
| 8627.9 | the grid | 0.38 | 0.37 | 0.86 | steps of the carousel
| 13321.2 | the carousel | 0.03 | 0.27 | 0.37 | the convergence, steps of the grid
| 16478.7 | the grid | 1.42 | 0.79 | 2.43 | steps of the carousel
|===

What is first at the first switch, and not at the others:

* *The first scene of the process.* No step, hover or switch came
  before it: the carousel was shown by frames of the CPU — the fade-in,
  then the frame at rest again for the live pictures. It is the first
  `PresentScene` of the process, the first draw of the scene program.
* *The first sampling of the grid's layers.* Its base, of 2520×1400, and
  its tiles were uploaded in the pauses of the fade-in, 1.1 s before
  (`uploaded_kb` 13781 and 12332 at two of its frames), and never drawn —
  the issue's suspicion: the driver places a texture at its first use, and
  the first frame that samples it pays.
* *A live pass beside it.* The record after its frame:
  `Live done_ms=41.36 ms=40.19 since_change_ms=1588.83 window=130023431`
  — a pass of 40 ms of the snapshotter's thread, of a window of 2556×1392
  taken from its frame by RENDER (022), changed 1.6 s before. Logged
  between the frames of 1782.56 and 1788.79 ms, it began between 1741 and
  1748 ms, and the key was read at 1744.2 ms: the pass began with the
  switch and ended 1–6 ms after its frame. The window was not in view in
  the carousel — passed there, it would have been passed from 753 ms, the
  end of the fade-in — and came into view with the grid: `BeginFrame` of
  the switch's frame, with the new list of the windows shown, wakes the
  snapshotter (020), and the frames do not come one after another then, so
  the pass is made at once. It is the window's first pass of the process:
  its pass makes and binds the pixmap it is scaled into (023), kept from
  then on for the window.

Against the suspicion, the log itself: the carousel's layers — its base,
of 2520×1400 too, and its cards at the selection — were uploaded in the
background as well, and first sampled at the switch back to the carousel
at 4176.8 ms, the grid shown at rest until then: presented in 0.25 ms. Another window taken from its frame,
`23068676`, had its first pass when the convergence ended:
`Live done_ms=40.82 ms=39.12` — 39 ms again, with no frame beside it.

The `draw_ms` of 8.22 ms, the items of the scene and the pictures taken
for it, is not split further; of it, the geometry of the live tiles is
made at the first frame of the grid of an activation, `GridLive` —
0.47–2.91 ms on ws1 for the 33 windows of E1, the first call the longest.

The first scene of a process. An earlier form of the benchmark, without

08:47: the first switch of the first activation 0.79, 0.73 and 1.56 ms

suspicion of the issue is refuted for the layers, and a draw of each
layer after its upload would pay for nothing measured.

== The first live pass of a window taken from its frame

A window drawn into its frame's pixmap — of depth 24 in a frame of depth
24: xterm, Dion, Telegram, KeePassXC on ws1 (022) — takes live passes by
RENDER into a pixmap of qws's own of its thumbnail's size, bound on the
GPU as a texture (`scaledFor`, `pkg/snapshot/live.go`, 023). The pixmap
and its binding are made at the window's first pass and kept while the
window keeps its size, across activations; the RENDER chain of the pass
is made at its first pass of each activation. A window with a pixmap of
its own is bound at its snapshots (008) — on change, or at the `Refresh`
of an activation when its pixmap is new — and only bound again by its
passes.

=== The steps of the first pass

Probes off the screen, not kept: the snapshotter of the tests of 022
(`frameSnapshotter`) on the thread of the test, a GLX presenter sharing
its context on a thread of its own presenting frames one after another —
the last frame again and `glFinish`, a millisecond apart — from 5 ms
before a step of the pass to 60 ms after; per window, a new client of
2556×1392 in a frame off the screen, captured once by RENDER; six windows
a run. 2026-10-08 08:49–08:56, load average 2.5–3.2:

[cols="2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| What runs beside the frames | Its time, ms | Longest frame beside it, ms
| nothing | — | 0.24–0.36
| the first pass | 8.9–34.4 | 4.9–15.8
| the X pixmap of 32 bits made (`CreatePixmap`) | 0.11–0.34 | 0.20–0.47
| its binding on the GPU (`BindPixmap`) | 1.2–25.9 | 0.3–15.2; once 76.9
| the first pass, its pixmap made and bound before | 1.0–2.1 | 0.5–4.0
|===

The pass holds the frames by the binding of its new pixmap — what
`glXCreatePixmap` and `glXBindTexImageEXT` do in the driver and the X
server — and not by its other work: a pass whose pixmap is already bound
costs 1–2 ms and the frames beside it little.

=== The frames beside the first pass (T1)

`BenchmarkFirstPassBeside` in `pkg/snapshot`, committed after this
document, before any change of the code: the same presenter and frames;
one window of 2556×1392 in a frame off the screen, whose rounds forget
what its passes made — its pixmap, its chain, its live textures, as for a
window new to the process — then capture it as a snapshot on change, with
no frame presented, and make its first pass beside the frames. Per round:
the snapshot, the pass on the snapshotter's thread, the longest frame of
the 65 ms beside it and of as long a stretch with no pass just before.

The binding's cost varies with the state of the host: in some stretches
2–8 ms with the frames beside it as alone, in others 4–34 ms with the
frames held up to 16 ms — beyond two refresh periods of E1. What makes the
difference is not established: the desktop and other agents' sessions ran
meanwhile, 24 of the 32 GiB of swap were in use at 09:09, and a load of
RENDER in the X server beside the benchmark (`BenchmarkFrameRender`, 3000
captures) did not slow it in a quick stretch. On ws2 the trial's two first
passes took 39 and 40 ms.

The first switch of the trial is so explained in kind: a binding beside
its frame, the frame held until it was done. Not reproduced at the level
of the switcher off the screen: a real snapshotter in `pkg/ui` would
capture the author's windows and bind them beside the service's.

== Where the binding can go

The snapshots on change are paused from before `Show` to after `FadeOut`
(`cmd/qws/root.go`, 007 and 011) and taken in the snapshotter's loop
while no live session runs (020): a snapshot on change never runs beside
a frame of the switcher. `Refresh`, the snapshot of the changed windows
at an activation, runs before its first frame and may still run beside it
— it waits for them 20 ms at most. Made at a snapshot on change, the
pixmap of a window's passes and its binding cost the snapshotter's thread
the binding — 1–31 ms (above) — at the window's first snapshot and after
a change of its size, while nothing of qws is shown.

The pixmap is 4 bytes a pixel of the thumbnail's size, at most 512×512 —
1 MB — in the X server; for a window shown in an activation it is made
now anyway, at its first pass. On ws1 4 of the 35 client windows are
taken from their frames (022).
