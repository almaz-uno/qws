== What and why

Since 028, while one layout is shown the other's layers are drawn and
uploaded in the background, and a switch of the layout is shown from them
at once: in the author's trial on ws2 (2026-10-05), 0.37–2.43 ms from the
key to the first frame — but the first switch, to the grid, took 37.8 ms:
its scene drawn in 8.2 ms, presented in 29.3 ms, nothing uploaded (028,
Results, K6). Its open decision guessed the cause: the driver placing a
texture at its first use, so that the first frame sampling the grid's
layers pays for it; the issue (#99) asks for the first switch as quick as
the others, the textures used once off the frame — warmed up — should that
be it.

What the research found:

* *Not the textures.* Off the screen on ws1 the first switch of an
  activation, the grid's layers uploaded and never drawn, takes what the
  later switches take: 1.6–2.0 ms from the key at p50, 2.3–3.1 ms at p95,
  presenting the same (T1). In the trial itself, the carousel's layers of
  the same sizes, uploaded alike, were first sampled at the switch back
  and presented in 0.25 ms. A draw of each layer after its upload would
  pay for nothing measured.
* *A live pass beside the frame.* The trial was the first activation of a
  fresh process, and its first switch brought into view a window taken
  from its frame (022) that had changed while out of view. The new list of
  the windows shown, given by the switch's frame, woke the snapshotter,
  which made the window's pass at once — beside the frame — and its first
  pass of the process makes and binds on the GPU the pixmap the window is
  scaled into (023): 40 ms of the snapshotter's thread, from the key to
  1–6 ms after the frame.
* *The binding holds the frames.* Off the screen on ws1, frames presented
  beside such a first pass are held by the binding of its new pixmap —
  not by the rest of the pass: up to 16 ms at p95, beyond two refresh
  periods, in some stretches; as alone in others, the binding's cost
  varying with the state of the host from 1 to 34 ms (research).

The change: the binding is not made beside the switcher's frames. The
pixmap a window taken from its frame is scaled into for its live passes is
made and bound at the window's snapshot, while no switcher is shown —
warmed up before the activation — so that its first pass in an activation
binds nothing. The slug keeps the issue's name: the warm-up is of that
pixmap's texture, not of the layers'.

The specification is a delta to `develop` at `1a6dbc8` (1.6.0). Unchanged:
every frame of the switcher — `pkg/ui` and `pkg/carousel` are not touched;
the pictures of the snapshots and of the live passes; which windows are
passed and when (020's schedule); the pixmap itself, its size and its
binding, but for when they are made; the configuration — no key, variable
or flag changes.

== Decisions

None taken by the author yet. Each lists its options and the one
recommended — for the author to confirm.

D1. *Where the pixmap of the passes is made.* Recommended: at a snapshot
on change of a window taken from its frame, when it has none of its
thumbnail's size — its first snapshot, or after its size changed. The
snapshots on change are taken only while no switcher is shown (007, 011,
020): no frame of qws is presented meanwhile. Not at the snapshot of an
activation (`Refresh`), which may run beside its first frame. The pixmap
and its binding cost the snapshotter's thread 1–31 ms (research) once per
window and size, as they do now at the first pass; the pixmap, at most
1 MB in the X server, is kept as now from the first pass. A failure to
make it leaves it to the first pass, as now; a binding that fails turns
the passes from frames off for the session, said once, as at a pass.
What stays: a window first captured by an activation's `Refresh` — new
within the settle of a snapshot before it — or whose size changed while
the switcher is shown gets its pixmap at its first pass, as now.

Alternatives:
(b) the passes of the windows that come into view with a switch held off
its frame — the snapshotter woken after the frame, not by it, and the
passes waiting for the frames to stop: only the switch's first frame
spared; the binding then lands in the pause after the convergence, or
under a key pressed meanwhile;
(c) the issue's: each layer texture used once off the frame after its
upload, by a draw into a small scratch framebuffer: rejected — no cost of
a layer's first use was measured (research), and the draws would cost the
pauses of the animations for nothing.

D2. *Only with the live thumbnails.* Recommended: the snapshotter makes the
pixmaps only when told, before its first snapshots, that the live
thumbnails will be asked for: qws tells it at start when
`appearance.thumbnail.live` is on and the renderer is `glx` — the
conditions of 020 that the configuration decides. Should the presenter
then not share the snapshotter's context, the live thumbnails are off
(020) and the pixmaps are made for nothing: 4 of the 35 client windows of
ws1 are taken from their frames. Alternative: always, whatever the
configuration — a pixmap and a binding per such window for nothing with
the live thumbnails off.

== Behavior

None the user sees but the time of the first switch: with the live
thumbnails on, the first live pass of a window taken from its frame, in
an activation, binds no new pixmap beside the frames, and a switch that
brings it into view presents its first frame within a refresh period, as
the later switches do. The record "Pixmap of the live passes made", at
debug level, with its window, size and time, follows the record
"Snapshot" that made it.

== Metrics

L:: time from the key of a switch to the presenting of its first frame:
`total_ms` of the record "Frame" of cause `key` after "Switching layout";
of the first switch of an activation, and of the later ones.
H:: off the screen, the longest frame of the presenter beside the first
live pass of a window taken from its frame, as for a window new to the
process, and the pass on the snapshotter's thread
(`BenchmarkFirstPassBeside`).
B:: where the binding goes: the snapshot that makes the pixmap, its time.

== Open decisions

. D1 and D2, for the author.
. K7, the author's trial.
. The first scene of a process: some 0.4–1.2 ms dearer off the screen on
  ws1 (research); on the screen not told apart from what ran beside it.
  Should K7 show the first switch still slow with no first pass beside it,
  the next suspect.
. The windows the change leaves to their first pass — new within a
  settle of an activation, or resized while the switcher is shown — and
  the windows with a pixmap of their own bound at an activation's
  `Refresh`, beside its first frame: not measured.
. What makes the binding cost 1 ms in some stretches and 30 ms in others
  is not established (research).
