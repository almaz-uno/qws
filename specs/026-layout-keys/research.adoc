== The keys of the switcher

* `handleEventsSync` (`pkg/ui/selector.go`) takes Enter itself, before any
  other key; every other press goes to `handleKeyPressSimple`, which looks
  at the keys in this order: the workspace modifier, the cancel key, `c`,
  `g`, Ctrl+`c` — the cancel without the configuration — the main key with
  or without the backward modifier, Right, Left. Anything else is ignored:
  Up and Down, and `q`.
* `c` and `g` are keysyms 0x63 and 0x67 fixed in the code. They count only
  without the workspace modifier — `state & workspaceModifierMask` — and
  with any other: Shift+`g` shows the grid. A layout already shown is not
  drawn again; another sets `LayoutMode`, drops the layers (`dropLayers`),
  draws the frame (`render`) and asks for the layers of the next steps
  (`prefetch`).
* `restoreInitialLayoutMode` sets `LayoutMode` back to `appearance.layout`
  on Enter, the cancel key, the release of the primary modifier and a click;
  `Show` drops the layers at the start of every activation.
* A key is matched by its keycode: `keysymToKeycode` looks the keysym up in
  the keyboard mapping, which the selector reads once an activation and
  keeps in `keymap`, as the modifier mapping in `modmap` (007). The range of
  keycodes comes from `xproto.Setup`, which only decodes `Conn.SetupBytes`.
  A test can thus hand presses to `handleKeyPressSimple` on an `xgb.Conn`
  holding no more than `SetupBytes`, its mappings set in advance, with no X
  server.
* `keygrab.ParseKey` trims the name, lowers its case and looks it up in
  `keysyms.yaml`: `q` is 0x71, `Up` 0xFF52, `Down` 0xFF54, `F2` 0xFFBF,
  `space` 0x20. A bad cancel key is reported — "Failed to parse cancel key,
  using Escape", the name in the field `cancel` — and replaced by a default.

== The steps

* `stepTo` (`pkg/ui/animation.go`) takes a step to a window more than one
  away for a wrap — `target − selected > 1` or `selected − target > 1` — and
  makes it at once, the carousel scrolling past every card otherwise (007).
  In the grid a step moves the selection frame in pixels, from the tile of
  the selection to that of the target (`gx`, `gy`); the step between two
  tiles a row apart is `cols` windows away, two at least, and would be at
  once by that test. Hence D7 of the specification: the callers say what
  is a wrap.
* Right from the end of a row to the start of the next is one window away
  and slides across the grid; from the last tile to the first is a wrap.

== The grid

* `layoutGrid` (`pkg/carousel/renderer-cpu.go`) lays n tiles out row after
  row in `appearance.grid.columns` columns, or, at 0,
  ⌈√(1.5 n)⌉ within 2 to 6: 2 columns for 1 or 2 tiles, 3 for 3 to 6, 4 for
  7 to 10, 5 for 11 to 16, 6 from 17. The last row is filled from the left.
  The tiles lie below `top`, the band of the header (005).
* `getWindowIndexAtPositionGrid` (`pkg/ui/selector.go`), the tile under the
  pointer, repeats the formula of the columns and lays the tiles over the
  whole window: its available height and its vertical offset leave out
  `top`. Where the tiles are of their largest size the two grids have tiles
  of one size, and the one of the pointer lies higher by half the band;
  elsewhere the tiles of the pointer are taller as well. Not changed here
  (spec, open decisions).

== The header

`drawHeader` (005, 014): with a hostname or a version, the hostname at 2.5
times `appearance.font.size` in the text colour at 0.9 of its alpha, 24 px
from the left and top edges, its baseline 24 px plus the ascent of its face
from the top; the version after it, 16 px on, on the same baseline, at the
font size, at 0.6. The band is 24 px, the height of the large face and
16 px; the grid lays its tiles out below it. gg's `MeasureString` gives
whole pixels — the advance in 26.6 fixed point shifted right by 6 — so text
placed at the margin from the right edge by its measure starts at a whole
pixel, as the version does, and the picture of the same text drawn there is
the version's moved by a whole number of pixels: what `TestLayoutHint`
compares.

The overlay is 2520 px wide on E1, a monitor of 2560 px less the default
padding of 20 px on each side. With the font of the tests — the Go font of
`golang.org/x/image`, which `TestScenes` draws with — at the size 20 of E1,
`Q — carousel` beside the hostname `ws1` and the version `v0.1.0` begins
16 px after the version on an overlay of 348 px, as `TestLayoutHint` logs
it: 24 + `ws1` + 16 + `v0.1.0` + 16 + the hint + 24. The figure for the fonts
of E1 — Noto Sans, and a version of `git describe` — was not measured; it
differs from this one by the widths of the glyphs and of the version.

== The configuration

* Viper treats an empty environment variable as unset (`clearEnvironment`
  of `internal/config` relies on it): `QWS_KEYBINDINGS_LAYOUT_TOGGLE=` keeps
  the value of the file. An empty value in the file, `layout_toggle: ""`, is
  read as empty, and so is an empty flag.
* `config init` writes `yaml.Marshal(config.Default())`, `config show`
  `yaml.Marshal` of the configuration read; yaml.v3 indents by 4 spaces, the
  key under `keybindings:` as `    layout_toggle: q`.
* `TestJoinedKeys` (015) derives the joined names from the fields: the new
  field gets its joined name, `keybindings.layouttoggle`, with no change to
  the test.

== The live thumbnails

The selector takes the windows in view for the snapshotter (`liveShown`)
and the live items of the frame at rest (`liveGeometry`) under keys made of
the generation of the layers and the layout; a switch of layout drops the
layers, which moves the generation, so both are computed anew for the new
layout at its first frame. Nothing of 020 depends on how the layout was
switched; `TestLiveFrames` switches it by setting `LayoutMode`.
