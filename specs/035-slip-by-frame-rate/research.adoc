=== Finding: the layers of the cards uploaded stall the frames

Not changed here, a candidate for a later specification. An upload of
1.2–1.3 MB of the layers of the cards in the pause before a frame costs
that frame 10–12 ms of presenting on ws1 at 144 Hz — two slots lost, a
real stall, not the jitter of the timer — in 2 of the 10 frames counted
with as much uploaded, and 6.6 ms in a third. The uploads of a pause stop
at 1 MB (`pauseUpload` of `waitFrame`), but a layer taken below it goes
whole. 031 found
the uploads not making the frames late on ws2 at 165 Hz ("The count
replayed": 1 of 416 such frames late in B); on ws1 they do.

== The rules replayed

The count over the records of the batches in their order, as
`TestSlipReplay` replays them: the intervals of the records of the kinds
counted — the steps, the hover, the convergence; not the fades —, the
first frame of an animation, without an interval, not among them; the
window carried across the activations; no probe — once still, the replay
ends. The rules:

* *3 late of 60* — 031, D5: an interval above 1.25 periods late, still at
  the third of the last 60 frames counted.
* *W s, S %* — the window of D2: the latest frames counted whose
  intervals sum to W or more, the fewest such; full once they reach W;
  still when it is full and its mean rate — its frames over the sum of
  their intervals — is below S % of the refresh rate.
* *W s, S %, two* — the same, and the window before it, of the frames
  just older, below S % as well: two windows in a row.

The batches: A and B of 031 (ws2, 165 Hz; A with a VNC viewer), the
frames of ws1 above — as recorded, and after a window of on-time frames,
since as recorded they span 0.33 s and fill no window —, B with every
interval and the period scaled to 60 Hz. Synthetic: a load — every
interval 30 or 50 ms — from an empty window and after a full window of
on-time frames; every other slot lost — every interval two periods —;
every third — one period and two, in turn —; a pause of 100–300 ms,
followed by a frame at once (0.4 ms) as the schedule starts anew, among
on-time frames; the stalls of activation 10 of ws1 after every four
on-time frames, over 3 s. A cell is the time of animation counted until
it goes still — for the load after B, since the load began; for a pause,
since the pause began —, or "never" with the lowest share of the refresh

* 66 % keeps a display that loses every third slot animated — 66.7 % of

The rounding of the test data to the microsecond moves the sum of a
window by half a microsecond a frame at most: no share above by less than
0.01 %.

== The choice (O1)

0.5 s and 75 %, one window, as D2 and D3 first said. Of the candidates it
is the one that makes a load still within 0.5 s of animation from an
empty window and keeps every recorded frame animated; it loses to the
others on a single pause: one of 150 ms makes it still — 0.15 s in the
table, at 144 and at 60 Hz —, one of 100 ms does not, the edge some
132–142 ms. A frozen frame of 150 ms is 21 slots lost at 144 Hz, 9 at
60 Hz; still for five minutes after it is the price of seeing a load
within the window. 1 s would keep pauses up to 257 ms but take 1 s of
animation — seven steps of 150 ms — from an empty window; two windows in
a row would keep any pause and take up to 1.1 s; 66 % would keep every
third slot lost.

The frames the window holds are bounded, 512: that many within 0.5 s are
above 1000 fps, never below 75 % of a refresh rate of 1365 Hz or less; a
window that holds 512 frames and spans less than 0.5 s is not full.

== The frames as test data

`pkg/ui/testdata/fast-ws1.txt` holds of the log of ws1 what the count
needs, in the form of `vnc-a.txt` and `vnc-b.txt` (031, "The batches as
test data"): of each record "Animation frame" with an interval while
animated — none between "Animation still" and "Animation back" —, its
activation, its kind and its interval in microseconds, a line a run of
records of one kind in one activation, in their order, and the period in
a comment. Made on ws1 in the clone of the branch:

[source,sh]
----
{
  printf '# The frames of the trial of specs/035-slip-by-frame-rate, research: ws1,\n'
  printf '# 2560x1440 at 144 Hz, 2026-10-14; the records "Animation frame" with an\n'
  printf '# interval while animated, in their order. A line is a run of records of\n'
  printf '# one kind in one activation: the activation, the kind, the intervals in\n'
  printf '# microseconds.\n'
  printf '# period_us 6944.525\n'
  sed 's/\x1b\[[0-9;]*m//g' research/ws1-2026-10-14.txt |
  awk '/Animation still/ { still = 1; next }
    /Animation back/ { still = 0; next }
    /Animation frame/ && !still {
      a = k = i = ""
      for (n = 1; n <= NF; n++) {
        split($n, f, "=")
        if (f[1] == "activation") a = f[2]
        else if (f[1] == "kind") k = f[2]
        else if (f[1] == "interval_ms") i = f[2]
      }
      if (i == "") next
      us = sprintf("%.0f", i * 1000)
      if (a == pa && k == pk) { line = line " " us; next }
      if (line != "") print line
      line = a " " k " " us; pa = a; pk = k
    }
    END { if (line != "") print line }'
} > pkg/ui/testdata/fast-ws1.txt
----

The table above is printed by `replay.py`, next to this document, from
the root of the repository: `python3
specs/035-slip-by-frame-rate/replay.py pkg/ui/testdata/vnc-a.txt
pkg/ui/testdata/vnc-b.txt pkg/ui/testdata/fast-ws1.txt`.
