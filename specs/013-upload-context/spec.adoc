== What and why

On `glx` the animation composes layers on the GPU (specification 007). The
card layers, and the frame at rest in pieces of 64 rows, go to the GPU in
the pauses between frames, at most a megabyte a pause and no later than 3 ms
before the frame is due — in the GL context that presents the frames.
Session S2 met K2 of 007 at p95, 7.09–7.10 ms against a period of 6.94 ms,
but not in full: 0.45–0.51 % of the intervals of the carousel missed a
vertical blank. Of the 283 missed in three runs, 211 came after a pause that
uploaded: 1.37 % of such pauses against 0.13 % of the others, and a piece of
630 KB cost most of it already — 0.96 %, against 1.53 % for more (research).
Open decision 3 of 007: the uploads from a GL context of their own.

The change: the uploads of `SetLayer` and `StageFrame` are made in a second
GL context that shares the textures of the presenter's, current on a pbuffer
on a thread of its own; fences order the commands of the two contexts. Where
no such context can be made, the presenter uploads in its own, as in 1.2.0.

The specification is a delta to `develop` at `f90abc7` (1.2.0). Unchanged:
the frames at rest of both renderers, byte for byte, and the scene set Σ with
its digests; the scenes of the animation — a frame shows every layer set
before it; the loop of the animation — its pauses, their megabyte, the spin
of the last 3 ms; the configuration; `cpu`.

== Decisions

Asked, 2026-10-01: the uploads of the animation in a GL context of their own,
on a thread of their own, sharing the textures of the presenter's; the
staging of the frame at rest there too; each upload followed by a fence that
the presenter checks without blocking before a texture is first drawn; a
fallback to the uploads of today where no shared context can be made; the
rest of the loop kept as is.

Made on the way, 2026-10-01, for the author to confirm:

* *A display of its own.* The context of the uploads is created on an Xlib
  display of its own, as the context of the snapshots (008), not on the
  presenter's: NVIDIA shares the objects of direct contexts of one process
  across displays (research), and no display is then used by two threads.
  Its X errors are counted, not fatal, by a handler that passes the errors of
  other displays on, as that of 008.
* *A frame shows every layer set before it.* The presenter checks the fence
  of an upload without blocking when a frame first draws its texture; when
  it has not signalled, the frame waits for it on the GPU — `glWaitSync` in
  the presenter's context — rather than drawing the layer as missing. Left
  out, a card would vanish for a frame, or the grid lose its tiles, and the
  loop's idea of a layer held — set, not uploaded — would have to change with
  it. The CPU of the loop waits only for the uploader to issue the job, which
  takes it some 0.3 ms for a layer. How often a frame waits is logged.
* *The staging of the frame at rest goes to the uploader as a whole*: the
  copy of the texture of the last frame that a new staging starts with, and
  the pieces. The two textures pass between the contexts, so their writes
  and reads are ordered by fences both ways (section "Behavior").
* *The loop is kept*: the megabyte a pause and the 3 ms before a frame stay
  the limits of 007, so that S2 compares the context alone. Whether they can
  go is open.
* *Through a pixel buffer.* In the uploader's context a layer goes through
  its pixel buffer, as the pieces of the frame at rest do, not from the
  memory of the process as in 1.2.0: uploads from memory in a second context
  held the frames back by a millisecond at the median, through a pixel
  buffer by a fifth of that (research, "The probe"). In the presenter's
  context, the fallback, the layers go as in 1.2.0.

== Behavior

All of it on `glx`, where the animation runs; `cpu` presents as before.

. *The uploader.* When the presenter creates its GL objects, at the first
  `Bind`, it starts a thread with a direct GLX 4.6 core context created with
  the presenter's as its share context, current on a pbuffer of 1×1. The log
  says, at debug level, `Uploads in a context of their own`, or `Uploads in
  the context of the presenter` with the reason; then the presenter uploads
  in its own, as in 1.2.0.
. *Jobs.* `SetLayer` and `StageFrame` queue a job and return. The uploader
  runs the jobs in order, each followed by a fence and a flush; the layers
  and the pieces go through its pixel buffer. `StageFrame` counts the bytes
  it queued, as it counted those it uploaded, and reports the frame staged in
  full when its last piece is queued.
. *Order.*
.. A frame that draws a layer, or presents the frame staged, first takes the
   fence of its job: signalled, it draws; not, it waits for it on the GPU.
.. A job that writes a texture the presenter has drawn — the staging
   texture, which was the texture of a frame before the two were swapped, or
   a layer set anew — waits first, on the GPU, for a fence of the frames
   presented so far.
.. A frame presented whole while a frame at rest is staged writes the
   texture of the last frame only once the copy of the staging has read it.
.. Textures are deleted — `DropLayers` — or made anew for a window of
   another size only after the jobs queued so far have completed; the CPU
   waits for that, at an activation or a change of the layout, not during
   frames.

== Metrics

As in 007, with a field more in the frame records of animations:
`upload_waits`, the uploads not yet complete when the frame drew what they
upload — it waited for them.

M1 — misses by pause:: The intervals of the carousel above 1.5 periods, per
pause before the frame, by what the pause uploaded — `uploaded_kb` 0, up to
640 KB, more — frames at rest and stalls over 50 ms apart, as in the research
of 007. `measure.sh summary`, line `A1 carousel by pause`.

M2 — a frame on the GPU, off the screen:: `BenchmarkPacedFrames`: frames of
a scene of 23 card layers on a base of 2520×1400 presented to a window off
the screen every 1/144 s, layers set and pieces of a frame at rest staged in
every second pause, in blocks of 400 frames that take turns — no uploads,
the uploads in the presenter's context, in the uploader's. Per mode, by timer
queries, at p95: how long the first command of a frame waits on the GPU after
it is issued, "queued", and how long the frame then takes there, to the end
of its swap; and the share of frames presented, to the end of `glFinish`, in
more than 2 ms, and of frames that waited for an upload.

== Open decisions

. The limits of the pauses: with the uploads in a context of their own, the
  megabyte a pause and the end 3 ms before a frame may be needless — to be
  seen after K4.
. Should frames wait on the GPU for uploads often — `upload_waits` in S2 —
  a frame could draw a layer not yet uploaded as missing, or its neighbour
  in place of it, rather than wait.
. The grid, whose tiles layer and frame at rest are 14 MB each: measured by
  S2 with `LAYOUT=grid` along with the carousel, no criterion of its own.
. NVIDIA moves the uploads of the second context mostly, not wholly, beside
  the frames: a frame that comes while one is under way starts some
  hundreds of microseconds late (research). Smaller jobs — a layer in
  pieces, as the frame at rest — would bound that, should S2 call for it.
