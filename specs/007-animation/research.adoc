== The cost of a frame in motion

A benchmark of `Draw3DCarouselWithData` on the E1 scene of Σ, 40 frames
each, 2026-10-01:

[cols="3,1",options="header"]
|===
| Frame | ms
| static, offset 0 | 37.1
| a fractional offset, the same every frame | 33.4
| a new fractional offset every frame, as an animation gives | 40.1
|===

With presenting, about 8.5 ms on `glx` (specification 001, T6), a frame in
motion drawn by the CPU takes some 42–49 ms: about 20–25 frames a second, 3
or 4 frames in a step of 150 ms. The third row is dearer because every new
offset gives the titles a new font size, and `NewMultiFallbackFace` makes
and caches a face for each size — without bound in an animation.

Much of a card depends on its offset, not only its place: the scale, the
alpha, the font size of the title and the workspace, the paddings and radii
of their pills, and the truncation of the title, `max(int(30/scale), 10)`
runes (`renderer-cpu.go:539`). A card drawn at one offset and scaled to
another is therefore not the card of that offset; drawn at the two integer
offsets around a fractional one and cross-faded, it ends as the card of the
target offset.

== Pacing

An experiment on a copy of the code, 2026-10-01: an off-screen override-
redirect window with the GLX context of `pkg/glx`, 300 swaps each followed
by `glFinish`.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Swap interval | Interval between swaps
| 1 | p50 999.8 ms — the driver throttles a window that is not on screen to
  one swap a second
| 0 | p50 0.14 ms — no pacing at all
|===

The overlay is on screen, where an interval of 1 may well follow vertical
blank; the tests of specification 001 draw off screen, and a compositor adds
its own frame anyway. Pacing by a timer at the refresh period does not
depend on either.

The same on the overlay itself, 2026-10-01, with the step animated as in T5:
an interval of 1 throttles it to a swap a second as well, under picom
`--vsync` on NVIDIA. The timer it is.

== The keyboard

`xset q` on ws1: autorepeat after 500 ms, 33 repeats a second — a held step
key sets a new target every 30 ms, less than the 150 ms of a step.

== What exists

`Draw3DCarouselWithData` takes `animOffset`, and the geometry of a card is
continuous in it (`carouselCard`); `Selector.animateTransition` sets it to 0
and draws once — the animation was planned and never done.
`handleEventsSync` blocks on `WaitForEvent`; an animation needs frames while
it waits.

== Frames on time

The step as in T5, on E1, 2026-10-01; each row one or more tries of one
activation — five steps 0.4 s apart, then twelve 30 ms apart, as a held key
— about 200 frame intervals each. "Late" is a frame that starts more than a
millisecond after it is due; a missed frame is an interval above 1.5
periods, 10.4 ms.

[cols="4,2,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| Change | A1 p50 / p95 / max, ms | Late frames | Tries

| A sleep to the time a frame is due
| 6.69 / 9.34 / 11.03 | many | 1

| A sleep to 1.5 ms before it, then a spin
| 6.94 / 8.17 / 11.85 | many | 1

| Layers uploaded in the pauses between frames, not in them
| 6.94 / 7.12 / 14.96 | 3 | 1

| One frame at rest drawn at a time
| 6.94 / 7.02 / 10.40 | 2 | 1

| Layers drawn on at most half the CPUs; the spin does not yield
| 6.94 / 7.00–7.03 / 9.81–16.07 | 1 a try | 2

| Copies of whole images a row at a time, through a call
| 6.94 / 7.01–7.04 / 7.10–14.90 | 0–1 a try | 4

| The last 3 ms spun, not slept; the keyboard mapping read once an
  activation; no snapshots while the switcher is shown
| 6.94 / 7.00–7.08 / 7.07–7.66 | 0 | 6
|===

What each row found:

. *Timer jitter.* A sleep wakes up to half a millisecond late; a spin over
  its last 1.5 ms puts the median on the period, 6.94 ms.
. *Uploads.* The frames that uploaded a layer drew in 0.5–0.8 ms and
  presented in 1.1–1.8 ms, against 0.02 and 0.2 ms without, and came after
  their vertical blank. An upload itself is short — 134 uploads of up to
  1.4 MB, p50 0.26 ms, max 1.6 ms. Made while the loop waits for a frame,
  no later than 3 ms before it is due, and while it waits for an event
  when no step moves, they leave the frames alone. The latter also took the
  response of the first step of an activation from 30.1 ms to 3–7 ms: the
  base and the prefetched layers are on the GPU before the first key.
. *Frames at rest.* One drawn per key of a held step ran a dozen at once, at
  50–77 ms of a CPU and 14 MB each; the heap of the process peaked at
  700–930 MB and was collected every 50–100 ms. One at a time, and after it
  only the latest target, still reaches the end of a step in time: a
  drawing takes less than half of 150 ms.
. *The collector.* With `GOGC=off` no frame was late in three tries — the
  collector it was, though its pauses reported by `gctrace` were long, not
  its work. An execution trace showed a stop-the-world of 5.7 ms waiting
  for one goroutine: the drawing of a frame at rest, in `newCanvas`, which
  copies the cached background of 14 MB into a fresh image. The copy is one
  `memmove`, and a `memmove` cannot be preempted; the fresh pages it
  faults in make it long, and the signal of asynchronous preemption,
  delivered on the return from a fault, lands inside it. Copied a row at a
  time, the pause was still up to 13 ms: the rows are `memmove` too. Copied
  a row at a time through a function that is not inlined — its prologue
  checks for preemption — the pauses are 0.2–0.7 ms. `crop` of the layers
  and the copy of a captured window in `pkg/composite` go the same way.
. *Wake-ups.* Once in 800 frames a sleep of the loop, locked to the thread
  of the GL context, woke 5.8 ms late with the CPUs busy. The loop now
  wakes once a frame, 3 ms before it is due, and spins the rest: 43 % of a
  CPU while a step moves.
. *The X server.* A key press made seven requests, `GetModifierMapping`
  and six `GetKeyboardMapping`, each waiting for a reply; once in 800
  frames they took 14 ms while the server captured a window for a
  thumbnail. Three instances of `qws` captured the active window every
  second on E1 at the time: the one measured and the two services of the
  author (`/usr/local/bin/qws`, v0.1.0, `snapshot_interval: 1s`). The
  mappings are now read once an activation and again on `MappingNotify`,
  and the snapshots of the measured instance pause while it is shown.

In the last row, over 1203 intervals: p95 7.00–7.08 ms, max 7.66 ms — no
frame missed; the longest pause of the collector 0.67 ms. The step response
A2 is 0.3–1.4 ms for a single key and 3.3–6.2 ms for the first step of an
activation. For the keys of a held step it is about 7 ms, a period: the
loop reads a key after the frame it waits for, not while it waits.

These intervals were those of the frames in motion only. The last frame of
a step, the frame at rest, came 10.7–17 ms after the frame before it in
every step of those tries — a vertical blank or two missed at the end of
each step: presented as a frame, it uploaded the rows that changed, up to
14 MB, in 4–10 ms. Staged in the pauses of the step instead, into a second
texture of the window size that starts as a GPU copy of the first
(`glCopyImageSubData`), and presented by swapping the two, it comes on time:
intervals 6.87–7.32 ms, presented in 0.12–0.56 ms, A3 148.6–152.6 ms. The
staging slowed the frames it went with, presented in 1.1–3.4 ms, until the
uploads of a pause — layers first, then pieces of 64 rows — stopped at a
megabyte: the frame after a pause waits on the GPU for them, and E1 took
about a gigabyte a second. Eight tries then: p95 7.00–7.03 ms, max
8.11–9.41 ms.

== The grid

The CPU draws a frame of the grid of E1, 29 tiles, in 350–385 ms, against
55–75 ms for the carousel: `gg.DrawImage` scales each thumbnail through
`x/image/draw` (`transform_RGBA_RGBA_Src`, `transform_RGBA_NRGBA_Over`),
2.5 s of CPU in 7 frames of a try. It does so on `develop` as well — a step
of the grid there takes about 360 ms; specification 001 made the carousel
fast, not the grid. The tiles layer of the grid costs the same.

A step of the grid moves on time — p95 7.02 ms, in three tries — but its
frame at rest comes 208–449 ms after the frame before it. The loop does not
wait for it: the step ends at 150 ms with its scene at the target, and the
frame at rest replaces it when it is drawn, keys handled meanwhile. A
faster grid, drawn to the same bytes, is work of its own, for a
specification of its own.

*The hover.* The author, trying the build on E1 (K9, 2026-10-01): the
carousel is good; on the grid the hover lags behind the mouse. Each change
of the hover drew a frame of the grid in the loop, some 480 ms, while the
motion events queued; each of them then changed the hover again and drew
another frame, so the hover crawled through the places the pointer had
left. It is the same on `develop`, at some 360 ms a frame. The hover is
now a scene of layers too — the hover frame and its shadow, at the tile
under the pointer, over and under the tiles drawn without a hover —
presented at once; the frame of `cpu` is drawn in the background and
replaces the scene when it comes, and a frame at rest drawn for another
hover is not presented.

== Fades

In a try of one activation that ends with a selection, 2026-10-01: the
fade-in 23 intervals, p95 6.97 ms, max 7.21 ms, A3 147.5 ms; the fade-out 21
intervals, p95 7.00 ms, max 9.53 ms, A3 145.9 ms; the chosen window
activated 0.68 ms after the release of the modifier was read (K6). The first
frame of an activation uploads a whole frame, 13 ms on E1, so the fade-in
runs from the end of its presentation: from the end of its drawing, it
lasted 133.6 ms. An `Expose` is not answered during a fade: the frame it
presents, at alpha 1, would flash.

K3 of specification 001 after the fade-in: an absolute error of 0 for
`cpu` and `glx`, three activations each.

== Stalls

Four times in about forty tries a frame of an animation took 260–300 ms to
present, in `SwapBuffers` and `glFinish`. Not the collector: its pauses were
under a millisecond then. Not the X server: the round trips of another
client, measured while the active window was captured every 150 ms, stayed
as without captures, p99 0.2–0.4 ms. Not a change of the performance state
of the GPU: it stayed at P0 around a stall, sampled every 50 ms. Open.

The capture of the active window by `pkg/composite`, 2556×1357, takes
101–121 ms in the client, on E1.


== Two monitors

Since 2026-10-01 E1 has a second monitor, DP-4, 2560×1440 at 59.95 Hz, to
the right of DP-0 at 144 Hz. The period of a step is that of the monitor
the overlay is on, from RandR; the overlay moves between monitors by
recreating its window and binding the GL context anew
(`Monitor changed, recreating selector window`).

A try with the pointer on DP-4: period 16.68 ms; the step p95 16.87 ms,
max 19.37 ms, A3 147.4–150.1 ms; the fade-in 9 intervals, A3 137.6 ms —
within 150 ms and a period, of 16.68 ms. At 60 Hz a step has 9 or 10
frames: the 18 of K2 are for the 144 Hz of E1.

The fades in the carousel runs: A1 p95 7.01–7.10 ms, 0–2 of 420–442
intervals missed each; A3 147.4 ms in, 143.9–144.6 ms out; 23 and 22
frames. The steps: 23 frames each, the frame at rest included. K5: the last
step of a held key at rest 152.7–152.9 ms after its key at p95 — once
474.7 ms, behind a stall — and on the window the keys lead to in all 60
activations.

Of the 283 frames the carousel runs missed, 211 came after a pause that
uploaded — a layer, or pieces of the frame at rest — 55 after one that did
not, 2 were frames at rest, 15 were stalls over 50 ms. The rate per pause:
0.15 % without an upload, 0.96 % with up to 640 KB, 1.55–1.58 % with more.
The upload need not be large: its being in the same GL context as the
frames is what costs. Uploads from a context of their own, on a thread of
their own, could go by the copy engine beside the frames; not tried.

The grid misses more, 3.7 %: its tiles layer is a frame in size, 14 MB in
one upload, and so is the frame at rest it stages. Its K5 is 920 ms at
p50: the frame at rest of the grid takes some 480 ms to draw (see "The
grid"). In this run the harness read the selection before that frame
came, took the frame before for it, and in 14 activations of 20 released
the modifier on a window other than the first — focus moved on E1. It now
waits for the frame of the selection it expects, and presses `Escape` when
none comes.

*Stalls.* Every run, of `develop` as of the branch, of `cpu` as of `glx`,
has presentations of 370–420 ms, one or two a minute: S1 `cpu` of

`cpu` presents through `PutImage`, so the X server it is that stops for
some 400 ms; qws reads RandR with `GetScreenResourcesCurrent`, which does
not probe the outputs. The cause is outside qws, on E1.

*K7.* M2 of `cpu`, S1, p50 / p95: `develop` 68.25 / 85.64 ms, the branch
