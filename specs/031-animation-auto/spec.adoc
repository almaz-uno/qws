== What and why

Over VNC the animation of the switcher stutters. Measured on ws2 with
`X0tigervnc` serving its display (research): with a viewer connected, the
steps of the carousel missed 2.3 % of their frame intervals against 0.1 %
without, in every activation; 9.8 % of the intervals were above 1.25
refresh periods against 0.5 %. The VNC server scrapes the screen about
every 30 ms, and the frames wait for the X server; the viewer then gets a
whole overlay to encode at every frame of a fade.

The author, 2026-10-07: the animation setting on, auto and off; in auto,
the animation off by itself when a VNC viewer is connected, and when the
frames slip — both — with a probe now and then to turn it back on.

The specification is a delta to `develop` at `4af8412` (1.5.1). Unchanged:
the pictures at rest; the animation itself when it runs; `enabled: false`.

== Decisions

D1 decided by the author on 2026-10-07; D2, D3 and D6 by the author the
same day, each as recommended; D4, D5 and D7 made in the specification and
taken with them. D5 amended in the plan the same day — the fade-out not
counted —, open for the author until the trial (O1).

D1 (the author's). *Three settings.* On: the animation always, as
`enabled: true` now. Off: never, as `enabled: false` now. Auto: on, but
still while a VNC viewer is connected or while the frames slip.

D2. *One key.* `appearance.animation.enabled` takes `auto`, `true` and
`false` (`on` and `off` read as `true` and `false`); `auto` by default,
written so by `config init`. A file that says `true` or `false` means what
it meant. The flag `--appearance-animation-enabled` becomes a string,
alone meaning `true` as now; the variable the same. None of the files of
ws1–ws3 sets the key: all three take `auto`. Rejected: a key `mode` beside
`enabled`, two keys of one thing.

D3. *Still as off is.* Still, the switcher changes at once as with
`enabled: false`: every duration 0, the switch of the layout from the
layers (028, D7). And no live thumbnails: each pass presents the overlay
again, a frame more for the VNC server to scrape and send, a load more for
frames that slip.

D4. *A viewer, before the first frame.* At the start of each activation,
before its first frame, qws reads `/proc/net/tcp` and `/proc/net/tcp6`: a
connection established on a local port of
`appearance.animation.vnc_ports`, `[5900]` by default, makes the
activation still from its first frame — no fade-in. The viewer gone, the
next activation is animated again, unless the frames slip (D5). An empty
list checks nothing. Some 0.1 ms; no request to the X server.

D5. *Frames that slip.* Counted over the frames of the steps of the
carousel and of the grid, of the hover, of the convergence (028) and of
the fade-out — not of the fade-in, which misses without a viewer too, its
first frame uploading a whole frame (research). An interval above 1.25
refresh periods is late; 3 late of the last 60 such frames, the count
carried across activations, make it still: what moves ends at once at its
target, the rest of the activation and the next ones still. From the
measurement: with a viewer some 5.9 late of 60, without 0.3 — tripped
within two or three steps, falsely some 0.4 % of the windows.

Amended in the plan (O1): the fade-out is not counted either. The frames
of the research replayed into the count (research, "The count replayed"):
with the fade-out, batch B — no viewer — goes still in its fourth
activation, a frame near the end of the fade-out of the third among its
three late; the fade-out misses without a viewer too, a frame near its end
in 9 of the 15 activations of B, 2.5 % of its intervals above 1.25 periods
against 0.3 % of the steps', and with a viewer 8.6 % against the steps'
9.4 % — little to tell the two apart by. Without it, B reaches 2 late of
60 at most, and A goes still where it did with it: at the 30th frame
counted, in its second step.

D6. *The probe.* Five minutes after it went still for the frames, the
first activation is animated again, its count started anew: 60 frames
with fewer than 3 late, animated from then on; 3 late, still for five
minutes more. A viewer connected (D4) needs no probe: its connection says
when it is gone.

D7. *Said in the log.* A record at info level as the setting changes —
"Animation still: a VNC viewer connected", "Animation still: the frames
slip" with the count, "Animation back" — once per change, not per
activation.

== Behavior

`auto`, nothing slow: as `true` now. A viewer connected: the next
activation shows at once, steps at once, no live thumbnails; disconnected:
animated again at the next activation. The frames slipping, from a load or
a viewer on a port not listed: animated until 3 late of 60, then still,
probed every five minutes. `true`: as now, whatever slips. `false`: as now.

== Open decisions

O1. *The fade-out in the count of D5.* Taken in the plan: not counted —
then K2 holds on the batches of the research. The alternative, D5 as first
written: counted, and batch B, without a viewer, goes still once, in its
fourth activation, for five minutes; K2 would then have to say so. The
author's to confirm with the trial (K7).
