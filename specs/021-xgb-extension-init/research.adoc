== The tables of xgb

The code of xgb 1.3.1:

* `xgb.go:194–226`: `NewEventFuncs` and `NewErrorFuncs`, `map[int]` of the
  constructors of events and errors by their number, and
  `NewExtEventFuncs`, `NewExtErrorFuncs`, the constructors of each
  extension by its own numbering, filled by the `init` of the extension's
  package. All four are variables of the package: one for the process.
* `(*Conn).readResponses`, the reader goroutine of a connection, started by
  `NewConn`: `NewErrorFuncs[int(buf[1])]` for every error (`xgb.go:519`),
  `NewEventFuncs[evNum]` for every event (`xgb.go:557`), with no lock.
* The `Init` of every extension package, `damage.Init` for one: it asks
  `QueryExtension`, sets `c.Extensions[name]` under `c.ExtLock`, then
  writes the constructors of the extension into `NewEventFuncs` at
  `FirstEvent + n` and into `NewErrorFuncs` at `FirstError + n`, with no
  lock, whichever connection it was given. `c.ExtLock`, "a lock used
  whenever new extensions are initialized", guards `c.Extensions` of the
  connection alone; BurntSushi/xgb#34, "Read/Write mutex for Extensions
  map", added it for that map only.
* The documentation of each package says "Init must be called before using
  the RANDR extension", nothing of the tables.

The extensions qws uses, from their packages, and their numbers on E1, from
`xdpyinfo -queryExtensions`:

[cols="2,1,1,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| Extension | Events | Errors | Opcode | First event | First error

| Composite | 0 | 0 | 142 | — | —
| DAMAGE | 1 | 1 | 143 | 91 | 152
| MIT-SHM | 1 | 1 | 130 | 65 | 128
| RANDR | 2 | 4 | 140 | 89 | 147
| RENDER | 0 | 5 | 139 | — | 142
| XFIXES | 2 | 1 | 138 | 87 | 140
|===

Go checks a map read against a write in progress: the write marks the map,
the read finds the mark and the runtime ends the process with "concurrent
map read and map write" — `internal/runtime/maps.fatal` in the stack below.
A fatal error, not a panic: `recover` does not catch it. The mark is set by
any assignment, the same value at the same key too: an `Init` run again on
a connection writes the map again. The check sees only an overlap in time;
the race detector sees any two accesses that happens-before does not order.

No issue of `jezek/xgb` is about the tables (its issues and pull requests,
2026-10-02).

== Where qws initialised extensions

`cmd/qws/root.go` makes, in this order: the main connection
(`x11.Connect`); the snapshotter (`snapshot.New`), with a connection of its
own; without it, the capturer of 1.0.0 on the main connection; the window
model (`x11.NewModel`), with a connection of its own; the focus watcher,
which selects the property changes of the root on the main connection; the
key grabs; then the loop of events, and the activations. The calls of
`Init` on `develop`:

[cols="3,2,3,4",options="header"]
|===
| Call | Connection | When | Reading events meanwhile

| `x11.GetMonitors`: `randr.Init`
| main
| every activation: `Selector.Show`, and `ui.NewSelector` at the first
| the snapshotter's — DAMAGE of every viewable window that changes, the
  structure of the windows, the root's properties; the model's — the
  properties of the client windows and of the root; the main one — keys,
  the root's properties, the overlay's events

| snapshotter, `initX`: `composite.Init`, `xfixes.Init`, `damage.Init`

| snapshotter, `initX` → `composite.NewCapturer`: `composite.Init`,
  `shm.Init`
| snapshotter's
| start, after it selected the property changes of the root
| the snapshotter's own: the root's properties

| snapshotter, loop → `newXRender`: `render.Init`, `shm.Init`
| snapshotter's

The activation is where the crash was seen: a window that changes every
20 ms sends DAMAGE to the snapshotter's connection all along. The start has
a race of the same kind, rarer: four `Init` while the root's properties may
change. Before 1.1.0, with one connection, `randr.Init` at an activation
raced the reader of that same connection.

== Reproduction

`TestMonitorsUnderEvents` (`pkg/x11/extensions_test.go`, committed with the
change): a connection of `x11.Connect` calls `GetMonitors` again and again
for a second, while a second connection, of `xgb.NewConn`, sends
ClientMessages with an empty event mask to an unmapped InputOnly window of
its own at −100,−100 — "If the event-mask is the empty set, then the event
is sent to the client that created the destination window" (X11 protocol,
SendEvent), no other client sees them — and reads them. On `develop` with
only the test file added (run T-before of the specification), the binary of
`go test -c ./pkg/x11`, run 20 times: 18 ended with

----

goroutine 21 [running]:

	.../src/runtime/panic.go:1181 +0x18

	.../github.com/jezek/xgb@v1.3.1/xgb.go:557 +0x2e5

	.../github.com/jezek/xgb@v1.3.1/xgb.go:159 +0x2e5

— the stack of issue #61 — 0.01–0.87 s after the binary started, a median
of 0.09 s; two passed, with 246 and 333 calls of `GetMonitors` and 252 748
and 242 778 events read in their second. A first version of the test, the
same flood and loop with a cleanup of its own, crashed in 25 runs of 26.
Built with `-race`, 9 runs of 9 failed with "race detected during
execution of test", each on one race: the write of `randr.Init`
(`randr/randr.go:27`, from `pkg/x11/randr.go:25`) and the read of

`/proc/<pid>/stat`, grew by 0.98–0.99 s in each run that passed. A paced
flood (`paced_flood_test.go` in the probes, `FLOOD_PER_MS` ClientMessages a
millisecond) spares it little and loses the crash: `GetMonitors` then runs
2 100–3 100 times a second instead of some 250, Xorg takes 0.6–0.75 s of
CPU in the second all the same, and 4 runs of 10 crashed at 20 events a
millisecond, 6 of 10 at 50. The test floods unpaced, for one second.

== Designs

Ways to write the tables once, before any connection reads events:

[cols="3,5",options="header"]
|===
| Design | Why not, or why

| `Init` of each extension once, on the first connection, before it
  selects any event
| Its reader runs while `Init` writes. The X server sends MappingNotify to
  every client, whatever it selected, when the keyboard mapping changes —
  "The X server reports MappingNotify events to all clients. There is no
  mechanism to express disinterest in this event." (Xlib, chapter 10) — as
  when a session starts and sets its keyboard up, the moment the service
  starts. And the guarantee would rest on the order of `root.go`.

| A lock of qws around `Init`
| The reader is xgb's goroutine and takes no lock of qws.

| A patched xgb: a lock in `readResponses` and `Init`, or tables per
  connection
| A fork, or a `replace`, to carry through every update of xgb (012);
  nothing upstream to take it.

| *The setup on a connection of its own, closed before the writes*
| *Chosen.* No reader of any connection runs while the tables are written:
  the setup's connection has ended, and every other is made after.
|===

Why no reader can see the tables being written (the Go memory model,
https://go.dev/ref/mem): the last act of the reader of xgb 1.3.1 is to
close its channel of events (`defer close(c.eventChan)`), or to send a
read error on it and then close it; every read of the tables by that
reader comes before that act, which is synchronized before the receive of
`WaitForEvent` that returns neither an event nor an error. The setup drains
`WaitForEvent` to that receive after `Close`, then writes. `sync.OnceValues`
returns to every caller once the setup has returned, and every later
connection starts its reader by a `go` statement in `xgb.NewConn` after
that. A connection made with `xgb.NewConn` before the first `x11.NewConn`
would break this; qws makes none.

The setup costs a connection: the first `x11.NewConn` took 0.87–1.45 ms,
a later one 0.28–0.40 ms, `xgb.NewConn` 0.26–0.45 ms — the three in turn
in a test of the package, run 5 times, with the change. It saves a round
trip per `Init`: seven at the start of the snapshotter, one or two at each
activation.

== After the change

T-after, the binaries built from `b510741` in a clean worktree: 20 runs of
20 passed, with 199–432 calls of `GetMonitors` and 221 649–261 938 events
read in the second, Xorg's CPU 0.97–1.02 s a run; built with `-race`, 5 of
5 passed with no race, 1 352–1 759 calls and 97 022–106 529 events.
